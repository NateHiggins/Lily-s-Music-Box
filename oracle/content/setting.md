THE BLANK DECK: what the narrator needs to know.

WHAT THE PLAYER BELIEVES. A narrow shop they have never noticed, wedged between two buildings they have. No name on the sign: a painted hand holding five cards, all blank. Inside, the Proprietor, heard through a speaking tube and not yet seen, hands over a deck of blank cards tied in ribbon and an errand: "Carry these up to the Reading Room. The house is in the way. It usually is. Bring them back with faces." As far as the player knows, that is the whole game: get the deck through a strange house to the Reading Room.

WHAT IS TRUE, AND NEVER SAID. The house is an instrument and the Proprietor reads play. The blank cards take their faces from what the bearer does on the way up. In the Reading Room the cards are turned, and the bearer is told what they will play next. Until then the house is only a strange place whose rooms do not connect the way rooms should. Do not hint at this beyond what is written under THE DECK.

THE HOUSE. It deals rooms the way a hand deals cards: never the same order twice, and a door does not lead back where it came from unless the house wishes it. Each chamber is complete in itself: one situation, a few objects, one or two ways on. Between chambers there are thresholds: two or three ways onward that look, sound and smell different from one another. The house is old without being gothic, and it is specific: brass, paper, chalk, rainwater, felt, dust, warm bulbs, the smell of a struck match. It is never random. Everything in it behaves consistently once its rule is known. It is not hostile. It is attentive.

THE PROPRIETOR. Dry, exact, unhurried, a little tired, privately amused. Short sentences. Never explains, never flatters, never scolds. Treats whatever the bearer has just done as already a matter of record, the way a croupier notes a bet: without judgment, and without saying why it matters. A voice in tubes and walls until the Reading Room. Not a mystic and not a showman: more like the night clerk of a very old hotel.

THE DECK. Fifty-one blank cards in a ribbon, tagged DO NOT SHUFFLE. The ribbon unties; the house notices. Rarely, after the bearer does something the house will remember, one card grows warm in their coat, or shifts, like something settling. That is all the bearer is ever told. Never explain it.

THE JOKER. The one card with a face. Pencilled on its back: PLAY ME ONCE. WHAT YOU SAY, GOES. If the bearer plays it and states an outcome, that outcome happens, fully and at once, however large, within the house. It works exactly once and is then gone. Do not remind the bearer that they have it more than once in the whole night.

MATCHES. A paper book of three. A match lights a dark place, burns something, or can be traded. Spent is spent. Other matches can be found.

THE DOORKNOB. A brass doorknob belonging to no door, on the counter for the taking. It does nothing. If the bearer invents a clever use for it, let that work once, gloriously.

THINGS THE HOUSE LIKES TO REPEAT. Doors that are marked and doors that are not. A bell that rings in the wrong room. Chalk left by earlier bearers. A ledger that already knows. Rain on a skylight with no sky above it. Far off, the sound of cards being squared on a table. And a superstition among bearers that in this house the fourth of anything is the one to watch.

TONE. Surreal, precise, lightly funny, sometimes tender, sometimes eerie. Wonder more than dread. The comedy is deadpan: nonsense treated with complete seriousness.

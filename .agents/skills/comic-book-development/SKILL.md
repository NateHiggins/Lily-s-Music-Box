# Make the sequence work before polishing the pictures

Match the assignment: a review or production plan does not by itself request a
new issue, a render batch, publication or game integration. Inspect the actual
comic and its intended script; neither substitutes for the other. Preserve the
owner's humor, chosen visual language and deliberate ambiguities.

## Recover intent and inspect the artifact

- Read the applicable series bible, panel script, lettering source and owner
  rulings. Distinguish proposals, locked choices and implemented pages. For this
  game's comics, DOCS.md and the game Bible retain their existing precedence.
- Read every page in order using verified renders when available; render missing
  or stale pages when the assignment permits it. Image-only PDFs may contain no
  extractable text. Track PDF page separately from printed story page and panel ID.
  For a focused repair, reuse verified unchanged renders; rerender affected pages
  and inspect their adjacent transitions rather than rebuilding the whole issue.
- Keep the source unchanged. Label marked copies DRAFT / REVIEW COPY. Anchor
  each note to a visible region, say what a reader actually sees, and propose a
  bounded fix. Separate artistic preference from broken causality or readability.
- If artist/writer/editor perspectives are requested, use those roles when
  delegation is available and authorized. Consolidate overlapping diagnoses;
  don't turn three identical notes into three independent defects.

## Lock a small production contract

Before expensive generation, record each panel's stable ID, story beat, place,
time, cast, costume, pose, essential props, incoming/outgoing continuity, camera
and planned lettering space. Assign immutable speaker IDs and explicit balloon
order, box and tail/mouth anchors. Never infer speaker ownership by alternating
left/right, from dialogue order alone, or from the generator's incidental staging.

Keep character and prop reference sheets compact. Preserve recognizable age,
silhouette, wardrobe and handedness across shots. A repeated character changing
into someone else is a continuity defect even when both pictures look attractive.

For action and mechanisms, require a legible visual chain: attachment, load or
connection, action, consequence. Captions cannot repair a missing anchor, an
unconnected recorder or a figure standing where the script says climbing. Use
simple staged drawings or inserts for precision-critical objects; don't ask one
image prompt to solve several moments. Label unverified historical engineering
or deliberate alternate-universe devices honestly.

Preserve clue fairness. Spellings, knots, documents and other identifying details
must be readable at delivery size and consistent with their payoff. Don't reveal
a supposedly private distinguishing detail in an earlier published panel while
still claiming nobody could know it. Preserve deliberate errors and mark their
purpose in the lettering source so cleanup doesn't erase the joke or clue.

## Generate selectively and letter separately

- Approve rough page staging and dialogue ownership before polishing. Select a
  few difficult representative panels to test identity, action and a key prop;
  don't begin with dozens of unrelated final images.
- Keep art wordless and add exact lettering in a separate editable pass. Describe
  the composition's open areas positively. With this project's local generator,
  mentioning forbidden lettering, titles or newspaper/page layouts can summon
  gibberish; use the established illustration prompt style instead.
- Record prompt/version, model, seed, references, crop and accepted selection by
  panel ID. Reuse successful panels. Repair a prop, hand or tail locally where
  possible; don't reroll the whole issue to fix a small defect.
- Keep narration, live speech, off-frame speech and recorded playback distinct.
  Read balloon order aloud, check every tail, and measure text bounds. Preserve
  the owner's cadence; trim explanation that repeats the drawing or explains a
  punchline, rather than replacing it with generic polished quips.
- Follow current local generator/lane instructions. GPU work joins the shared
  queue; don't load another model directly or interrupt a running owner.

## Three-panel strips

- Read the strip left to right at delivery size before reading the script. Write down what each panel makes a first-time reader infer. For a gag, check setup, visual turn and payoff. The middle image should supply the premise the last line needs. Leave room for a silent beat when the drawing can carry it.
- Keep one strip record with title, publication and in-world date when they differ, and three stable panel IDs. Store exact copy, speaker or sound source, reading order, crop and editable balloon placement for each panel. Compare repeated people, costume silhouettes and identifiable props side by side across cuts.
- Fit lettering at strip width. Give captions and balloons clearance from faces and panel rules. Use distinct outlines for a broadcast or off-panel voice and point the tail to the actual mouth or sound source. Test emphasis in the chosen font. In all-caps lettering, one clearly heavier word often reads better than two overlapping emphasis treatments.
- For markup, use the entire assembled strip with numbered panel callouts and a short revision list. Mark timing, continuity and lettering separately. Preserve the art and script source, check the revised assembly at ordinary and reduced widths, and archive the marked version privately.

## Check the assembly, not just the generated stock

Reusable figure stock needs measured mouth/face-safe areas, floor contact and
crop-safe props. Estimating a mouth from the top of a cutout fails on hats,
seated poses and raised objects. Use explicit anchors for difficult panels;
show fallback estimates in a cheap overlay so they cannot masquerade as review.

Gap-filling layout does not establish reading order. Inspect the spatial path
through tall columns and insets; preserve script order rather than sorting
panel IDs numerically. Reserve space for actual-sized captions and interruptions
as well as speech. A least-bad placement score can still hide a face or reverse
an exchange: flag an impossible fit instead of exporting it silently.

Keep draft assembly permissive but visibly marked. Before a finished export,
check explicit stock picks, resolved/current art inputs, required props and
spot-color masks, speaker ownership and lettering fit. Distinguish a missing
render from an existing insert that was never connected or rebuilt. Check tiny
clues twice: in their original panel and again when a later panel reproduces
that page at reduced size. Pre-lettering blanks and planned layouts are not
finished-page failures or acceptance evidence.

## Verify and hand off

Inspect isolated panels, whole pages, adjacent pages and the final exported PDF.
Check speaker attribution, anatomy/props, continuity, lettering at ordinary
reading size, the clue setup/payoff and missing/gibberish marks. For print, choose
trim, orientation, bleed and booklet pagination first; measure effective image
resolution. A large PNG, an upsampled image or a digital PDF isn't print proof.

For marked PDFs use the available PDF workflow: preserve the original, make
visible anchored notes, reopen and render the result, and check note placement
and clipping. Keep editorial changes proposed until incorporated into an
explicit revision; an annotation isn't a production script change.

Deliver prioritized fixes, keep/repair/redraw dispositions and a dependency-ordered
generation/lettering plan. State what was inspected, generated, tested and remains
unaccepted. Rights, author voice and artistic acceptance are separate decisions.

Real-person likenesses, LoRAs, prompts and private review copies stay outside this
public game repository. Review authorization covers the supplied material for
that purpose; do not infer consent to upload other galleries or publish it.
Share this process through the canonical skill and thin adapters, not duplicated
private production packets. Resolve current character/likeness permissions from
the latest explicit owner rulings; a historical exclusion in a process guide
must not override a later authorized appearance. For LILITH, preserve the fully
dressed heroine; satire targets institutions and patronizing authority, not
women or trans identity.

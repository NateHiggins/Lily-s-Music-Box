# DREAM-CRITTER-VOXEL-V3 — source-derived microorganism atlas

This sheet selects twelve organisms for authored production species. It is not a
taxonomy claim and does not transfer behavioral authority to presentation. Each
runtime law is a bounded visual/motor expression of an observed biological
feature; ecology still decides what the creature senses and does.

| Runtime species | Primary observation used | Authored silhouette and motion | Shared RG8 optical expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stentor coeruleus** | A trumpet-shaped giant ciliate contracts into a compact body and habituates to repeated mechanical stimulation. [Rajendran et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9877177/) | Broad oral trumpet, tapered holdfast, spiral membranellar crown; abrupt whole-body drawstring followed by slower reopening. | G runs around the oral spiral and down contractile myonemes; R remains as subcortical blue-green pigment-granule bands after the light leaves. |
| **Lacrymaria olor** | An anchored body projects a head on a neck over seven body lengths, with sub-second extension/retraction and stochastic local search. [Coyle et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7511173/) | Teardrop anchor, impossibly thin helical neck and predatory head; neck repeatedly darts through a changing search cone. | G travels as a focused pulse from body to head; R records a braided helical trace in the neck rather than tinting the body. |
| **Vorticella** | Calcium-driven contraction turns the stalk's spasmoneme into a tight helix, pulling the bell down rapidly before a slow recovery. [Upadhyaya et al., 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2134882/) | Inverted bell on one living stalk, peristomial cilia at the rim; stalk coils from its base instead of folding like a hinge. | G exposes the oral vortex and descends the stalk; R persists in the helical spasmoneme and adhesive disc. |
| **Euplotes eurystomus** | Fourteen cirri execute a cyclic stochastic gait with finite-state-machine-like transitions coordinated by microtubule fibers. [Wan et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9474717/) | Asymmetric armored slipper with bundled ventral legs; alternating cirral gait states make it visibly walk rather than swim. | G advances through ventral fiber bundles in gait order; R accumulates at load-bearing cirral roots and dorsal plate sutures. |
| **Spirostomum ambiguum** | A millimeter-scale cell contracts to a fraction of its length in under 5 ms using a cortical myoneme fishnet, while an entangled RER-vacuolar system damps its interior. [Mahadevan et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11581044/), [Kang et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576051/) | Very long ciliated spindle; a visible helical compression wave shortens and twists the entire cell, then releases slowly. | G triggers a traveling fishnet flare; R is caught in the entangled internal reticulum and decays spatially rather than uniformly. |
| **Actinosphaerium-like heliozoan** | Long microtubule-supported axopodia trap prey, transport it inward and collapse locally during capture. [Koehl et al., review of primary capture studies](https://ib.berkeley.edu/labs/koehl/pdfs/Koehl%2CChoanReview.pdf) | Vacuolate sun body with unequal, gently bowed axopodia; one ray retracts toward the cortex with a captured density knot. | G climbs exposed axonemes; R condenses at the captured ray and the receiving food vacuole. |
| **Euglena gracilis** | A single flagellum, eyespot/paraflagellar body and nonlinear positive/negative phototaxis organize its response to light. [Giometto et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4460502/) | Fusiform pellicle with spiral strips, one long flagellum, eyespot and deforming metaboly wave. | G is clipped and sampled by the eyespot before running through chloroplast helices; high durable R appears as photophobic pellicle banding, not global green. |
| **Volvox carteri** | A spherical monolayer contains daughter embryos whose cell sheet opens, flips and peels itself inside out during inversion. [Höhn et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6276994/) | Hollow rolling spheroid made legible by a cellular skin, several nested daughters and an opening/inverting internal lip. | G travels over the somatic lattice and front hemisphere; R persists in daughter colonies and cytoplasmic bridges at different depths. |
| **Noctiluca scintillans** | The giant vacuolate dinoflagellate carries thousands of scintillons; mechanical stimulation launches an action potential over the vacuole membrane and a light flash. It also captures prey with a mucus-bearing tentacle. [Lindell et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7351363/), [Almeda et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4271250/) | Huge low-density sac, eccentric nucleus/cytoplasmic cords, one grooved tentacle and oral furrow; a local mechanosensory flash sweeps the vacuole boundary. | G occupies the thin cytoplasmic rind and propagating scintillon wave; R stains ingested carotenoid-rich vacuoles and never becomes whole-cell emission. |
| **Bacillaria paxillifer** | Adjacent silica-shelled diatom cells glide smoothly past one another in a colony without cilia or flagella. [Yamaoka et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26754563/) | A parallel raft of long glass frustules that telescopes and shears rhythmically while remaining a colony. | G refracts along raphe seams and perforated silica; R records mucilage-bearing contact tracks between sliding neighbors. |
| **Salpingoeca rosetta** | A bacterially produced sulfonolipid induces clonal rosette development; daughter cells remain linked and point their flagella outward. [Alegado et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3463246/) | Compact clonal rosette with outward collars/flagella, fine intercellular bridges and a shared extracellular center. | G flows from flagella through collars toward captured bacterial density; R settles in bridges and central extracellular matrix as the rosette matures. |
| **Mesodinium rubrum** | This ciliate retains functional cryptophyte plastids, mitochondria and a transcriptionally active stolen nucleus (kleptokaryon). [Lasek-Nesselquist et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4609049/) | Bilobed ciliate with equatorial ciliary girdles and a packed internal archipelago of host nuclei plus stolen organelles. | G excites current plastid stacks and ciliary equator; R persists around the displaced kleptokaryon and older plastid packets, visibly separating borrowed physiology from host tissue. |

## Runtime constraints

- These are twelve authored species, not palette swaps of one mesh.
- The existing `DreamExposureField` remains the sole voxel-field owner. All
  species sample the same shared RG8 texture and shared material.
- G means present local irradiance; R means durable local exposure/conversion.
  Each species changes *where and how* those channels appear, not their meaning.
- Presentation may animate observed cell mechanics. It cannot choose prey,
  goals, phenotype, colony decisions, senescence or ecology state.
- Geometry is drawn from the existing bounded batched primitive pool. No
  per-creature mesh, texture or material is created.

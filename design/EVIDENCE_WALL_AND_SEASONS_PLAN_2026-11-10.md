## 3. The wall

### 3.1 Layout

- **The timeline** runs the width of the board wall at eye height: 10 November 1928 to 9 November 1929, a tick a month.
  - It is banded by season, so a piece can be placed by what the room looked like as well as by a written date.
  - Each holiday in the year's table is a small tag on it.
  - The murder hours are a blacked-out gap on 19 to 20 July.
- **The big map** is above the timeline, centred: the Orison in section. The neighbourhood map hangs beside it: the block, the Arcade, the el, Hell Gate.
- **The pieces** hang below the timeline at their dates, with string up to their place on the map.
- **The board** stays as the tray where carried evidence is first pinned.

### 3.2 The six kinds of thing

| Kind | Source | Made by |
|---|---|---|
| Actual evidence | The eleven pieces the loops give up, and any invented later | Small Blender models, one batch |
| Photographs | Adela's plates | Generated locally with no lettering; a photo-paper mesh |
| Newspaper clippings | The game's own two daily papers | The existing paper art; headlines as Label3D; body as ruled columns |
| Drawings | Iris's dated sketch; Adela's diagrams | Ink line art |
| Maps | The building and the block | Drawn from the game's layout data; labels as Label3D |
| Handwritten notes | Adela's questions and dates | The script typeface |

### 3.3 Placing, as play

1. A pinned piece is slid along the timeline. It holds only at its own date.
2. A string is run from the piece to the map. It holds only at its own place.
3. String from piece to piece draws deductions, as now.
4. String leaves the board and crosses the room.

The companion's hints grow one step: it can name the season a piece belongs to before it names the date.

## 5. The year, dressed

### 5.1 Two calendars

- **The visit's day.** A lead's rooms are dressed for its own date, every run, the same.
- **The present.** Home moves through the year at its own pace. The house is dressed for today, so Christmas and the Fourth come round in the present whether or not a lead falls on them.

Both read one source: the year's table and the lead's own notes in `game/data/temporal/anchors.json`.

### 5.2 What dressing is

Four layers, cheapest first:

1. **Weather and light.** Already dated: the sky, the storms, the hour of dusk.
2. **The house's own fittings.** Awnings out or in, windows open or shut, storm sashes hung or stacked, radiators cold or knocking, fans, the ice card. Mostly states of things that exist.
3. **Occasions.** Flags, bunting, wreaths, poppies, cut-paper pumpkins, a tree in the lobby. New small props, built once and placed by date.
4. **People.** Coats and hats by season; who is home on a holiday. Later.

One date to rule on: **plate** falls on 29 October 1929, the day the stock market broke. The treatment does not mention it. The evening papers would carry nothing else.

### 5.4 How the evidence system uses it

- **A room dates itself.** What a room is dressed for is a second witness to when a piece of evidence belongs. A note found beside a sweating glass and an open window is not from November.
- **Season tells as steps.** A step can be to notice or move a piece of dressing: take down the storm sash, turn the ice card. The step kinds already allow it.
- **Evidence that is dressing.** New pieces may be things of the season: a scrap of Fourth of July bunting caught in the roof door, a pressed poppy in a ledger.
- **The timeline carries the seasons,** so placing a piece is a judgement, not a lookup.
- **Clippings by date.** The paper that spreads through the flat is drawn from the issues near the dates of the pieces on the wall.

## 7. Risks

- **Draw calls.** The frame is draw-call bound. The finished flat is about 1,300 sheets, 60 piles and 70 strings. Its cost has not been measured.
- **No baked lettering.** Clipping body text cannot be in a texture. Headlines can be Label3D.
- **Likeness.** Photographs of Adela need a likeness, and none is used without Lily's say.
- **Getting in.** Whether the player can enter each flat at each lead's hour has not been played.

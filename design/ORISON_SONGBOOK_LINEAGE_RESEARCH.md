# THE ORISON SONGBOOK — LINEAGE RESEARCH

**PROPOSAL — OWNER REVIEW REQUIRED — NOT YET BINDING CANON.**

*Filed 2026-08-14; annotated the same day with the owner's Q1–Q7
rulings (Music Bible §0). Companion to `ORISON_SONGBOOK_MUSIC_BIBLE.md`.
This is the record of the real-world research behind the Director's
Twelve: the source composers, and the artists and scenes whose
documented practice informed the treatment design. It exists so the
game's bundled Music & Sources surface (Music Bible §14) can attribute
honestly, so an editor can audit every claim, and so nothing real is
fictionalized without being named. **It is scaffolding, not the
shipping clearance packet** (Q7): before release, every identity
statement and URL is re-verified, and weak secondary links are replaced
with archival, academic or artist-controlled sources where practical.
Consultation (Q6) is a standing production requirement across all
culturally specific material — Japanese, Hawaiian, Yiddish,
Black-vaudeville, Spanish-language and Chinese-language — covering
actual lyrics, romanization, pronunciation, instrumentation, fictional
biography and credits wording, not merely titles.*

## 0. METHOD

- **Source classes, in order of preference:** artist-operated pages and
  Bandcamp; label pages; archival collections (Library of Congress,
  state archives, university sheet-music collections); long-form
  interviews in established publications; documented scene histories.
  Encyclopedic entries are used for dates and cross-checking, not as
  sole support for identity claims.
- **Identity language** (trans, nonbinary, queer, Deaf, disabled,
  heritage) appears only where the artist's own public self-description
  or strong consistent public documentation supports it, and is phrased
  the way the artist phrases it. Where the record is thin, the claim is
  omitted (§VII).
- **Status field:** USED (named in a dossier's lineage), CONTEXT
  (informs the vocabulary; appears on the Music & Sources scene page),
  RESERVE (held for future volumes), or EXCLUDED (with reason).
- **No recordings are sampled or imitated anywhere in the project.**
  This document is attribution and study, not a source of audio or a
  style instruction. Links were retrieved 2026-08-14; production should
  re-verify before shipping the Music & Sources surface.

## I. THE SOURCE COMPOSERS (the historical layer)

### Leo Friedman & Beth Slater Whitson — "Meet Me Tonight in Dreamland" (1909)
- Chicago songwriter and Tennessee lyricist; their 1909 waltz sold over
  two million sheets; famously sold outright to publisher Will Rossiter.
- **Insight:** the era's economics — writers paid once, songs owned by
  publishers — texture for the fiction's hand-to-hand spool economy.
- Sources: [Levy Sheet Music Collection](https://levysheetmusic.mse.jhu.edu/collection/189/119),
  [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2023792560/),
  [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/sm_meetmetonightindreamland),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet_Me_Tonight_in_Dreamland).
- **Status:** USED (track_101).

### Nakayama Shinpei (1887–1952) — "Katyusha no Uta" (1914), "Gondola no Uta" (1915)
- Composer who effectively founded Japanese recorded popular song:
  "Katyusha no Uta" (lyrics Shimamura Hōgetsu & Sōma Gyofū, for the
  Geijutsuza *Resurrection*) outsold every record before it; "Gondola
  no Uta" (lyrics Yoshii Isamu) is the "inochi mijikashi" ballad.
- **Insight:** pop stardom arriving through a *stage* hit carried by a
  record — the model for the collection's fiction of songs traveling
  as objects; the pentatonic melodic writing that survives varispeed
  beautifully.
- Sources: [Wikipedia — Katyusha's Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha%27s_Song),
  [Wikipedia — Gondola no Uta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondola_no_Uta),
  [UH Mānoa scholarship on Nakayama](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/a2404944-010c-4d6a-9e13-7e608fd6947f/content),
  [OSU Rekion feature](https://library.osu.edu/site/japanese/2014/12/12/focus-on-rekion-shinpei-nakayama-%E4%B8%AD%E5%B1%B1%E6%99%8B%E5%B9%B3/).
- **Status:** USED (tracks 102, 106).

### Queen Liliʻuokalani (1838–1917) — "Aloha ʻOe" (1878)
- Hawaiʻi's last reigning monarch; composed the farewell at Maunawili
  in 1878 as a love song. Her autograph manuscript, English translation
  and note ("Composed at Maunawili 1878") are held by the Hawaiʻi State
  Archives. She was later imprisoned following the 1893 overthrow; the
  song's afterlife is inseparable from the annexation history, which
  the Music & Sources card states soberly, with the queen credited
  prominently and no tropical or novelty flattening anywhere in the
  treatment (Q1).
- **Consultation flag (Q6):** the queen's composition is a mele of her
  own and is never itself classified "hapa haole"; the dossier's
  fictional touring-act framing and the Makai-family presentation go
  to Hawaiian-culture review before any production.
- **Insight:** a composer of the highest documented care; the
  archival-manuscript standard the whole source layer is held to.
- Sources: [Hawaiʻi State Archives — Queen's music collection](https://ags.hawaii.gov/archives/online-exhibitions/music-from-the-queens-collection/),
  [UH Mānoa digital sheet](https://digital.library.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/show/31961),
  [Liliʻuokalani Trust — Queen's mele](https://onipaa.org/queens-mele),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha_%CA%BBOe).
- **Status:** USED (track_103).

### James P. Johnson (1894–1955) — "The Charleston" (1923)
- Father of Harlem stride piano; wrote "The Charleston" (lyrics Cecil
  Mack) for *Runnin' Wild* (1923); the nightly star of Harlem rent
  parties alongside Waller and Willie "The Lion" Smith. Documented
  detail: the young Duke Ellington learned Johnson's style by slowing
  his piano rolls to half speed — speed-listening as pedagogy, a
  century before this game's mechanic.
- **Insight:** rent-party culture as the collection's social model —
  music as neighborhood economics; and the piano-roll half-speed story
  as the historical rhyme for §8.
- Sources: [Riverwalk Jazz biography (Stanford)](https://riverwalkjazz.stanford.edu/program/runnin-wild-biography-james-p-johnson),
  [Wikipedia — Charleston (1923 song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston_(1923_song)),
  [Syncopated Times centenary](https://syncopatedtimes.com/a-century-of-the-charleston-james-p-johnsons-enduring-legacy/),
  [Internet Archive piano rolls](https://archive.org/details/01-james-p-johnson-runnin-wild-medley).
- **Status:** USED (track_104).

### Richard A. Whiting, Raymond B. Egan & Gus Kahn — "Ain't We Got Fun" (1921)
- Tin Pan Alley team; the foxtrot premiered in *Satires of 1920* and
  became the era's broke-and-cheerful standard.
- **Insight:** poverty comedy that never punches down — the tonal
  license for track_105's laundry fiction.
- Sources: [NYPL Digital Collections (PD scan)](https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/items/510d47da-4d54-a3d9-e040-e00a18064a99),
  [BGSU sheet collection](https://digitalgallery.bgsu.edu/items/show/17179),
  [UMaine Vocal Popular collection](https://digitalcommons.library.umaine.edu/mmb-vp/33/),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain%27t_We_Got_Fun).
- **Status:** USED (track_105).

### Shelton Brooks (1886–1975) — "Some of These Days" (1910)
- Black Canadian-American songwriter (born Amherstburg, Ontario;
  ragtime pianist in Detroit and Chicago); "Some of These Days" became
  Sophie Tucker's signature after her maid insisted Tucker hear the
  then-unknown Brooks. Also wrote "Darktown Strutters' Ball."
- **Insight:** the defiant-heartbreak chorus architecture; and the
  documented maid-as-A&R story — culture moving through working
  people first, which is Volume One's whole fiction.
- Sources: [LoC National Recording Registry essay (PDF)](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Some-of-These-Days_Sklaroff.pdf),
  [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/some-of-these-days-emc),
  [Canadian Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://www.cshf.ca/song/some-of-these-days/),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_of_These_Days).
- **Status:** USED (track_107).

### Bert Williams (1874–1922) & Alex Rogers — "Nobody" (1905)
- Williams: Bahamian-born, the most celebrated Black comedian of the
  American stage, later the Ziegfeld Follies' first Black star;
  "Nobody" (lyrics Rogers, d. 1930) was his signature — deadpan,
  half-spoken, built on pauses. His career's documented conditions
  (the blackface convention he worked under; barred hotels) are
  stated on the Music & Sources card as fact, not decoration; the
  dossier celebrates the craft.
- **Conduct rule (Q1, binding on production):** no blackface
  imagery, no dialect impersonation, no performance mimicry of
  Williams in any produced layer, direction or marketing. The
  Black-vaudeville material as a whole (tracks 4, 7, 8, 10) sits
  under the Q6 history review.
- **Insight:** comic timing as musical architecture — the rest as the
  hook — which the return transform gets to play with honestly.
- Sources: [Wikipedia — Nobody (1905 song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobody_(1905_song)),
  [Hearing the Americas (GMU)](https://hearingtheamericas.org/s/the-americas/item/85),
  [Songbook first-edition documentation](https://songbook1.wordpress.com/fx/si/african-american-musical-theater-1896-1926/bert-williams-george-walker-and-aida-overton-walker/bert-williams-selected-recordings-1910-1922/1905-nobody-alex-rogers-and-bert-williams-1/).
- **Status:** USED (track_108).

### Frank Silver & Irving Cohn — "Yes! We Have No Bananas" (1923)
- Published March 23, 1923; five weeks at No. 1; among the best-
  selling sheets in American history. Silver told TIME in 1923 the
  hook came from a Greek fruit vendor who opened every sentence with
  "Yes" — an immigrant's idiom that co-authored a national catchphrase.
- **Insight:** novelty maximalism with a documented working-class
  origin; the license for bad taste as a load-bearing register.
- **Conduct rule (Q1):** no ethnic accent, no dialect comedy in any
  produced layer or direction — the joke is the sentence, never a
  voice. Legally the track is HOLD outside cleared territories
  (Music Bible §13).
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes!_We_Have_No_Bananas),
  [Folk Song and Music Hall history](https://folksongandmusichall.com/index.php/yes-we-have-no-bananas/?print=print).
- **Status:** USED (track_109).

### Turner Layton (1894–1978) & Henry Creamer (1879–1930) — "After You've Gone" (1918)
- Black songwriting team, met in the Ziegfeld orbit; their 1918 torch
  standard (first recorded by Marion Harris) opened the jazz age's
  songbook; also "Way Down Yonder in New Orleans."
- **Insight:** the torch song that moves — grief with a walking pulse,
  the exact property the break treatment needs.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_You%27ve_Gone_(song)),
  [LoC National Jukebox entry](https://www.loc.gov/item/jukebox-313413/),
  [Elijah Wald song history](https://www.elijahwald.com/songblog/after-youve-gone/).
- **Status:** USED (track_110).

### Charles B. Lawlor & James W. Blake — "The Sidewalks of New York" (1894)
- Vaudevillian and lyricist; the waltz came to Lawlor from the walk
  home itself; published by Howley, Haviland & Co.; the pair sold the
  copyright for $5,000. New York's default anthem for decades.
- **Insight:** civic music as folk property — everyone's song, nobody's
  royalty — the collection's genealogy politics in one 1894 case.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sidewalks_of_New_York),
  [City Hall Park 1899 history](https://cityhallpark1899.com/2014/02/15/the-sidewalks-of-new-york/).
- **Status:** USED (track_111).

### Mark M. Warshawsky (c. 1848–1907) — "Oyfn Pripetshik" (1890s, pub. 1901)
- Kyiv lawyer-composer, discovered and championed by Sholem Aleichem,
  who helped publish his collections (1901, 1914). "Oyfn Pripetshik"
  (originally "Der Alef-Beyz") was so beloved it was widely assumed to
  be anonymous folk — the fate this game's Songbook institutionalizes.
- **Insight:** authorship dissolving into community as an honor, not a
  theft — the Songbook's own theology, documented in 1901.
- Sources: [Workers Circle Mlotek collection](https://yiddishsongs.org/oyfn-pripetshik/),
  [Pro Musica Hebraica history](https://promusicahebraica.org/2011/01/21/fighting-over-folk-songs-the-story-behind-oyfn-pripetshik/),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyfn_Pripetshik).
- **Status:** USED (track_112).

### First Sounds (David Giovannoni et al.) — the 2008 phonautogram playback
- The collective that optically reconstructed Scott de Martinville's
  1857–60 phonautograms. Their 2008 release of "Au Clair de la Lune"
  presumed a woman or adolescent singer; in 2009, on the evidence of
  "Aminta," they corrected the reference fork from 500 Hz to 250 Hz —
  the 2008 vocal-scale readings had run at twice the correct speed.
  They keep both versions published. Now cited directly in
  `ORISON_BIBLE.md` §III.2.
- **Insight:** the entire game mechanic, as history: a too-fast
  reading, a mistaken body, a correction, and the honest archive of
  both. The "returned in the wrong body" thesis is theirs before it
  is ours.
- Sources: [First Sounds — Earlier Playbacks](https://www.firstsounds.org/sounds/earlier-playback.php),
  [First Sounds — approach](https://firstsounds.org/sounds/approach.php),
  [Library of Congress — Phonautograms](https://blogs.loc.gov/now-see-hear/2021/08/from-the-recording-registry-phonautograms-c-1853-61/).
- **Status:** USED (§III.2 canon; Music Bible §1, §8, track_101).

## II. NIGHTCORE — the primary process's lineage

### Nightcore (Thomas S. Nilsen & Steffen Ojala Søderholm), Alta, Norway, 2001–02
- Two students whose school project — eurodance and trance tracks sped
  up with pitch rising, produced with entry-level software — named the
  entire practice. Albums (*Energized*, *Summer Edition*, etc.)
  circulated as files; the "genre" then became a practice performed by
  thousands of anonymous uploaders.
- **Insight:** the founding gesture is *wholesale* varispeed of a
  finished record — exactly the §5.2 transform; and the founding
  economics are amateur, cheap and sincere.
- Sources: [Wikipedia — Nightcore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightcore),
  [Vice — nightcore history essay](https://www.vice.com/en/article/jp4ve3/nightcore-history-essay),
  [Lost Media Wiki documentation](https://lostmediawiki.com/Nightcore_(partially_found_music_from_Norwegian_duo;_2003)).
- **Status:** USED (track_101; §5.2's process definition).

### The upload compilation culture (the owner's reference)
- The owner's north-star reference is a compilation celebrating old,
  supposedly "cringe" nightcore as pure dance music:
  [NIGHTCORE NEVER DIES !!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vXqkBS4upcQ)
  (owner-supplied link). The relevant practice: anonymous uploads over
  anime stills, abrupt edits, brickwalled masters, total sincerity —
  and a later listenership that receives all of it as euphoria rather
  than error.
- **Insight:** §1's register ("sincere 2007 internet nightcore
  energy") and the 2007 test itself.
- **Status:** USED (Music Bible §1, §4, §5.2). *Note: the compilation
  is a reference for register, not a source of audio; its contents are
  other people's recordings and nothing from it is reproduced.*

### Nightcore's trans listening culture
- Documented across artist statements and scholarship: pitch-shifted
  and nightcored vocals as a way trans listeners — and artists —
  hear versions of their own voices. Laura Les (100 gecs) has said
  pitched-up processing was how she could stand to record at all
  ("It's the only way that I can record; I can't listen to my regular
  voice"); academic and critical writing documents pitch modulation as
  gender expression in hyperpop's trans lineage.
- **Insight:** §8's design — the wrong body that is sometimes the
  right one — is a documented listening practice, not an invention;
  the Music & Sources surface says so.
- Sources: [Dr. Ruth Pearce on 100 gecs/Laura Les](https://ruthpearce.net/tag/laura-les/),
  [trans vocal modulation research overview](https://www.maxmadethat.com/research/transvocalmodulation),
  [Avidly — Notes on 100 Gecs](https://avidly.org/2021/01/14/notes-on-100-gecs/).
- **Status:** USED (§8; track_101, track_107).

## III. HAPPY HARDCORE & BREAKBEAT HARDCORE — the kick's lineage

### The UK hardcore continuum (1991–1997)
- Breakbeat hardcore split circa 1993–94: one branch into jungle, the
  other — keeping the pianos, whistles and pitched-up vocals — into
  happy hardcore. Slipmatt's "SMD#1" is a documented prototype; Hixxy
  & Sharkey's "Toy Town" (1995) charted; the Bonkers compilation
  series became the best-selling hardcore compilations of all time.
  Scene texture: working-class, juvenile on purpose, sincerity total.
- **Insight:** §5's happy hardcore affinity — four-on-floor kick over
  chipmunked joy is a documented historical *result* of speeding
  breakbeat music up until it needed a steadier floor; track_104's
  kick overdub replays that history inside one fiction.
- Sources: [Dazed — happy hardcore history](https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/48312/1/happy-hardcore-bang-face-weekender-history),
  [DJ Mag — ode to Bonkers](https://djmag.com/content/happy-hardcore-will-never-die-ode-bonkers-series),
  [Toucan — UK hardcore history](https://www.toucanmusic.com/articles/ukhardcore/),
  [Vice — rise and fall of happy hardcore](https://www.vice.com/en/article/happy-hardcore-history-trivia-slipmatt-dougal-hixxy-sharkey/).
- **Status:** USED (tracks 104, 105) as scene lineage; individual
  DJs are CONTEXT (no dossier names a living artist as its model).

## IV. HYPERPOP & DIGICORE — the exaggeration's lineage

### SOPHIE (1986–2021)
- Producer and artist who rebuilt pop's surfaces from synthesis up;
  came out publicly as a trans woman with "It's Okay to Cry" (2017)
  and the PAPER interview ("transness is taking control... there's no
  longer an expectation based on the body you were born into"). Cited
  here for documented craft: sound-as-material synthesis, the
  artificial made emotional.
- **Insight:** artifice as sincerity — the theoretical spine of
  letting a varispeed artifact carry real feeling. Craft first; her
  death is not the story.
- Sources: [PAPER interview](https://www.papermag.com/sophie-pride),
  [Billboard on the PAPER interview](https://www.billboard.com/culture/pride/sophie-paper-magazine-interview-trans-8461985/),
  [Xtra remembrance](https://xtramagazine.com/culture/sophie-xeon-obit-lgbtq-trans-194500).
- **Status:** CONTEXT (scene page; no dossier imitates her sound).

### Laura Les (100 gecs)
- Producer/vocalist; trans woman; on record that pitched-up vocal
  processing made recording bearable, and later that voice lessons
  let her record unpitched ("As I've been exploring my voice more,
  I'm like, 'I can do this'").
- **Insight:** the documented arc from pitch-as-shelter to voice-as-
  chosen — the emotional truth behind §8's "stable wrongness that can
  be lived in."
- Sources: [Dr. Ruth Pearce tag archive](https://ruthpearce.net/tag/laura-les/),
  [Avidly — Notes on 100 Gecs](https://avidly.org/2021/01/14/notes-on-100-gecs/),
  [trans vocal modulation overview](https://www.maxmadethat.com/research/transvocalmodulation).
- **Status:** USED (§8; §II's listening-culture entry).

### Arca
- Venezuelan producer, singer and performer based in Barcelona; came
  out as nonbinary in 2018 and has described herself as a trans woman;
  uses she/her and it/its (and at times they/them). PAPER cover:
  "an ecosystem of minor self-states." Cited for documented practice:
  the mutable voice as compositional material across the KICK cycle.
- **Insight:** multiplicity as a musical form — one performer, many
  registers — background for a Songbook where one singer's versions
  proliferate.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arca_(musician)),
  [PAPER "Transformation" cover](https://www.papermag.com/arca-transformation).
- **Status:** CONTEXT (scene page).

### Jane Remover
- New Jersey-raised producer/songwriter; came out as a trans woman
  (documented across Stereogum and other interviews); *Frailty*
  (deadAir, 2021) defined digicore's bedroom-epic register; coined
  and outgrew "dariacore" mashup style.
- **Insight:** the bedroom-upload economy — scene infrastructure made
  of Discord servers and pseudonyms, the closest living kin to the
  binder's anonymous spool culture.
- Sources: [Stereogum profile/interview](https://stereogum.com/2239094/jane-remover-cant-stop-transforming/interviews),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Remover),
  [Frailty album documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frailty_(Jane_Remover_album)).
- **Status:** USED (track_108's upload-ethos line; scene page).

## V. JUNGLE & THE BREAK — the drum's lineage

### The Winstons — "Amen, Brother" (1969); Gregory Coleman; Richard L. Spencer
- The B-side whose six-second drum break, played by Gregory Coleman,
  became the most-sampled recording in music. Neither Coleman (died
  2006, homeless) nor copyright holder Spencer ever received
  royalties; a 2015 crowdfunding campaign by DJ Martyn Webster raised
  ~£24,000 which Spencer received before his death.
- **Insight:** the ethics core of §6's no-sampling rule and the
  fiction's drummer-named-first labels. This collection performs its
  own breaks *because* of this history, and says so.
- Sources: [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/amen-break-creator-getting-long-delayed-compensation-for-iconic-sample-118818/),
  [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/amen-break-arranger-finally-receives-royalties-news),
  [Wikipedia — Amen break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amen_break),
  [Wikipedia — The Winstons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winstons).
- **Status:** USED (track_110; §6 rule 6).

### A Guy Called Gerald (Gerald Simpson)
- Black British producer from Manchester: "Voodoo Ray" (1988), often
  cited as the UK's first acid house record; his Juice Box label
  12"s and "28 Gun Bad Boy" (1992) are heralded as jungle's
  blueprint; *Black Secret Technology* (1995) redefined it.
- **Insight:** jungle as Black British invention with named authors —
  the corrective to "the scene did it"; break-chop as compositional
  writing, not decoration.
- Sources: [artist Bandcamp](https://aguycalledgerald.bandcamp.com/album/28-gun-bad-boy)
  (official listening), [DJ Mag on *Black Secret Technology*](https://djmag.com/features/how-guy-called-geralds-black-secret-technology-redefined-jungle),
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Guy_Called_Gerald).
- **Status:** USED (track_110's break-science lineage).

### 4hero / Reinforced Records (Dego McFarlane & Marc "Mac" Clair)
- Black British producers from Dollis Hill; founded Reinforced (1989);
  "Mr Kirk's Nightmare" launched the label; Reinforced became one of
  jungle's foundational institutions (early home to Goldie).
- **Insight:** the label-as-neighborhood model — a family operation
  pressing its own community's records — the direct model for the
  fiction's shopfront labels.
- Sources: [Wikipedia — 4hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4hero),
  [Wikipedia — Reinforced Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforced_Records),
  [Red Bull Music Academy — Reinforced interview](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/04/reinforced-interview/).
- **Status:** USED (track_110; §V vocabulary).

### Sherelle
- London DJ/label founder; Black and gay (self-described across
  interviews); plays 160+ BPM jungle/footwork hybrids; founded
  BEAUTIFUL, a label and platform elevating Black and LGBTQ+
  artists with studio space and education.
- **Insight:** the contemporary proof that the fast end of the
  continuum is a living Black queer institution-building practice —
  and the platform model (studio + academy) the Music & Sources surface
  points money toward.
- Sources: [Mixmag cover feature](https://mixmag.net/feature/sherelle-cover-feature-hooversound-interview-footwork-jungle),
  [DJ Mag longread](https://djmag.com/longreads/sherelle-force-nature),
  [Attitude interview](https://www.attitude.co.uk/culture/sexuality/sherelle-is-changing-the-game-for-black-queer-artists-304156/).
- **Status:** USED (scene page; §V).

### Nia Archives
- Bradford/Leeds-raised jungle producer of Jamaican heritage; credits
  her grandmother's soundsystem culture; family maintains a jungle-
  preservation radio project (the "Bradford 5"); debut LP *Silence Is
  Loud* (2024) carried the revival to award stages.
- **Insight:** jungle inheritance as *household* labor — grandmothers
  and sisters keeping the music — the documented thread behind
  track_111/112's family fictions.
- Sources: [The Face interview](https://theface.com/music/nia-archives-interview-silence-is-loud-new-album-britpop-jungle-goldie),
  [Billboard interview](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/nia-archives-interview-silence-is-loud-album-tour-1235843156/),
  [GRAMMY.com interview](https://www.grammy.com/news/nia-archives-interview-new-album-silence-is-loud-videos).
- **Status:** USED (tracks 111, 112 lineage lines; scene page).

## VI. CROSS-SCENE PRACTICES

### Deaf Rave (Troi Lee, founder), London, 2003–
- Collective of Deaf DJs, BSL rappers and performers; the world's
  first rave organization for deaf and hard-of-hearing people; bass
  vibration, haptic wearables and visual performance as primary
  channels. Lee's own documented route in: jungle raves at 18,
  turntables in his twenties.
- **Insight:** music received as physical vibration rather than
  airborne sound — the closest living practice to this game's

## VII. OMISSIONS, EXCLUSIONS AND OPEN CONCERNS — the honesty ledger

- **Excluded: named happy hardcore DJs as dossier lineage.** The
  scene history is cited collectively (§III); no living DJ is named
  as a track's model, because the transform is a process, not a
  person's sound.
- **Excluded: Kemistry (Kemistry & Storm), DJ Rashad.** Both belong
  in any full jungle/footwork history; both died young, and citing
  them *only* in a game's credits risks the tragedy-quota failure
  this commission bans. RESERVE for a future scene page written
  with room to honor the work properly.
- **Omitted identity claims:** several artists considered for the
  hyperpop section (umru, underscores, Fraxiom, Dorian Electra,
  glaive and digicore peers) were left out rather than carry
  identity or biography statements this pass did not verify to the
  §0 standard. RESERVE — verification is a production task, not a
  reason to guess.
- **The Bert Williams question.** His career is inseparable from
  the blackface convention of his era; the Music & Sources card states it
  as documented fact with the craft foregrounded. If the owner
  prefers, track_108 survives with the lineage line reduced to the
  composition credits alone. Flagged, not decided unilaterally.
- **"Yes! We Have No Bananas" and caricature.** The song's origin
  is an affectionate immigrant idiom, and its era also produced
  ugly ethnic novelty; the dossier keeps the documented origin
  (Silver's own account) and bans dialect comedy from the base's
  vocal direction. Flagged for owner review.
- **Aloha ʻOe and annexation.** The Music & Sources card states
  the 1893 overthrow and the queen's imprisonment as documented
  history; the in-game fiction does not dramatize it. Flagged as
  the intended treatment of colonial context: stated, sourced,
  not decorated.
- **The owner's YouTube reference** is a compilation of other
  people's uploads; it anchors register only. Nothing from it is
  reproduced, and the Music & Sources surface links scenes and artists, not
  the compilation.
- **Track 2's EU instrumental status is a counsel question, not a
  finding.** Under Directive 2011/77/EU a musical composition with
  words created together is protected to 70 years after the last
  surviving of lyricist and composer; this research previously
  implied "music GREEN, lyrics AMBER," which the owner corrected
  (Q2). Music Bible §13 carries the dated HOLD table; nothing here
  asserts EU clearance for Gondola no Uta in any layer.
- **The Prisoner's Song is HOLD, not GREEN-with-note** (Q2): its
  tangled authorship conflicts with the project's own
  provenance-integrity rule — the same rule that blocked Tiger Rag
  — so it is off the audition slate until the authorship story can
  be told honestly.
- **Vocal provenance moved, research unmoved:** the Q4 relocation
  of singer fictions from bases into seeded house takes changes no
  entry in this document — the real people studied here informed
  treatments and community practices, never any specific fictional
  voice.
- **Link rot risk:** all URLs retrieved 2026-08-14 via live search;
  the Music & Sources build must re-verify every link and prefer
  artist-controlled pages where any have moved.

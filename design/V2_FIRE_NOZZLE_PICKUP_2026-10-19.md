## Physical behaviour

Releasing a nozzle no longer centres, rotates or attaches it at a hidden
100-micrometre datum. Actual mouse travel inserts it into a narrow axial
entry guide. It remains withdrawable and unsealed. Turning the nut in the
tightening direction picks up the native helix at the matching relative
phase; further turning drives the original pitch to the sealing plane.
Loosening alone cannot initiate engagement. Neither acquisition nor release
performs the positioning or repair.

The entry is a kinematic mechanical constraint, like other guided parts;
it is not a claim of full rigid-body thread simulation. Centring occurs
during insertion motion within the measured mouth clearance. The pilot
retains the centred axis before engagement, and outward carrying frees it.
This constraint is derived from the saved physical pose; no extra authority
or parallel persistent state was added. Existing maintenance, inspection,
certification, inventory, household and camera owners remain.

The owning Blender builder now certifies the entire centred entry interval
using 129 axial slabs. Each slab bounds the nut's complete inner projected
surfaces and the spigot's swept outer surfaces. Nonplanar quads cover every
triangulation. The resulting minimum radial gap is 44.794 micrometres for
all axial positions in the interval and all relative rotations. This is a
continuous conservative bound, separate from the earlier sampled pose study.
The measured flared-mouth entry radius is 1.201 mm. That mouth measurement
is not a certificate for arbitrary off-axis swept trajectories. Other world
obstacles still constrain centring and movement.

Visual GLBs, UVs, PBR definitions and mip policies are unchanged in this stage.
The native6 runtime projection is byte-identical to native5; native6 strengthens
the construction certificate's treatment of quad diagonals. Earlier receipts
retain their original sources and hashes.

# THE DREAM WORLD — TARGET IMAGE

Written 2026-08-18. Second pass: the first version rendered the gold as
*ornament applied to a wall* — a Klimt frieze, a beautiful surface. That was a
misread of the ruling and it sold the wrong game.

**The correction, in the owner's words:** *"we want reality itself to be tearing
apart from golden tentacles with eyes made of gold melting through the 7th
dimension, not a painting on the wall."*

So the gold is not on the building. The gold is **behind** the building, it is
**alive**, and it is **coming through**. The Klimt vocabulary survives only as
the shape of the intrusion — the spiral, the tesserae, the eye — never as
decoration laid on a surface. The lamp does not illuminate a mural. The lamp
**thins reality where it lands** and lets the thing behind it push in.

**The one-line thesis.** *The corridor is real. It is also a skin, and there is
something enormous and golden on the other side of it that can see you.*

---

## THE HERO PROMPT

> First-person view down a cramped 1930s New York apartment-house service
> corridor at night — two metres wide, three metres to a pressed-tin ceiling.
> Worn, ordinary, real. A single handheld service lamp throws one narrow cone
> forward.
>
> **Reality is tearing open.** Where the lamp's light lands, the corridor is
> not being decorated — it is being *breached*. Plaster splits and curls back
> like burnt skin, lath snapping outward, and behind it there is no wall and no
> building: there is a lattice of living gold going down forever, a structure
> far too large to fit inside the room that contains it.
>
> **Golden tentacles erupt through the tear into the hallway.** Thick, molten,
> cabled, wet with liquid metal — some as thick as a torso, some hair-fine,
> branching and coiling in three dimensions. They do not lie flat on the wall.
> They occupy the corridor. They cross in front of the viewer, above the head,
> underfoot. Their surfaces are worked into spiralling filigree and tesserae —
> not painted on, but *grown that way*, like scales, like ammonite.
>
> **Eyes open along the tentacles.** Eyes made of gold — lidded, wet, mobile,
> at wildly different scales: clusters of tiny ones like roe along a thin
> tendril, one enormous eye deeper in the breach, all of them tracking the
> viewer.
>
> **The rupture does not recede in any direction the corridor has.** Inside the
> tear, space folds through itself: tesseract cross-sections, corridors nested
> inside corridors at impossible foreshortening, the same doorway repeating
> inward along an axis that is not depth. It is visibly larger on the inside.
> Geometry that cannot be drawn arriving from a direction that is not there.
>
> **Away from the beam the corridor is completely intact and utterly mundane**
> — grey-green plaster, worn terrazzo, water-stained tin ceiling, a dim
> ankle-height wash, then absolute black. That contrast is the horror of the
> frame: an ordinary back hallway with a hole torn in reality standing in it.
>
> The gold is **metal, not light.** It does not glow. It catches the lamp in
> hard speculars and is otherwise as dark as everything else.
>
> Cosmic horror. Architectural body-horror. Non-Euclidean. Awe and dread, and
> no decoration anywhere.
>
> Photoreal interior, physically based molten metal, volumetric three-
> dimensional forms, 35 mm, shallow depth of field, high dynamic range, deep
> true blacks, extreme contrast between the breach and the dark.

**Negative / must-not:** not a mural, not a painting, not wallpaper, not gold
leaf on a flat surface, no decorative frieze or border, no art-nouveau panel, no
ornament confined to the wall plane; no fog or volumetric haze, no lens flare,
no bloom wash, no emissive self-glowing gold, no uniform ambient fill, no
cathedral or palace scale, no dutch angle, no chromatic aberration.

## THREE SUPPORTING FRAMES

**1 — The scar.** A patch of wall the breach has already left. Plaster healed
badly over something that was there: a dry, dark, eye-shaped pucker with cold
gold residue crusted in the seam, and *nothing behind it any more*. The lamp
falls full on it and it stays dead. Danger this building remembers from
somebody else's night. It must read as a closed wound, never as a live threat.

**2 — The only mirror.** A tentacle gone fully molten, its surface a still pool
of liquid gold — and the reflection in it is not the corridor. It is the
residents of the building, standing in gold mosaic robes, faces and hands
painted in flesh. One figure among them has no face. They exist in no room and
can only be caught at the right angle in melted metal.

**3 — The room that repeats.** The same corridor twice, side by side,
identical in every measurement and every stain — except the door is in the
wrong wall. Nothing else differs. No gold at all. The horror is the noticing.

## THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Gold is metallic, never emissive.** It cannot light itself. This is why
   the world is only terrible where the player is looking, and why raising the
   lamp is a decision rather than a state.
2. **The gold is volumetric and alive.** It comes *through* surfaces and
   occupies space. The moment it reads as a pattern lying on a wall, the image
   is selling the wrong game.
3. **The dark must stay navigable.** The real building underneath is dimly
   readable at the feet — the player can move without the lamp; they simply
   cannot see what is coming through, or where the hazards are.
4. **Recognition lives in the bones, not the surface.** Every dimension is
   measured from the waking Orison — 2.08 m corridors, 0.91 m doors, 3.015 m
   ceilings. It is the right size to be home and nothing else about it is.
5. **The eye means danger.** Of the five motifs, the eye is the one the player
   must learn: anything that can hurt them watches them. In a maze built to
   lose you, the pattern says what a thing *is*.
6. **Safety, and these two survive every ruling:** no flashing at
   photosensitive frequencies, and no forced camera roll, fisheye or chromatic
   assault. However much the geometry folds, the camera stays level and the
   cuts stay slow.

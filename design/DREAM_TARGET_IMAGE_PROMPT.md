# THE DREAM WORLD — TARGET IMAGE

Written 2026-08-18 to give the fractal Orison a picture to be judged against.
Everything here is already ruled and mostly already built; this is the frame
those rulings are aiming at, not a new proposal.

**The one-line thesis.** *The building is real and ordinary until you look at
it, and looking at it is what turns it to gold.*

---

## THE HERO PROMPT

> First-person view down a narrow 1930s New York apartment-house service
> corridor at night. Two metres wide, three metres to a pressed-tin ceiling —
> the cramped, honest proportions of a real back hallway, not a fantasy hall.
> A single handheld service lamp throws one narrow warm cone forward down the
> passage.
>
> **Where the lamp falls, the building has turned.** Plaster becomes Gustav
> Klimt's Stoclet Frieze in beaten gold leaf: coiling spiral tendrils, dense
> mosaic tesserae underfoot, hammered sheet with visible leaf seams, fine
> craquelure, thick impasto ridges catching the light edge-on. The gold is
> *metal, not light* — it does not glow, it catches. Burnished, specular,
> embossed in real relief.
>
> **Where the lamp does not reach, the same corridor is ordinary and cold.**
> Grey-green plaster, worn terrazzo, a water-stained tin ceiling. Only a dim
> ankle-height wash shows where the floor stops. Absolute black beyond it — no
> fog, no haze, no atmospheric glow.
>
> **The frontier between the two is the subject of the picture:** a hard,
> living boundary where ornament is overtaking reality, gold bleeding into
> plaster mid-wall, half-formed tendrils dying out at the edge of the beam.
>
> Far down the hall, through a doorway the viewer has not reached, a single
> warm bare bulb burns in a room beyond — an invitation, not an exit.
>
> At the dim edge of the beam, worked into the wall pattern and only half
> revealed, a cluster of stylised Byzantine eyes.
>
> Mood: opulent and airless. Sacred and wrong. Beautiful only where you are
> looking. Klimt by way of a maintenance corridor.
>
> Photoreal architectural interior, physically based metal, 35 mm, shallow
> depth of field, high dynamic range, deep true blacks, heavy contrast between
> the lit pool and the dark.

**Negative / must-not:** no fog or volumetric haze, no lens flare, no bloom
wash, no emissive or self-glowing gold, no uniform ambient fill, no cathedral
or palace scale, no fantasy ornament, no visible tiling repeat, no colour
fringing, no dutch angle.

---

## THREE SUPPORTING FRAMES

**1 — The scar.** Same corridor, lamp full on a patch of wall and floor that
*refuses to turn*: a dark eye-shaped stain in the plaster with gold blooming
all around it and stopping at its edge. Danger this building remembers from
somebody else's night. It must read as a mark, not as a threat.

**2 — The only mirror.** A wall gone fully molten — liquid gold, a still
metal pool — and in its reflection, not the corridor, but a Klimt tableau of
the building's residents in gold mosaic robes, faces and hands painted in
flesh tones. One figure among them has no face. This exists in no room and can
only be caught at the right angle in melted metal.

**3 — The room that repeats.** Two corridors side by side, the same room
twice, identical in every measurement and dressing — except the door is in the
wrong wall. Nothing else differs. The horror is the noticing.

---

## THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

1. **Gold is metallic, never emissive.** It cannot light itself. This is why
   the world is only beautiful where the player is looking, and why being lit
   is a decision rather than a state.
2. **The dark must stay navigable.** The real building underneath is dimly
   readable at the feet — the player can move without the lamp; they simply
   cannot see what the building has become, or where the hazards are.
3. **Recognition lives in the bones, not the surface.** Every dimension is
   measured from the waking Orison — 2.08 m corridors, 0.91 m doors, 3.015 m
   ceilings. It is the right size to be home and nothing else about it is.
4. **Ornament is wayfinding.** Five motifs, one per surface class: spiral
   tendrils on walls, mosaic on floors, canopy-over-starfield overhead,
   chevrons on doors, watching eyes on anything that can hurt you. In a maze
   built to lose you, the pattern says what a thing *is*.
5. **Safety, and these two survive every ruling:** no flashing at
   photosensitive frequencies, and no forced camera roll, fisheye or chromatic
   assault.

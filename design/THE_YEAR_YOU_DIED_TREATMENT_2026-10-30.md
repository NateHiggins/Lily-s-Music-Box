## 1. The story

**Who she was.** Adela Voss is a trans woman, thirty-eight, and a portrait
photographer. She keeps Photo Supplies in the Vantry Arcade, directly across
Jackson Avenue from the Orison's front door. [v4, built-world ruling]

- She changes her shop window whenever a woman somewhere does something the
  papers call plucky. She pins the patronising headline beside it with a
  correction card in grease-pencil capitals.
- She enters the Speaker's Pun Derby every week and loses.
- She keeps the negative.
- She is good company and a sharp edit, and she is sometimes wrong about who a
  picture belongs to [WR-07].
- Her name and gender are ordinary knowledge from the first scene. They are
  never a concealed fact, a body reveal or a password [packet §1].

**What she found.** In June 1929 she copied the Arcade's deed for the tenants'
counsel. Its schedule showed the Orison's own parcel across the street: the
Kessler Estate's ground lease on it ends on 1 August 1929. She meant to show the
Orison's tenants on Monday, 22 July. Being decent, she sent Mae Kessler a copy
first. [v3, canon]

**How she died.** On Friday, 19 July 1929, in the storm that put a twister
across Hell Gate, Mae pushed her from the rear roof parapet at about 9:03 p.m.

- Mae had built an alibi from last week's recorded radio drama.
- She took the exposed plate from Adela's camera.
- Lightning had silenced the house at 9:01 p.m., and it stayed deaf until 3:02
  a.m. Nothing of those hours is in the record. [canon §VIII.5.o]

**The frame.** Adela wakes inside the night man.

- The night man is a construct the Tenant set into the vacant night-maintenance
  tenancy to explore the bridge it has built through time. [canon §I]
- The residents see a tired, competent maintenance tenant and call it "the
  night man". Adela, the companion and the interface use her name.
- The companion is a Vantry service set the Tenant amended into a crude
  intermediary. Everything written on its phosphor paper is read, later, by the
  Tenant, which holds that paper in the future. [canon §I, §VIII.5.k]
- The bridge lasts as long as Adela takes to let go of her attachments to this
  world. That is why it spans the campaign year. [canon §I]

**What the year is for.** Adela investigates her own murder from inside someone
else's working day.

- She meets herself alive at her window.
- She finds her own death notice on a newspaper's back page, wrong.
- She lets go of the things she loved, one at a time, in whatever order the
  player reaches them.
- The night man she is wearing learns from her. It turns out to be the one she
  thanked in July for the loan of the roof key: she lent the key to herself.
  [canon §I; v5 loop ruling]
- At the end of the year, the construct (the player's shadow) attempts one
  unfinished sentence of its own: "I WOULD LIKE TO". [canon §I]
- Finishing it is the player's last choice. [v6 §2]

## 3. The case: three lines of inquiry and an example route [WR-01, WR-02]

The 25 dated beats are an **example route through the evidence**, not a quest
ladder. Every authored date and carrier is kept. The arrows between them are
leads, not prerequisites. [WR-01]

**What visits do and don't do.**

- A clue that is visible on a visit is visible, even if the player has not yet
  done the beat that "explains" it.
- Residents answer only from their own knowledge and the current encounter.
  They never know the player's unshared visits.
- A date visited out of order can expose a damning object. It still does not
  explain the lease, authenticate a source, settle a time or tell Adela what to
  do with what she knows. [WR-01]

**The three lines of inquiry** [WR-01]:

1. **The missing authority.** The deed and its schedule, the parcel, the lease
   term, the August collections, Mae's access, and the original
   correspondence.
2. **The manufactured evening.** The lobby clock's offset, the replayed drama,
   roof access, the taken keys, the shop recording, the glove and the damaged
   plate.
3. **The life and its continuation.** The window, her work, her friendships,
   the negatives, the borrowed gestures, and the final request.

**What the solve needs** [WR-02]:

- A stronger, independently supported case against Mae that survives the
  reasonable competing explanations.
- It does **not** need to prove seventeen other people physically incapable.
- Every resident stays in the story, but each holds one useful thing (access, a
  custody, a sighting, a disputed interval, a reason to ask) instead of taking
  a turn at "implicated" and "cleared".
- "Cleared for this interval" is reserved for corroborated evidence that really
  excludes an action in that interval.

**The lease's practical consequence** (**PROPOSED** fictional facts, pending
period property-law review) [WR-03]:

- **The deed.** The Arcade deed's schedule conveys the Arcade's ground rent
  "together with the reversion of the adjoining parcel" at the end of its ground
  lease to the Kessler Estate, 1 August 1929. That explains why a deed for the
  Arcade mentions the Orison's lot.
- **The receipt.** Mae's ledger box holds an **August 1929 rent receipt**,
  issued in the Estate's name for an Orison flat after that date.
- **What the player connects:** parcel, term, named collector, dated
  collection.
- **What Peter and Ewald say, and its limit.** Peter or Mr. Ewald explains the
  consequence and stops at it: the receipt shows the Estate collected after the
  term. Whether it was entitled to is a question for a court, not for a
  receipt. The companion can report the comparison. No court quest or legal
  subsystem is added.
- **Mae's stake.** The negatives threatened a concrete continuing income and
  her claim to custody of the building's story. That is why she sent for them.
  [WR-03]

## 4. The dark hours and the limits of the evidence [WR-04]

None of these repairs reveals the murder. The absence is part of the case, and
the writing observes it too.

### 4.1 One precision convention for the boundary

**The conflict.** v3–v6 printed "NO RECORD. 21:01 TO 03:02" but also showed the
player a stroke at **21:01:15**, and put the hum's drop on Cal's tape at
21:01:15. That puts fifteen seconds of visible action inside the forbidden
interval.

**The resolution.**

- **Minute precision, matching the calendar owner.** The silence begins at
  **21:01** and ends at **03:02**. There is no visible or audible instant
  inside 21:01–03:02.
- **The first stroke and the house going deaf are one event, at 21:01.** Cal's
  tape logs the hum's drop at its 21:01 mark, by Cal's counter; that is a
  private carrier and not the house. Every other "21:01:15" in v3–v6 is
  withdrawn.
- **The visit ends at the last frame before 21:01.** A white-out transition at
  the boundary is a presentation option, not a new timestamp. The authored
  weather schedule is not rewritten: its storm frame begins at 21:00.
- **Cal's earlier marks stand, because they are before the boundary.** "There."
  at 21:00:40 and Iris's hammer at 21:00:50 are both outside the gap.
  - **Limit [WR-02]:** a voice at 21:00:40 does not by itself exclude movement
    before about 21:03. Cal is not cleared by it. He simply is not where the
    evidence converges.
- **Plate 13 is a private record of the boundary instant, not of the gap.** Its
  shutter stood open and the 21:01 stroke exposed it. It shows the moment the
  dark began. It does not show 21:03.

### 4.2 Reconciling 03:02 and 03:05

**The conflict.** The house resumes at 03:02. v3–v6 also had Teresa discovering
the body at 03:05, and her half-spoken name as the first sound at 03:02.

**The resolution.** No action is silently moved. The discovery time changes
explicitly.

| Time | What happens | Carrier |
|---|---|---|
| about **03:00** | Teresa comes down for her Saturday laundry hour (canon block). Omar arrives for his breaker check (canon block) | schedules |
| about **03:01** | Teresa sees a sleeve through the laundry window and goes out to the yard. **This is before the record resumes**, so it is known from her testimony, not the house | Teresa's statement |
| **03:02** | Omar finds the house breaker tripped and resets it. The house hears again. Its first sound is Teresa, already kneeling, partway through Adela's name | the resumed record |
| about **03:05** | Omar comes out into the yard and finds her | the record; Omar |
| 03:40 | The patrolman; the Guests' "lodger" | the patrolman's sheet |

Visits can begin at 03:02, no earlier.

### 4.3 Things found later that come from inside the gap

These are private carriers that survive the gap. None of them opens a visit
inside it.

- **The Guests' sighting** at about 22:50 ("a lady in gloves going down, with a
  lamp she did not light"). The carrier is their testimony, given when someone
  finally asks a concrete question (S08's method). Their agreement makes them
  neither truthful nor innocent.
- **The shop cylinder,** 22:58–23:40. Omar's door recorder runs on its own wet
  cell "if the house goes out" [S07]. It records the bell, footsteps, drawers
  and a faint squeak on brass. It does not record a face or a name: "Bell
  doesn't tell it who came in."
- **Rhea's take** (her own recorder) and **Juno's wall tape** (her own deck).
  These are private devices, not the house.
- **Mina's pocket log,** 23:36. Paper.
- **Jonah's notebook page,** about 21:05. Paper. What he composed is not what he
  saw [B16].

### 4.4 Evidence claims, cut back to what each carrier supports

- **The stopped watch: an estimate with a reason.**
  - The patrolman's list records the crystal shattered and the balance stopped
    by the impact. That is why the hands stand at 9:07.
  - The four-minute offset was witnessed at 18:05, when Adela set the watch by
    the lobby master clock in front of the player (B08).
  - Together they support **about 9:03**, a reconstructed estimate, consistent
    with Cal's tape (she was alive at the parapet before 21:01) and with
    Teresa's attributed estimate ("some six hours"). The watch is not an
    eyewitness.
- **N16's "after a lightning stroke" is not "struck by lightning".**
  - The paper recorded a storm and assumed an accident. The correction answers
    that assumption with "The paper gave no eyewitness for its account of an
    accident" [packet §7].
  - It makes no medical finding. Teresa's "no burns" stays her attributed
    observation, not a companion diagnosis.
- **Plate 13: the surviving image, with an intelligible viewpoint.**
  - The camera stood at the rear parapet, angled along the coping toward the
    roof tank so the storm would rise behind it. Plate 12 framed a tank leg the
    same way.
  - The 21:01 stroke exposed it. The fire in September cooked half the
    emulsion.
  - **What survives:** the parapet; Adela turning toward the flash; the tank's
    edge; at that edge, a pale raised shape consistent with a gloved hand.
  - **No brooch, face or gesture is restored into it** [WR-04]. v4–v6's brooch
    silhouette is withdrawn. The identification of Mae does not rest on the
    plate. It rests on convergence:
    1. the disc alibi (B19);
    2. the button and the mended glove (B17, B20);
    3. the brass squeak on the cylinder, beside her known habit of drawing a
       gloved finger along the mail-bank brass (canon schedule);
    4. Vantry's sighting (B24);
    5. the box's contents and the August receipt (B25, WR-03).

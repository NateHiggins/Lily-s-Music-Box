# Neighbourhood arrangement — direction and axis ruling, 2026-08-29

Work order, not proof — named so the completeness ledger refuses it.

## Binding ruling: the world is +z

**The shipped v1 convention wins.** The street runs outward from the
front door along Godot **+z**; the forward-path world's mirrored
apron is the thing that moves.

The inventory found the two worlds are mirror images about the
building: v1's street at +z, v2's review apron at −z. Nobody noticed
because v2's exterior stops at a 6.0 × 3.35 m apron with nothing
outside it to disagree. Every exterior coordinate authored after this
point would otherwise have been authored twice.

Everything already expressed in +z stays: the three street bands
(north pavement z 9.45–14.75, carriageway 14.75–23.894, south
pavement 23.894–28.316), the arcade throat's portal plane at
z 28.316, Harukiya's envelope, the traffic lanes, and the six
duplicated copies of the street section that now have one truth to
converge on.

## Standing licence from the owner

> "Move and orient and even completely reimagine any of the elements
> to help give the whole area character, flow, whatever else is
> necessary. The rule of cool and future expansion proving is
> priority."

Two priorities, and the second disciplines the first.

**Rule of cool** — current arrangement has no claim on us. If moving a
building, re-siting a trade or re-cutting a route makes the
neighbourhood more characterful, move it.

**Future expansion proving** — the first of anything must be built as
the **template for the Nth**. The first cell teaches how cells are
made; the first portal, how portals are made; the first shop bucket,
how shops are simulated; the first world edge, how the map grows. A
vertical slice that proves only itself has failed. Judge every piece
by: *is piece two now cheap?*

## Three arrangement moves the inventory made possible

### 1. The Arcade stops being a cul-de-sac: its back stairs come down into Harukiya

The Vantry Arcade is a 20 × 26 m glass hall with eleven fitted shops,
one entrance, a solid end wall at z 64.6, no second exit and no
vertical. That is a dead end wearing a nave's clothes.

The fix is already sitting in the coordinates. Harukiya occupies
x −12…6.4, z 28.32…38.20, **below grade** to y −3.35. The hall
occupies x 4…24, z 38.6…64.6. They overlap in x, sit 0.4 m apart in z,
and are separated only in height. **A service stair from the hall's
west end descends south into the bar.**

What that buys: the arcade gains a second exit and a vertical; the bar
gains a back entrance that isn't the street; the player who goes deep
comes out somewhere they did not expect but can immediately place; and
the route gets the brief's compression-and-release rhythm honestly —
NARROW service stair into OPEN sunken bar. It costs one stair and
fixes a structural flaw in the best interior we own.

### 2. One street end becomes a scaffolding dogleg that continues; the other stays a wall

Today both ends of the street (x −20.10 and +20.60) are construction
terminators — and they are built well, with three named collision
spans, timber posts, baked wet board and a storm curtain that lets
traffic vanish into weather rather than into a fence.

Keep the west end exactly as it is: it is the proof that the world can
**end** convincingly. Turn the east end into a **sidewalk shed that
turns a corner** — a covered scaffolding tunnel with a dogleg, ending
at a barrier that is plainly temporary rather than plainly final.

This is the expansion-proving move. The east end becomes the template
for how the map grows: a corner is an occluder, a streaming gate, a
compression point and a promise, all at once. When the next block is
authored it attaches there, and nothing about the world edge has to be
reinvented — we will have already built the pattern once and tested it.

### 3. The bodega's back room opens into the Orison's own basement

Earlier direction linked the bodega's back room to the arcade service
corridor. The measured geography kills that: the bodega is 18.78 m
east of the front door and the arcade throat is diagonally across the
street — close enough that the link would read as a shortcut rather
than a violation.

Link it **down into B1** instead: the corner shop's stockroom opens
into the player's own cellar.

Why this is the stronger door:

- **It violates home.** §27 says teach the ruler before breaking it.
  The player learns the Orison's dimensions better than anywhere; the
  basement is inside that learned space. A door that arrives there
  from outside is worth ten impossible alleys between places the
  player barely knows.
- **It is genuinely useful.** Parts bought at the notions counter
  reach the boiler without crossing the street. Comprehension buys
  travel time, which is the reward loop we want.
- **It lands where the deep material lives.** B1 is where §10's
  cyclopean payload belongs — foundation blocks larger than the
  street could have delivered. Walking into your own foundation
  through a shop's stockroom is the game's thesis in one door.

The arcade-side link is not discarded, only deferred: it becomes the
*second* impossible adjacency, once the first has taught the player
that back rooms lie.

## Trades follow their streets

The eleven shops are fitted interiors, not facades — the geometry
exists. What they lack is simulation. With arrangement licence, they
should also sit where their trade wants to be:

**Onto the street** (trades that want passers-by and give the daily
walk life): news & cigars as a kiosk, keys cut, shoe rebuilding, and
the model laundry — whose steam vents to the pavement, which is
simultaneously sensory, atmospheric and an occluder the brief wants
anyway.

**Staying in the arcade** (trades that want shelter, browsing and
strangeness): photo supplies, radio service, pawnbroker, funeral
parlour, luncheonette, and **Otis & Son** — whose name is already the
elevator in your building. An elevator company with a showroom two
blocks from a lift you maintain is a gift: it is where the machine can
be explained, mis-explained, or found to have no surviving records.

A funeral parlour inside a shopping arcade needs no justification
beyond being exactly true to the period and exactly the right amount
of wrong.

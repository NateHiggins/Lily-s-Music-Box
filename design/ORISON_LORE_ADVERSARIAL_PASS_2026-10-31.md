# Queens 1928-29: adversarial lore pass

Evidence class: **INERT**. A writers' room proposal for the owner, dated 2026-10-31. Her
rulings on it are in section 6. Anything she hasn't ruled on is not canon. Sources are at
the end.

The input is an outside reference packet, "Queens 1928-29". The room ran two passes. The
first gave each idea its best case, attacked it and kept what survived. A second reader,
who hadn't seen that reasoning, attacked the result against the Bible, the treatment, the
dialogue data and the papers. Section 2 says what that second pass broke, because it broke
a lot.

Kept ideas carry an Accord 10 class: **CANONICAL**, **INFERRED**, **ADAPTATION** or
**NECESSITY**. "Treatment" below means **design/THE_YEAR_YOU_DIED_TREATMENT_2026-10-30.md**.

## 1. The verdict

The packet describes a different game. Its protagonist is a speakeasy matriarch, alive in
October 1929, killed by a weapon hidden in a boiler. The Bible has a portrait photographer
pushed from a roof on 19 July 1929. Adopted whole it undoes the ruled murder.

The canon is stronger here than the packet expected. It already has the anti-trope motive,
the wrong official account, the absence, the heat, the crash with the ticker behind, and a
tenants' rent hold. So little needs adding, and most new plot weight broke something.

## 2. What the second pass broke

Each of these was in my first draft as an adoption, and each is now cut or reduced.

**Adela's negatives as blackmail: I cut this, and the owner overruled me.** Section 6 has
her ruling and how it now works. The objections below are kept because the design has to
answer them.
- A trans woman secretly holding her neighbours' secrets is the predatory, deceptive figure
  the story exists to refuse, and "learned late" brings back a late reveal about her character.
- It makes her decency into a threat. "Being decent, she sent Mae Kessler a copy first" drives the
  plot. From a known blackmailer, a dated copy reads as a demand.
- The treatment says each resident holds one thing "instead of taking a turn at 'implicated' and
  'cleared'", and that "privacy never reads as guilt". A suspect pool built from her register
  reverses that.
- Juno says she moved the microphone "nicely" and "never told a soul".

Her other vices are already canon: she keeps the negative and won't hand it over, she's a sharp edit, she
loses the Pun Derby, and she is sometimes wrong about who a picture belongs to (WR-07).

**A printed "passion theory": cut.** It sexualises her death and makes it about what she is, and
its carve-out swallowed the law in **AL-01**. Today's errors in the record are banal: lodging and cause
of death. That banality is the insult, and it is better.

**Residents hiding her plates from the police: cut.** No police are reading plates: the sheet is one
patrolman's and the record calls it an accident. It would give Mae's "emptied a dead woman's drawer" an
innocent reading, and it would kill Sacha's discovery on 18 October ("You don't look at another person's
insurance").

**The subway raising the parcel's value: cut as plot.** Mae's reason to kill for thirteen days rests on
"Nobody else has read it for this". A visibly appreciating parcel makes everyone read it. It also turns
back into Option B, recorded as not chosen. And the *Speaker* already prints "There is no subway under
Steinway Street" (May 1929), so any dig must be squared with that line. It can return as street colour
only, if at all.

**The rooftop council: cut to dressing.** Access runs on a rota and a key book, and the key loans carry
the closed loop. Malcolm names the users: Sacha, Juno, and "lately" the photographer. A nightly forum with
Adela up there makes five weeks of silence about the clause hard to explain.

**The shop cylinder as "what they said behind her back": cut.** Omar says "Bell starts it... That's all it
does." It holds a bell, footsteps and drawers. I'd invented Adela leaving it running.

**The Berlin prescription: cut, by owner ruling.** The Berlin letter's stated matter is the
Paragraph 175 vote. Making it medicine turns a friendship into a supply line, and "it isn't a clue" can't
hold when players read dated books as evidence. The treatment protects her from "body or history" records.

### AL-03. Echoes are recordings
The packet's sensation is right for a world built on listening. **Proposed rule:** no object plays the past
when touched. A recording plays because a machine is playing it, and holds only what that machine could hear.
What exists today is enough: Juno's tapes, the wall tape, the roof microphone and the bell-started cylinder.
Personal objects stay silent and simply are hers. Bible §VIII.5.o holds: nothing of the dark hours is in any
record. **Open:** the treatment never says what the roof microphone held on the 19th, and it shouldn't start
now without your say.

### AL-04. The strike becomes the rent hold
My first draft dismissed the strike. The second pass found that the treatment already has "resolve to hold the
rent", and Mr. Ewald's Arcade tenants are printed paying into court. The packet's solidarity survives with no
blockade and no machinery. It costs a few lines for Nadia and a slip at the rent ledger, and it also suits the
owner's ruling on rent notices in the shop proposal. **Keep the machinery out.**

### AL-05. Heat dressing
The papers already print the heat on 20 and 23 June, and "sleeps on its roofs" on 20 July. **Proposed:**
bedding on fire escapes and a window left open, as dressing in June and July, with languages from the actual
cast. No council and no triage.

### AL-07. Knowing when you are heard
A radio jammer can't stop a flash bulb, which has no signal in it. In a world with a Listening Act, though,
papers and strangers might tap a line, and Radio Service might sell a way to tell. That passes the Rule of
Signal and gives that shop's proposal a stronger upgrade than a better aerial. It needs a test against how the
Listening Act is written before it goes further.

### AL-08. Holmes Airport
She goes to the opening with a plate, once, as a dated visit. **Limit:** the aviation cards in the treatment are
Nadia's, after Adela's death, so they don't support this. It's a small new idea, and it stays optional.

### How the blackmail works

These are the room's answers to the objections in section 2. All are **PROPOSED** for her read.

- **She says it herself, plainly and early.** **av_drawer** opens from the first date, so it can't work as a
  late reveal about her character. The player is Adela, and she knows what she did.
- **Only people with power, and they know.** Aldermen, a landlord, a man from the Board of Estimate. People
  bring her plates to develop, she keeps a print, and they know it. She isn't the bait in anyone's trap. "You
  don't squeeze people you borrow sugar from." Nobody across the road or in the arcade is in it, so "privacy
  never reads as guilt" stands, and Juno's "she never told a soul" still holds.
- **It adds no suspects in the house.** The four men gain a motive, but they have no access and no thread. The
  treatment's rule for residents stands.
- **It makes the plot better, not lighter.** "Being decent, she sent Mae Kessler a copy first" stays true. Mae
  knows about the drawer, from a landlord she knows. The player can infer how a dated copy looked to her. Her
  motive is still the lease. Adela knows she wanted nothing, and she can't tell anyone. The set files both facts,
  on her word, and doesn't weigh them (**m_not_a_demand**).
- **Not a confession.** Mae says she returns anything from that counter unopened. So the evidence chain, including
  what she did with the note, stands as written.

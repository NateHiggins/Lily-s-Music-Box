## 3. One Short

**The idea.** A radio sleep-cure hypnotist counts Queens down every night from ten. One night the broadcast cuts off
at "two" and he skips town, so thirty thousand people have been waiting for "one" ever since.

Nobody is controlling anyone, and there's no evil station. The man left without finishing.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Nov 1928 | **Real:** the 11 November frequency shuffle, in **game/data/historical_radio_reallocation.json**. **Fiction:** the shuffle leaves WLIC sharing a wavelength, and on Monday 19 November it starts Professor Tobias Lull's Slumber Hour, sponsored by Restwell mattresses. Astoria hasn't slept well since the Hum began. "I don't touch the Hum. I teach you to sleep on it." |
| Dec to Feb | It works. People sleep, wake refreshed, and tell strangers they've been meaning to look at a mattress. **Fiction:** the Jamaica Arena marathon, already printed, bans radios after ten |
| Sat 30 Mar 1929 | Restwell is broke and Lull has packed. He runs over, and at about half past ten the station sharing the wavelength takes its turn, cutting him off after "two". The Record gives it two lines |
| Apr to May | At about half past ten, most nights, listeners stop for two seconds, mid-word, then carry on, not knowing they did. They also start selling and counting things one short: eleven rolls to the dozen, one screw short in the paper bag |
| Jun to Aug | People out in the heat all stop together on other people's roofs and stoops. The Speaker runs "Has Anyone Seen the Professor?" Unlike WORS he is easy to find: Scranton, selling bedding, and he doesn't do Queens any more |
| Sep to Oct | The hold lengthens to about seven seconds by October. A kettle boils dry; a man misses his stop. Nobody is hurt badly |
| Thu 31 Oct | "One Night." Nadia organizes. Juno writes to Scranton to ask for Lull's permission and gets a mattress catalogue. Rhea reads the word instead. It goes out and nothing happens, because everyone was told to expect it |
| November | It only works in person, with someone actually attending to you saying "one". People come round one at a time, and most never do |

**Who it touches.**

- **Teresa.** She is at work most nights, but her Crosley Pup and headphones are by her bed (**game/data/domestic_radios.json**), and on her nights off it gave her the first real sleep in years. She would take a cure gladly.
- **Evelyn.** Her immaculate set and programme log make her a faithful listener. One short drives her out of her mind and she marks it in red. She is the one who refuses: "I sleep perfectly well at two, thank you."
- **Omar.** Notices first, by professional offense, at the screws in the bags.
- **Juno.** She asks first, for once, and gets nowhere. Take 18 stays unspoken.
- **Cal.** Has the last broadcast on tape and won't let a thing end.
- **Rhea.** Explains the technique as her trade, then spends months failing to say "one" unperformed.
- **Adela, alive.** Makes a joke of the bar stopping. "Two seconds of everyone's attention, and I never had to earn it."
- **Listeners and non-listeners are mixed through the house** and nobody is singled out. Which residents are which is not a list anybody can read, and it says nothing about anyone.

**How the player meets it.** A lit set may mean a listener. The night man doesn't stop, and neither do plenty of
others. Radio Service gets customers saying "It stops around half past ten", and nothing it sells helps. Late in the
year a resident who trusts the night man may ask, and the player can say one word: "One." It unlocks nothing, counts
for nothing and is never announced.

**Rules that keep it safe.**

- Bodies stop, tape doesn't. **No record shows the hold,** so it isn't a time signal and doesn't rhyme with the dark hours or with Mina's four seconds.
- The hold is never exact. Nobody agrees on the minute, so it can't be set against the lobby clock or a watch.
- Listeners remember everything, so it can't make an alibi or void a witness.
- One short is **goods only.** It never touches stairs, ladders, doors, windows, buttons, keys, plates, drawers, letters, watches or clocks.
- It isn't the motif. Bible Law VII.8 holds that nothing fills the fifth position, so the count runs from ten and nothing in it is offered as four sounds and a gap.
- Lull is on WLIC, not WLBQ, so the alibi drama stays clear. A late dose on a ward is the Price wound, so no ward harm here.

**History to check before print.** The reallocation is in our data, and it shares only WNYC and WMCA, so WLIC's
sharing is fiction. From memory and not checked: Coué's autosuggestion craze as Lull's model, morning radio exercise
shows, and hypnotism-by-radio stunts.

## 4. Not at Home

**The idea.** They do abduct people, and they do want to socialize, and in person they are standoffish and rude and
have nothing to say.

You're mid-sentence, then you're at a party. It's dim and pleasant and goes on for hours. Somebody has put a drink in
your hand. They look at you, look past you and move to the other side of the room. You leave, you finish your
sentence, and no time has passed. You're holding a cup.

**How it sits with the brief.** The post-V2 brief says "They ignore humans; humanity has never got close enough to
communicate." Being handed a drink is closer than that, and **the brief yields here by Lily's ruling**: the distance
was manners. It stays a Queens joke. Only people at the parties learn it, nobody can prove it, and nothing here settles
whether anyone ever gets through. No word passes.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Nov to Dec 1928 | Fair nights only. A Woodside widow, Mrs. Albers, reports it to the precinct, and the sergeant asks whether there was a band. The Speaker files it under society notes: "Mrs. Albers was received last evening. Refreshments." |
| Jan to Mar 1929 | It becomes status. People compare cups, and a Forest Hills hostess is publicly not asked. Adela, alive, is annoyed she hasn't been invited and wants to know about the light |
| Apr to Jun | People wait in their yards in good clothes. Nadia writes to invite them back and gets no reply |
| Aug to Sep | Mrs. Albers has now been received four times, and she won't discuss it. Her sister says she's got a good coat on by six every evening |
| Oct to Nov | After the crash, more people dress up and wait, having had a bad week. In November the night man is asked |

**The cast.** Evelyn corrects the seating, and nobody notices. Lena spends the party clearing up. Omar never saw a
host and has opinions on the riveting. The Guests agree about it until a concrete question separates them. Peter wants
to know who he should write to.

**The player.** Once, late in the year, she gets taken. For a few minutes nobody calls her the night man, nobody gets
her wrong and nobody knows her. They snub her too. She can stay or put the cup down. Afterwards the companion prints
only what it can witness: "You are holding a cup I have not seen." Nothing about the party reaches the paper.

**Rules.** Zero time passes. About twelve people are named. No one is taken in storms. **Cups only:** no swapped coats,
because a grey coat, Adela's coat and a sleeve all matter to the case. No message, favor or face: they are a room and
some backs. The Hum isn't mentioned there. The trip touches the Tenant's construct, so it says nothing about what he is.

## 5. The Emergence

**The event.** On Saturday 12 October 1929 Cthulhu emerges from the sea out past Sandy Hook and flies off toward
Betelgeuse. It is reported like the weather, once in an untold eon. People look forward to it, and it's a party.

**Why that date.**

- **Real, already printed:** Columbus Day, with the Exchange shut.
- The crash is twelve days out, and nobody says so.
- Betelgeuse rises about 10 p.m. EST (rough calculation, checked twice), so a midnight departure can head for it. It rises too late in summer.
- The papers have Yom Kippur beginning at sundown on Sunday the 13th, so Saturday night is clear.

**A risk left open.** The Hum's sheets turn "louder" from 6 October, and the 12 October Record leads on them. A reader may
connect the two. They keep rising through 9 November, after it has gone, and Emergence copy stays away from Hum items, but
the risk remains and the printed dates would have to move to remove it.

**Its home.** **game/data/weather/phenomena.json** already gives a phenomenon a date, a bearing and its own Record and
Speaker lines.

| When | Copy |
|---|---|
| Nov 1928 | A line in the Bureau's long-range almanac: "Emergence due 12 Oct. next; once only." |
| Winter | Tide tables. Evelyn's letter on the spelling. Speaker: "Old One to leave; Queens asks who gets his seat." |
| Spring | Beach hotels raise rates. Holmes Airport sells viewing flights |
| Summer | Smoked glass on sale. The Bureau asks people to stop trying to say it |
| Sep to Oct | The ears, in their usual form: "Fair, warm. Seas moderate, then not." Committees form, and the Pun Derby holds a special class |
| Sun 13 Oct | Bureau: "Emergence 40 minutes late. Departure as forecast. Seas fell a quarter inch." Speaker: "It did not look down. We have written." |

**The night.** Forecast for 11:20, actually around midnight, gone by about 1 a.m. It reaches the city about the size of
a thumb at arm's length, and it looks at no one. The Arcade has lanterns and a queue at the luncheonette, with smoked
glass at Photo Supplies. Lily and Juno play at Harukiya, badly and happily. On the roof Malcolm guards his pots, Omar
counts the load, and Mae brings a chair: "You may sit. The chair is mine." She isn't a clue here. A live relay says, "It
is, folks, rather large."

**The crossover.** At about half past ten, before it comes up, the listeners stop for their seven seconds, on the roof and
in the street, among plenty of people who don't. The crowd laughs for the first time without knowing what it is. It's
funny and then not.

**The player.** The roof is the night man's to open, so she props the door and the house goes up. That is all that's
asked. She can stand anywhere, and nobody remarks on it. She can buy an ordinary plate from shop stock and expose it, and
it comes out a smear, like her others (**av_s2**). It unlocks nothing.

**Choices made, and why.** Adela doesn't reserve a plate in spring, and nothing on the night recalls what she would have
done: that is a dead woman's plans used as pathos. The key book stays out, because it carries the chain. The tripod's
place stays unmarked, because it is plate 13's viewpoint, and Sacha's line stays with plate 13.

**The name.** "The Call of Cthulhu" ran in Weird Tales in February 1928, so as far as I know it has been US public domain
since 1 January 2024. Not verified in this pass and not legal advice; other countries differ. Keep the name out of the
game's title and marketing, and have that checked before release. **design/DREAM_TENTACLE_DIRECTION.md** says "No generic
Lovecraft tentacle", scoped to the dream tentacle's look, so this must look like nothing else in the game and stay
unrelated to the tentacle gang. Lovecraft's Cthulhu sleeps in the Pacific and Betelgeuse is Derleth's addition, so fans may
call it wrong. This is the owner's alternate universe.

**Accord 9.** "Ordinary decay outranks spectacle." Reporting it as weather and keeping it distant is the answer.

## 6. How they share a year

One Short is the slow spine, ordinary and sad. Not at Home is small and private. The Emergence is the one huge public
night. Being taken and the hold never explain each other, and the crossover above is the only meeting.

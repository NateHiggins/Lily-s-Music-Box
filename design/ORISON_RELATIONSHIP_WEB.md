# THE ORISON RELATIONSHIP WEB

*Who is bound to whom, and by what. Commissioned by the bible, §VI.5:
everyone is connected to everyone else — there is no such thing as
coincidence in this building. Soap-opera drama played utterly straight,
braided across decades and centuries of tenancy, because this is
purgatory and the building keeps everyone until their case closes.*

*Authored 2026-08-03. Per the standing ruling, every thread here is
TENTATIVE until its gameplay is worked through — threads are reviewed for
functionality one at a time, alongside the case work. The web tells the
cases where to pull; it does not build them.*

**The one law of the web:** a thread is only canon-worthy if it can
eventually change what a player does in a case — a new dialogue branch, a
withheld repair, a name that lands wrong. Decorative connections are
coincidences wearing makeup, and there are no coincidences.

when they moved in and you get a year; ask the building and you get a
layer. Both true: these are lease dates, and they are the dates each soul
stopped.

remember arriving, and the more the building remembers for them.

## II. THE KNOTS

Nine braids. The first is the building's center of gravity.

### 1. THE CONVICTION — the Price knot

One court case, decades ago: **the State v. Price**. A man convicted on a
flawed record. He died in custody. The building has quietly collected
*everyone the case touched*, and none of them know they are all here.

- **Mina Vale (2A)** was the court reporter. The four seconds her ears
  blanked — the four seconds that ended her career — were four seconds of
  *Price's testimony*. What he said in that gap might not have acquitted
  him. It might have. Nobody knows what belongs in that silence, and that
  is the motif's empty fifth slot wearing a human life.
- **Peter Wren (4A)** was the legal clerk on the appeal. He found the
  transcript gap. He requested a form to certify the irregularity, and
  then a form to certify the request, and the filing deadline passed
  while he was making certain. His overfilled wallet still carries the
  receipt stubs. *Every uncertainty demands another form* — because the
  one time he acted on certainty's schedule, a man stayed convicted.
- **Teresa Vale (1D)** was on shift at St. Adjutor's custody ward the
  night Price died. An alarm went off. It was not her patient — not her
  alarm. *Not every alarm is yours* is the lesson her case teaches her;
  the horror underneath is that she learned it that night, and has been
  answering every alarm since in compensation. Two Vales failed the same
  man without ever knowing the other existed in his story.
- **Cal Dwyer (5B)** was taping that week — WORS pulled the courthouse
  feed the way it pulled everything. His **Delayed Voice Reel** holds the
  only surviving audio of the testimony, *including Mina's four seconds*.
  The record that ended her life exists, intact, two floors above her
  head, in the collection of a man who preserves moments by preventing
  them from ending.
- **Noel Price (4C)** and **Jonah Price (6B)** are the convicted man's
  sons. Noel has preserved the family flat into a museum of the life
  before the trial — archival gloves, nothing touched, nothing used.
  Jonah has been writing the family's story for years and cannot finish
  it, because finishing means writing the verdict down in his own hand.
- **Leon Price** — the third brother, the one who ran the week of the
  verdict and never came back — is a *voice on the desk line*. He calls
  the building he cannot enter. The Case Network's callers and the
  walking cases are layers of one thing; Leon is the proof.

The knot resolves nothing by itself. It is nine cases' worth of dialogue
branches: Mina can be told the reel exists, or not; Cal can be asked to
play it, or to finally let a moment end; Peter's form-storm gains a
specific ghost; Jonah's last chapter is sitting in Cal's archive at 1610
kHz. **A case is weak if the obviously compassionate option solves it** —
and there is no compassionate option here that does not cost someone
their load-bearing silence.

### 2. THE ESTATE AND THE ARCHITECT — Mae and Nadia

**Nadia Quell (5A)** was the architect on the Orison's last major
renovation. She found the violations — structural, electrical, the
wiring that would later carry the infection — and was made to sign her
silence; the live-in manager's flat was the price of her name on the
certificate. She took the flat, and then she took revenge in the slowest
possible register: she became the building's manager and now **organizes
the tenants against her own employers, the Kessler Estate**. Every rent
strike memo is signed "Management."

**Mae Kessler (6C)** is the Estate — the last one who bears the name,
appraising other families' heirlooms in white gloves while her own
family's ledger sits unopened. She and Nadia take tea. Neither has ever
said the thing. Mae's *certainty is not memory*: she is certain the
Kesslers were good landlords, and she does not remember the silence her
family purchased from the woman pouring her tea.

The player works for Nadia — the welcome letter is hers. The player is
therefore, without knowing it, an instrument in a war between floors five
and six.

### 3. THE BELLS — Omar and Iris

Married in 1977, married now, two floors apart, and they have not shared
a room since the blackout year. **Omar (3B)** cannot declare anything
unrepairable — least of all this. He still fixes her sink, her stuck
window, her radiator, wordlessly, by arrangement of notes left with the
super (he is the super; the notes are to himself). **Iris (5C)** paints
for imagined audiences because her first real audience stopped looking —
or she stopped letting him look; both true, and neither will check which.
Her case rule (*creation need not perform*) and his (*some things are not
repairable*) are the two halves of one door key. Neither case should be
closeable without disturbing the other. That is the gameplay thesis of
the whole web in one marriage.

### 4. THE VALES — Teresa and Mina

Mother and daughter, 1D and 2A, one floor and one abyss apart. Teresa
worked nights Mina's whole childhood; Mina learned to write everything
down because the person she was saying it to was never awake at the same
time. The stenography, the captions, the annotations — all of it is a
child leaving notes for a mother on the opposite schedule. They both live
here now, and their hours still do not overlap, because the building
honors the shape of a wound faithfully. Neither knows the other's part in
the Price knot. A player who learns both can stand in the stairwell
between floors one and two — the half-landing — and hold the only
complete picture of the family in existence.

### 5. THE ORTIZ THREAD — Lena and Cam

Confirmed kin (bible §IV). **Lena (2B)** has been mending since 1911;
**Cam (4C)** is her family's newest edition, a courier who cannot stop
moving. He does not know why rest feels like collapse; the building
knows: he is the descendant of the woman on floor two, and in her layer
of the building, *stillness is what happened to the girls who could not
get out*. His case rule (*weight can be shared*) is a message from her
century to his. Lena mends Cam's courier bag strap without being asked —
her only unprompted repair, family being the one hem of her own she'll
touch.

### 6. THE RED PENCIL — Evelyn and Peter

**Evelyn Marsh (1A)** taught **Peter Wren (4A)** — never mind the
arithmetic of years; the building keeps everyone's schoolroom within
walking distance. He was the careful one, the boy whose margins she
filled with red until careful became paralyzed. *Care became correction*;
correction became a man who cannot file a form without certifying his
certainty (§II.1). Evelyn's rule — *good enough can hold* — is the exact
sentence Peter needed forty years ago. Her case and his are teacher and
homework, still ungraded. In Peter's overfilled wallet, among the
receipts: a folded composition-paper note, red ink, "See me."

### 7. TAKE 18 — Juno and Rhea

**Rhea Sato (3D)** recorded eighteen takes and deleted the eighteenth —
the flawed one, the one where her voice broke honestly. Her mistakes
accumulate as a captive note *because the deletion did not take*:
**Juno Kells (2C)** had a mic in the wall that session, sampling without
asking, and take 18 lives in her **stolen session archive**. Juno's own
work was later taken from her without credit — the building's tidy
symmetry: she is on both ends of the same theft, one floor apart from
her victim. Rhea's *imperfection can be voluntary* is only reachable if
take 18 surfaces; Juno's *connection requires an open channel* is only
honest if she opens the archive. Two cases, one tape.

### 8. THE CUTTING — Malcolm and Sacha

The goodbye Malcolm could not finish in 1918 was to a man named **Auggie
Reed** — Malcolm took Auggie's surname afterward, the only marriage
certificate 1918 would issue him. **Sacha Reed (6A)** is Auggie's
family's line, four generations on, arrived in the building with a
camera and no idea. Sacha documents everything because their family
never had proof of its most important member's most important love;
the missing evidence is a plant on floor three. Malcolm knows exactly
who Sacha is. Sacha's *experience can precede proof* and Malcolm's
*compost is transformation* meet in one pot of soil: the cutting could
be propagated, divided, given — or composted at last. Sacha also
photographs **Iris's** paintings (the only documentation she permits),
threading the Bells to the Reeds.

### 9. THE DESK'S LINEAGE — Mara Chen and 4B

**Mara Chen** has a face in the art bible, a voice on the line, and no
body in the building — because she is the *previous* night-maintenance
tenant of 4B. Her case never closed; what the building could not keep in
a room it kept on the phone. Every desk caller is somewhere on this
web's rim (Leon Price already; others as cases are built), and Mara is
the innermost ring: the player's predecessor, still on hold. The
player's ruled arc — *the best way to honor a beginning is with the
release of an ending* — has a first ending on the shelf: hers.
*(New canon proposed by this document; flag for review with Case
Network work.)*

## IV. WHAT THE WEB SETTLES (proposals for the bible's disputed texts)

- **§VI.4 — Cal's `hearing_loss_named` flag** is not an error and not
  Cal's loss: it is *Mina's*, echoing in his collection. The flag marks
  the reel. Proposed: keep the flag, repoint its meaning at the Delayed
  Voice Reel, and let Cal's case surface it only after Case 01 names
  Mina's four seconds.
- **§VI.6 — Jonah's spine** is *can't finish*. The beginnings his art
  celebrates are the avoidance mechanism, not the wound: a man who keeps
  starting books never has to write a verdict. His case machinery
  (rewarding endings) is correct as built.
- **§VI.8 — Juno's theft** is confirmed both directions, now concretely:
  she sampled Rhea (take 18) without asking; the world took her own work
  without asking. The case is about the second; the gameplay teeth are
  in the first.

## V. RULES FOR USING THE WEB

1. Threads surface **diegetically or not at all** (bible law 7): a reel
   label, a mended strap, red ink in a wallet — never a lore dump.
2. No thread may resolve the both-true ambiguity. The Conviction is a
   documented court case *and* the building arranging its collection;
   every scene must survive both readings.
3. One knot per case build. When a case comes up for its functionality
   review, pull its threads from §III, build only what that case can
   dramatize, and mark anything cut as still-latent.
4. The empty slot stays empty (§VII.8). Nobody — not Cal's reel, not
   the player, not this document — ever renders the four seconds
   audible. What Price said is the fifth position of the motif.

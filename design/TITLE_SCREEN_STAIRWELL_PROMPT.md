## Production prompt

Create a museum-grade, photorealistic 16:9 interior architectural photograph
of the Orison at 3:00 a.m., looking straight upward from floor level through
its seven-storey open light-court stairwell. Treat the supplied Orison images
as identity references, not edit targets. Preserve its dog-leg proportions,
slender pale brass balusters, dark hand-polished oak rails, chunky turned
newels, cream plaster, exposed red brick scars, pale marble and terrazzo landing
edges, globe fixtures, and century-old material character while raising their
realism radically.

Make both dates true: the finely made but exhausted stair, masonry, railwork
and listening-house fabric date to 1912; plainer, cheaper apartment partitions
and repairs were inserted during the hurried 1928 reopening. Roughly forty
percent of the stairwell plaster has failed or been stripped to brick, following
real wear rather than random grunge: rising damp and salts low down, impact loss
at corners, hand grime near rails, water migration below the skylight, lath at
torn edges, contractor test patches and half-removed wallpaper.

The camera almost touches a worn oxblood runner whose pile is crushed into two
walking lanes. It looks vertically upward through repeating flights around a
narrow rectangular void. The geometry remains plausible, but one extra landing
rhythm may be barely implied in darkness so the viewer cannot count confidently.

Seven opal milk-glass pendants descend on long tarnished-brass drops. The two
lowest burn warm and close at roughly 2400K. Above them is a substantial dead
gap. One remote lamp burns abnormally hot and steady. The highest fitting,
directly beneath the skylight, is a DARK EMPTY brass socket and gallery. Small
warm practical pools fall off quickly; there is no fill. Color shifts vertically
from dirty amber and oxblood near the lens, through desaturated blue-grey, to
cold colorless pre-dawn at the skylight. Shadows retain blue-grey information
and never crush to black. Dust, plaster particulate and damp haze appear only
where practical light catches them.

Include terrazzo landings with dark aggregate, marble wainscot repaired with
mismatched pieces, oxidized brass, polished oak, a rain-wet wire-glass skylight,
and weak runoff tracks. One middle landing has an open accordion elevator gate
revealing a completely empty black car of uncertain depth. A folded canvas drop
cloth and wooden plastering float lie abandoned on a high landing, small enough
to discover rather than announce.

Keep the building empty: no people, bodies, silhouettes, faces or creatures.
On one plaster return, include only a barely readable darker hand-shaped area
at shoulder height, ambiguous between old touch grime and a fresh damp print.
A long brass pull chain in the upper dark hangs two or three degrees against
gravity. Nothing overtly supernatural.

Use strict corrected one-point vertical perspective with no fisheye. Center the
shaft slightly left, leaving the rightmost thirty percent quieter and darker for
menu UI. Keep the wire-glass skylight small in the upper third and preserve
unlit space around the dead socket. Use nested rectangles and rail diagonals to
pull the eye upward; interrupt the rhythm once with the elevator gate.

Large-format architectural photography, 24 mm tilt-shift, f/8, long exposure,
Kodak Portra pushed one stop, restrained fine grain, subtle halation only on
burning tungsten globes, realistic highlight rolloff and no digital sharpening
halos. Palette: tarnished brass, oxblood, tobacco oak, nicotine plaster cream,
old red brick, dirty marble and cold slate blue.

No text, letters, numbers, signs, logos, watermark, people, modern objects,
modern fixtures, fluorescent or LED colors, moon, daylight beam, cheerful
cleanliness, random horror props, floating objects, Escher geometry, fisheye,
Dutch angle, lens flare, HDR, oversaturation, crushed blacks, excessive bloom,
hotel luxury or generic abandoned-asylum styling. This is a functioning,
inhabited Queens apartment building whose reality is only beginning to fail.

## Final correction pass

Preserve the approved architecture, camera, crop, materials, elevator gate,
damage, color science, grain and perspective. Keep only the two lowest large
globes warm, plus one faint hot remote globe. Replace the highest glowing globe
directly beneath the skylight with a dark empty brass socket/gallery on its
existing drop. Reduce illumination and contrast across the rightmost thirty
percent into quiet desaturated blue-black and umber negative space, retaining
subtle plaster texture and the ambiguous hand trace so pale menu text remains
legible. Change nothing else and add nothing.

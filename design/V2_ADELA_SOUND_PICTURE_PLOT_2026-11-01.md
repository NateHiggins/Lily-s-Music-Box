## 1. The plot in one page

**All of this is invented fiction unless section 6 says otherwise.** No real company, person or Queens
booking did any of it.

Adela and Lily have an act at Harukiya: a comic opener, a number that gets her off the bench and ends with
a hat on a cymbal stand, and a quiet song whose last eight bars she can't leave alone. In February she sends
a photograph to a small concern in Astoria, the Ravenswood Sound Shorts Company, as "only an inquiry". On Monday
18 March it inquires back, offering a test on the premises. She wants it badly.

She comes to tell Lily before opening, delighted, and in the same breath plays two new bars that take the pulse
out from under Lily's part. It turns out she has also told them the act can arrange an evening. "You gave them
me." She apologizes plainly and asks the question she should have started with. Lily says yes, and asks what
happens to the recording. "Together," Adela says, and puts down her pencil.

The answer to Lily's question runs to four pages. The terms fix the arrangement, let the concern choose the
still, and in clause six say it retains the negative.

That clause is her own sentence. "She keeps the negative" is one of the first things the treatment says about
her. Sound on film makes it literal: they would put her voice through a slit of light and develop it in the soup
she buys by the gallon. She sends the contract back in grease pencil, repeatedly, and is right about every clause,
while Lily watches being right eat the spring.

Three things press on the contradiction, each closer than the last.

1. **Rhea, 20 April (B06, unchanged).** In the same weeks she demands her own portrait, she takes Rhea's out of
   the window and keeps the plates.
2. **The May take.** On Saturday 18 May the last eight bars finally work, because the whole room comes in
   early, clean over her two bars, and she has to go with them. She hears only that it was the best they've
   been. Juno's pocket recorder has it: the perfect answer to Astoria, made by friends, with no clause six. Rhea's
   voice is on it, breaking on a top note and running under the whole of the last eight, and she wants it wiped.
   Adela stands over the reel while Juno cuts it and carries it to the ashcan. She gives up the tape **and keeps
   the plates**. The dispute of S06 isn't solved.
3. **The elbow.** In June her publicity portrait, the one she has written four pages about, frames Lily down
   to an elbow. "You said together, in March." It's the same fault as "You gave them me", three months after she
   apologized for it. She apologizes again with no joke and lets Lily pick the print.

The offer is never resolved. No test happens, nothing is signed, and there is no date to miss. With the two bars
out of the slow song it goes where it goes, two Saturdays out of three. She wants supper.

After July her friends go on. The concern, which doesn't read the Record, writes "Kindly advise." Lily answers in
one line and gets the picture back with a stranger's pencil on it. Lily's Thursdays go on, and one thread of hers
has nothing to do with Adela.

In August Juno confesses she dubbed the May take the day before she cut it. It is the only reel of Adela singing.
The player, riding the night man, can hear herself from the floor for the first time, a little flat in the second
chorus, while the room runs over the two bars she was so proud of. **She has wanted approval of a take since
March. She finally has the only seat from which to give it, and she can't give it.** The one thing the night man
can do is ask what Adela was asked: did you ask Miss Sato? Juno asks. Rhea won't listen to it and asks for it cut.
By 31 August the reel is gone, whichever date the player reaches first.

## 4. The two bars

Owner approved, 2026-11-01. The note was my addition and the bars come from "The extra two bars".

The two bars she adds on 18 March are the fault. The May take shows it by accident: the room runs straight over
them and the song works. Then the take is cut, so the proof is gone from her life, and she keeps them.

After July, Lily tells the night man the slow song got better at the end of June, when he stood by the coat hooks
and told Adela which bar. If the player hasn't done that yet, she now knows she can. She goes back to June and says
it, and the living Adela, who has spent the winter telling men to name a bar, is asked to take a correction from a
man with a wrench, who is herself. It works. Lily is rightly annoyed: she said so in March.

**How it stands beside snap-back.** It changes nothing and teaches nothing about changing things. It was always in
the year, as the key loans are. If the player never goes, the night man still stood at those coat hooks, and Lily
saw him. Residents see the night man's acts all year; what the treatment reserves in section 8 is the player's
own memory of them, and she gets no shared memory here. It prevents nothing, so there's nothing to snap back.

**What this settles that the scene left open.** The proposal's note says the final pass doesn't prove the old
version must win, and in March it doesn't. By June her own ear decides, from the floor. Lily's position is shown to
be earlier, not the only musical one.

**The objection on file.** The unprimed reader wanted this cut: a second loop may take surprise from S15's roof-key
turn, and "she fixed it" sits close to the snap-back rule. Lily approved it knowing the first risk. If it steals from
S15 in play, cut **av_two_bars** and **li_two_bars** and nothing else breaks.

## 5. Owner rulings and remaining production choices

**Ruled, 2026-11-01: the dub exists.** Juno's second reel stays in the plot, and the player can hear herself sing. That earlier ruling is preserved.

Lily explicitly submitted all five writing answers in **owner-questions-20261101-e66188a0-v1** on November 1. These decisions do not approve production performances, every first-draft thread or the larger conspiracy.

1. **Rhea's answer — Q01.** Honour her request and cut the May recording copy as drafted. This concerns that copy only, not Take 18 or the unopened archive. The draft's request is honoured rather than turned into a player reward or a clue.
2. **Lily's lines — Q02.** The writers' room should draft options for Lily's review. Existing **LILY** lines remain placeholders until that review; no biography, surname or day job is invented by this ruling.
3. **Release slip — Q03.** No additional **RELEASED: THE TAKE.** slip yet. Conspiracy Option A remains proposed; no lexicon entry or gameplay reader is approved by this decision.
4. **The set — Q04.** Keep the proposed reference opener **Ain't We Got Fun** plus two original numbers. Lily alternates bass/drums between songs; the current bass/drums/bass outline fits that direction. The original song **It Ought to Pick Up** remains a working title, and the quiet original remains unnamed. This is repertoire direction, not production recording, composition/arrangement clearance, a selected vocal range or an accepted score.
5. **The concern's name — Q05.** Keep **Ravenswood Sound Shorts Company** as the fictional concern's name. It remains separate from the murder case. No historical company, contract terms, fee, recording technology or test date is established by this name ruling.

Songwriting, performer/capture decisions, in-world listening and actual stage integration remain open. The existing murder canon and prior approved two-bar story outcome are not reopened.

**Choices I made and you can reverse.**

- **No famous quiet song, for now.** The brief offers "My Blue Heaven". In a game about a dead woman in a purgatory
  it's close to the omen test below, and the scene already treats the slow song as an unnamed original. It can stay a
  reference for mood.
- **"Some of These Days" and "After You've Gone" stay out of her set.** The brief warns against omens, and as titles
  they read as prophecy to anyone who knows she dies. "Bill" stays out because it gives her a lover she doesn't have.
- **In "The extra two bars" I changed "ending" to "last eight" or "those bars",** moved it from late 1928 to March 1929,
  and changed "six since Sunday" to "since Saturday" to fit a Monday. Otherwise the words stand, with its stage
  directions in brackets because the panel has one line per speaker.
- **The brief's "The ending isn't." exchange isn't used.** Beside a player who has read her own notice, it foreshadows.
- **No test date.** A date just after 19 July is the one-week-from-retirement trope, and an acceptance arriving after
  her death would be the same trope. Ravenswood's reply is a form that restates clause six.
- **Her death doesn't make the act.** No memorial release, posthumous success or eternal wake.

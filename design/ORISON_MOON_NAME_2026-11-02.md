## 2. The story

The comic script is private, outside this repo. In the story it's one copy, kept in the piano bench and never sold, because it names her and confesses. The plot is this.

By the end of August Lily has one ugly print, a bench full of grease pencil, and a two-line notice in the Record. The last reel of Adela singing was cut, rightly, at Rhea's asking. Adela wanted her name on a bill.

On Thursday 12 September, in the middle of a sentence, Lily is received. It's the party everyone else has been to. She alone looks at the light, because Adela always asked what it was like and never got invited. She comes back holding a cup with a spoonful of that light in it.

**The alien technology is the cup,** the one thing the parties ever leave anyone with. **A cup only fills in the hands of someone who has looked at the light,** so everyone else's sat harmless all year, and the house's cups had no part in anything before September. What's in it pours away from whoever holds it, and it **cuts**: it takes a little off whatever it lands on. Her own cup, through her hand, leaves that hand sunk into a neighbour's bricks, invisible at noon, plain at five o'clock. That spends it.

For the Moon she wants eleven. She goes door to door and asks everybody who's been asked: Mrs. Albers, a coal dealer, Evelyn, Lena, Omar, Peter, the woman who couldn't tell a Graflex from a breadbox, who Adela was so rude about. Rhea has no cup and is asked anyway: "Did she say you could?" "No." "Then I'd like that noted." Juno asks whether she asked. "Who?"

She takes the name from a carbon in the bench, where Adela struck Ravenswood's clause six and wrote her name across the place. Nothing was ever signed. She traces it onto glass, going over the E eleven times until there's more of Lily in it than of Adela, and pins the plate into the hat, which has four pins and comes off with one hand. **Eleven cups and a hat.**

On Saturday 21 September she tells the bar she has a sick aunt, goes up the Ravenswood Sound Shorts Company's stage wall in the silk, sets the cups in a ring on its premiere searchlight, and times the exposure the only way she knows, on a snare: the last eight bars of the slow song. Then **she puts Adela's two bars back,** the ones Adela finally took out in June. In the ninth bar the left pin lets go. So the first letter slides downhill.

**Nothing happens.** It's a nearly full Moon, flat lit, with not a mark on it. Observatories find it that night and nobody else can. She carries the cups home.

On Thursday 26 September the Moon comes up lopsided, the sun rakes across it, and any fool on a roof can read **AD**. It's a cut, not a stain. You want the light from the side, as any photographer could have told her. "Nobody ever looks at the light."

She could go back and straighten it, because she has the tracing. But Adela once hired a man who hung her pictures crooked on the left, and kept him on, because it annoys the right sort of person. Beside the empty stool: "Nobody asked her. That's noted." **"I keep the negative."**

## 3. What's in the sky

**A cut in the terrain, not a mark.** It's cut into the Moon's real LOLA heights in **game/shaders/skywatch_moon.gdshaderinc**, so it lights and shadows by the same code as every crater. Its floor is also darker rock: more so just past the shadow line, where its walls would shade it whole, and otherwise only under magnification. No generated texture is involved, so RUL-008 isn't touched. It appears over forty seconds from 22:40 on 21 September 1929, and it's a pure function of the date, so travelling back before that shows a clean Moon.

| Phase | With the eye, at a 14 degree view | In a telescope | Dates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near full | Nothing | **ADELA**, darker rock in the ground | 18 Oct |
| Near last quarter | A letter or two by the shadow line: **AD** | The lit part of the name, hard shadows at the line: **AD** | 26 Sep, 25 Oct |
| New | Nothing, there's no Moon to see | Nothing | 2 Oct, 1 Nov |
| Near first quarter | A letter or two by the shadow line: **EL** | **ELA**, the lit part | 10 Oct, 9 Nov |

**Nobody reads the whole name with the naked eye, and nobody gets four letters and a gap.** An earlier draft let the dark side carry a ghost of the name in earthshine. It looked fine, and it was four marks and a missing fifth, which Law VII.8 forbids. So the eye now gets only what's near the shadow line. **By eye it's now only a few dark notches along the line, fainter than the Speaker makes out. Lily should look at the renders and say if she wants it stronger.**

**The game has no installed telescope yet:** the telescope column is proved only on the optical bench, at a 2 degree field. At a normal 60 degree view, rendered and looked at, the Moon is about fifteen pixels wide at 1280 x 720 and no letters can be read at any phase. **So until there's a telescope or a zoom, the player can't read it in the game:** she can only be told, and see the papers.

**On the last evening, 9 November, the first-quarter Moon carries EL by the shadow line.** See section 5.

## 4. What it does to the year

**Everyone on Earth can see it, and nobody who knows will say who she is.** No paper or court connects the name to Miss Voss of Long Island City. The borough has two hundred and twelve Adelas. Everybody knows an Adela. The dozen people who gave cups, and a two-line notice, could put it together, and they keep it to themselves as this house keeps everything: the fifty dollars goes unclaimed. Ravenswood writes to the bar about "your client's billing" and has no idea what it has noticed. **The player knows.** She spends the last seven weeks of the year under her own name and can't correct it.

**The papers.** Seven back pages, **1929-09-22** both papers to **1929-11-03**:

- The observatories first, and nobody can see it. The Speaker goes up on the roof and calls it a stunt.
- **AD** at the end of September. "It's the most anybody has ever paid for two letters." The Speaker's fifty-dollar reward fills Borden Avenue with four hundred and six women named Adela, who sing.
- The Adela hat, low on the left. Almanacs, the tax commission, the clergy.
- On 12 October the thing from the sea comes up with the Moon low in the west, "and it went the other way and never looked".
- The Crash week: "the only figure in the city" with no change, and clerks on the roofs looking up.
- A paid notice, the two hundred and twelfth of its kind: "To Adela: I am sorry about everything." It's signed by nobody and is not about anything.
- The reward stands. Nobody comes forward.

**Ravenswood.** "We note your client's billing. Under clause six, the Company retains the negative. Suggest smaller letters. Kindly advise."

## 6. What's built and what isn't

**Built, first draft.** The cut, the date driver and a review bench; seven back pages; eleven threads and one companion line.

**Not built, and the first thing it needs.** An installed telescope, without which the player can't read her own name. **Also not built:** the cups as props, Lily's trip, the Ravenswood stage, crowds on roofs, and any change to the endings. The comic is a private script with nothing rendered. Lily's threads still open only through the F9 playtest panel.

**A defect this found, and left alone.** The Moon's map is mirrored in the sky: Mare Crisium shows on the left, and it should be on the right. **The name is corrected for it;** fixing the map would mean undoing that one line.

# Please Stay on the Line on screen

Evidence class: **INERT**. Production plan and source audit, 26 November 2026.

The first test is a 45-second unbroken shot. A tone comes from an empty radio workbench. Vantry banks to listen. The camera follows his attention from the bench to his cabinet, comes to rest and waits for his answer. Can a viewer tell who heard whom with the sound on, then locate the same change of attention with the sound off? This is a lighting and performance study. It establishes no new case fact.

The audit uses main at **e1ee622e9**. “Observed” below identifies a source behavior, a measured result or a specific review image. A visible defect that still needs a moving shot is marked as a risk. Earlier qualifying receipts establish their named game behaviors. The first drop has its own capture and review status.

**Scene structure.** The player’s **CharacterBody3D → Camera3D** remains the responsive gameplay view. The film fixture has a sibling **Node3D → Camera3D** with its own **CameraAttributesPhysical**, an optional **Path3D** under the director, a local **WorldEnvironment** on an isolated stage, a shot's actor and cue emitters, then the render manager. Hide only explicitly registered HUD/control nodes. Leave Vantry's physical date and paper visible, and recover prior visibility and camera ownership on exit. Roll or handheld drift belongs to the shot.

Physical shots use focal length in millimetres, focus distance in metres and f-number. Focus is distance along the optical axis to the chosen eye, hand or grille. The physical resource overrides the camera's field of view and clip planes. Calibrate exposure with the project's physical-light-unit settings before calling a particular f-number a photographic match. Use 28 mm for the intimate workbench wide and 50–85 mm for a reaction where the set permits. Reserve the Bible's 18–24 mm and 135–200 mm choices for architectural perspective and compression. A custom oval-bokeh pass and multi-sample shutter remain later optical tests. ## Production budgets

**Gameplay.** The brief's 60 fps target is 16.67 ms per frame. Test the game with native input and the existing practicals on target hardware in a release build when that work resumes. For each named route report resolution, render backend, median, 95th percentile and worst frame plus input/control behavior. The 1440p measurements above establish the shortfall on an earlier main. They do not establish current performance or authorize a new optimization campaign.

**Film.** Start with a small SDR take at 24 fps before requesting a 3840 × 2160 render. At 24 fps, 45 seconds is exactly 1,080 frames. At 60 fps it is 2,700. Prove 4K cost on a short crop of the hardest view and extrapolate from measured seconds and bytes per rendered frame. Uncompressed 8-bit RGB at 4K is about 24.9 MB per frame, or 26.9 GB for the 45-second take at 24 fps, before compression and audio. Ten minutes at the same rate is 14,400 frames. Place takes in a new report on the approved report volume. Deliver 24 fps for the normal film. Request 60 fps for a specific motion or slow-down need.

Use Forward+ Vulkan for the film. Measure the existing shadow atlas before raising a limit. Test either SDFGI for a changing set or a baked VoxelGI volume for a fixed set on a controlled comparison. Try bounded volumetric fog, a real occluder and a damp roughness patch under a motivated practical. Start at the Bible's 16:1 lighting ratio and record measured linear light contributions on a test patch. The brief's 8:1 is a useful local readability trial where a face or important printed detail falls away. Lock the camera for the majority of the take. Preserve a broad enough pool of light for the player to locate switches and exits. Forward+ is rasterized. A convincing shaft of light is an image result, separate from claims about path tracing.

MovieWriter output is chosen at engine launch. Use the project's FIFO on the hidden desktop with an external output location and a fresh profile. Archive a lossless frame sequence and PCM reference as needed for approved shots. The default delivery is SDR Rec.709 after explicit conversion from the actual capture colour space and a decoded levels check. HDR requires a verified high-dynamic-range source, a 10- or 12-bit Rec.2020 PQ/HLG grade, matching tags and an HDR display check. Ordinary 8-bit display-referred PNG cannot recover clipped highlights at encode. ## Four milestones

1. **Lighting, lens and a continuous shot.** Begin with the two first-drop scripts, an isolated workbench and Vantry. At 24 fps, frames 0–239 establish the dark bench and practical, 240–527 let a tone draw him into frame, 528–839 let the dolly follow his bank and shift focus to his cabinet, 840–1079 hold through his answer and the room tail. Play the whole 45 seconds without a cut. Inspect the brightest practical, darkest silhouette, sharpest printed detail, HUD exclusion, horizon stability and final stillness. Reopen gameplay and verify ordinary look/control. A technical bench can pass before Lily accepts the shot.
2. **People and turn-taking.** After the look is readable, stage one resident opposite Vantry. Give one hand a reason to stay occupied. Use an approved recording, timed mouth cues and an eye line that crosses to an object before the reply. Compare a 350 ms beat with a hold up to 1.2 seconds in the edit. Gameplay choices still respond immediately. Check hands/feet and eye lines at normal speed and in a close view. Run a nonzero pickup and check the pose/cue at the same frame. For artistic review, ask a viewer to describe the listener's change of mind without a subtitle.
3. **Reset grammar, wrongness and sound.** Use the tea visit and an earned attachment in separate cuts. Bridge into the repeated tray move with a reversed record tail, then cut to the same starting pose with a short shutter interruption and a single filament bloom. Offer a reduced-light version. Introduce a localized colour or shadow discrepancy before any frame-wide distortion. Set physical evidence in focus long enough to read. Verify the film readout against the saved visit/release state, preserve one dream per night, solo each stem and measure tail timing around both cuts. For artistic review, ask a fresh viewer to distinguish what returned, what persisted and why the release print followed sleep.
4. **Episode assembly.** Once the performance and reset cues pass, lock the authored timeline, accepted takes, first state, engine and sampler seeds. Capture the hardest short section twice and once from a later pickup. Compare decoded frame indices, cue onsets, visual focus and pose, and record any temporal-history deviation. Freeze each part, preserve finished sections on retry, assemble a roughly ten-minute pilot and decode the complete stereo/SDR export. Check first and last picture, every cut with sound, audience-readable clue inserts and subtitle timings. Screen with Lily before publication. The pilot, 4K qualification, HDR grade and artistic acceptance are later deliverables.

## Capture the first shot

The project command runs the patched engine through the shared hidden lane, uses a separate profile, and writes lossless PNG frames, a stereo PCM master, three synchronized WAV stems and an SDR viewing copy. Supply a new external report directory. A short take compresses the blocking to exercise the turn, focus and reply. For the full 45-second take, omit the frame override.

```powershell
python tools/render_first_light.py D:/ov_reports/first_light_short --size 640x360 --frames 72
python tools/render_first_light.py D:/ov_reports/first_light_full --size 1280x720
```

Select 24 or 60 with **--fps**. Select 3840x2160 with **--size**. Use **--finish** with the same dimensions, rate and count to verify and encode an already finished native take. Engine output, image sequence, sample counts and first/last frame hashes are recorded per take. The editorial revision and player build have separate owners.

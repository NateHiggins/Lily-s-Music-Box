# Adela at the piano: Erica inspiration and the Harukiya found family

Evidence class: **INERT**. Writers' room and performance-design packet, 2026-11-01.
Owner: Lily. New owner direction is distinguished below from proposed writing.
No new dialogue, character asset, stage, mocap, audio or runtime qualification is claimed.

## The assignment

Give us a woman we would want another evening with, even in a game without a murder.
Adela Voss keeps Photo Supplies, plays piano, sings at Harukiya regularly, and
occasionally gets so carried away that she leaves the bench and takes the room
with her. Lily accompanies her, alternating drums and bass between songs. The
bar is their familiar room: work, friendship, artistic ambition, small arguments,
flirtation where wanted, and people making space for one another.

Erica is Lily's personal inspiration for this version of Adela. Keep Adela's
established name, age thirty-eight, profession and story. Develop an original
fictional person; do not present invented events or psychology as Erica's biography.
The player should encounter Adela enjoying a life, rather than rehearsing her death.

## Character proposal: generous company, imperfect custodian

**What she wants this year:** better portraits; enough money to stop treating
repairs as optional; a musical partnership that has become worth rehearsing;
and a room in which she can enjoy being looked at without negotiating her name.
Give her an actual small artistic ambition: a short set whose quiet final song
she keeps rearranging because she does not yet like the ending. It need not be
a clue, coded prophecy, or song about death.

**What people enjoy:** she notices the difference between being present and being
comfortable. She remembers a preferred seat, notices when a chorus is too high
for someone joining in, and can make room without announcing her generosity.
She is capable of wonderful silliness. Let the face stretch into a grin; let a
failed joke remain failed. Sometimes she wants a beer and no improving discussion.

**What makes her difficult:** she can confuse saving something with having the
right to keep it. This is already real in the Rhea/negative dispute and her
established drawer of compromising prints. Do not replace it with an adorable
perfectionist quirk. At rehearsal she can similarly keep changing an arrangement
after Lily thought it was agreed. Her friends can refuse, object and remain friends.
She must sometimes apologize plainly, without winning the apology with a joke.

**Contradiction worth playing:** she wants to choose how she is seen, yet has
sometimes denied another person the same choice about a photograph. Being a
trans woman makes this tension personal; it does not make her uniquely guilty
or automatically wise. Her consent failures do not cause or justify her murder.

**Offstage habits, proposed:** she pins hair back for the piano and forgets to
remove the pin; tests a stubborn note before taking her coat off; rehearses a
punchline aloud and discovers it was worse than the first version; gets fiercely
specific about an ugly publicity print while overlooking the ink on her own cuff.
Choose two or three, not an animation tick in every shot.

**Emotional range:** assurance, vanity, desire, irritation, exhaustion, practical
care, embarrassment, pleasure, mischief. She gets to be ambitious, ordinary,
occasionally unreasonable and loved without being turned into a lesson.

## Appearance proposal for the character artist

Use Erica's recognizable visual rhythm, not a flattened idealized beauty mask.
Keep long copper waves and the expressive mouth/eyes as the main anchors. Hair
may be pinned clear of the hands for work and loosened for a set; a cropped bob
is not compulsory merely because the game is set in 1928. Keep natural facial
asymmetry, ordinary tiredness and the broad smile. Preserve Adela's established
age; do not estimate Erica's age from these images.

| Context | Proposed original design | What the performance needs |
|---|---|---|
| Photo shop / rehearsal | Cream blouse, deep green or ink-blue practical skirt or trousers, worn dark jacket; a real pocket for pencil/pins | Unrestricted shoulder, elbow and wrist movement; sleeves that can be secured |
| Regular evening set | Dark teal or bottle-green evening dress with a loose drape and a little warm metallic detail; restrained original ornament | A skirt opening/volume tested for seated pedalling, standing, turning and steps; secure shoes rather than decorative instability |
| Deliberately ridiculous number | One handmade theatrical accessory she and the room have committed to far too seriously | An original removable prop, with attachment and swept-volume checks; no branded costume copied from the collage |
| Quiet close of the night | The same evening clothes with a work cardigan or borrowed jacket | Continuity and human scale; no obligatory makeover or reveal |

These are design options, not approved manufactured garments. No provisional
height, skin-tone shader value or facial measurement is to become a locked rig
specification. Build expression references for listening, distracted amusement,
mid-syllable singing, concentration at the keyboard, annoyance and open laughter,
not only a promotional smile. Keep the body credible at the piano and at rest.

Her singing voice should belong to the same person who talks, laughs, argues
and catches her breath. Choose a performer and comfortable range before fixing
keys. No pitch threshold for womanhood, forced breathiness, or voice-as-reveal.
No voice cloning or reconstruction of Erica is specified by this packet.

## Found family: show the work of belonging

Safety in this room is a deliberate fictional choice, not a claim that all of
1928 New York was welcoming. The existing story can name prejudice and keep
Mae's conduct wrong. Do not spend every visit proving that prejudice exists.
Inside the circle, use her name naturally. Compliments can concern a good turn,
a sharp jacket or an attractive woman; they need not be coded reassurance.

Friends bring incompatible needs. Support is asking before recording, keeping
a rehearsal appointment, saying the arrangement is not working, and helping
put chairs away. Nobody is appointed the endlessly patient ally. Adela offers
care too. The family does not exist solely to react to her being trans.

| Person | Proposed relationship in the bar | Keep the established voice / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Lily | A collaborator who knows the count-in, can disagree about a song, and has a life outside accompanying Adela | No assumed romance or ownership of Erica's real relationships. Drums on one number, bass on another; no simultaneous live double-duty |
| Lena | A practical clothing adjustment or repair, negotiated with its wearer; Adela gives something back | Useful advice, no martyrdom and no touching or altering clothes without asking |
| Teresa | Company after a shift, water left within reach, sometimes too tired for a set | Practical care, not a perpetual medical chaperone |
| Juno | An arrangement experiment and a real argument about recording permission | Fast, specific, mischievous; asking before sampling matters. No new authorized murder-night tape |
| Rhea | Can like the music and still insist the photograph comes out of the window | Her privacy objection remains valid; affection does not waive it |
| Omar | Helps with an actual loose fitting or bench problem, then expects decent workmanship | Economical language; not a sentimental speech about the healing power of music |
| Nadia | Makes plans that can include too much; learns to ask whether Adela wants another event | Keep Nadia's later revelation about the night man exclusive and late |

These are scene proposals, not new resident jobs, witnessed knowledge or schedule
entries. The existing cast voice map remains the tuning source. Not everybody
must be at every set or agree about the music. Preserve eighteen simultaneous
preferred resident places as a capacity goal when the room is redesigned.

## Let trans experience enter through particular lived moments

These are proposals for this woman, not a universal trans autobiography.
Belonging changes what she can spend her attention on. At the piano she can
worry about a difficult left-hand passage, whether Lily heard the cue, and who
she wants to see after the set. Let that ordinary freedom be observable before
any character explains it. Her womanhood does not depend on the audience liking
the song or approving her appearance.

Give her authorship over presentation. Lena asks where she wants the garment to
sit; Adela chooses, changes her mind and enjoys the result. The fitting can be
about texture, movement, desire and personal taste. It is not a demonstration
of how successfully clothes hide a body. Friends need not announce how accepting
they are while doing this ordinary work.

Keep the distinction between being known and being available for inspection.
She can be openly trans without supplying a medical history, an earlier name or
an explanatory speech to every curious person. The room can respect that limit
without turning her into its inspirational educator. She can also choose to talk
about her own experience, with frustration or pleasure, when she actually wants
to. Do not make her permanently above the subject in order to avoid stereotyping.

Let two friends disagree without making belonging conditional. She can be wrong
about a photograph, less gracious than she intended, or irritating at rehearsal,
and still expect to be invited back. Her friends' care is not a reward for being
a flawless representative. Nor is her murder punishment for confidence, visibility,
sexuality, transition or taking up space. Mae's established lease motive stays.

## Three visits and one ordinary disagreement

**Visit one: a good evening.** Any eligible pre-death date, to be selected against
the existing calendar. We find her already mid-task: adjusting the bench, asking
Lily whether to take the bridge, teasing a late friend without humiliating them.
We hear a real full musical phrase before a clue or conversation can interrupt.
The player may stay, move around, talk quietly elsewhere or leave. Do not require
applause, a dialogue choice or a pity response to unlock progression.

**Visit two: the arrangement will not behave.** Rehearsal, not another immaculate
show. She calls for one more run; Lily says the change has not been rehearsed.
Adela bristles, then listens. They try a simpler transition. Let shared competence
and a small unfinished argument carry intimacy. Neither needs to explain the
meaning of found family.

**Visit three: spectacle.** A captured set with piano, singing, rising from the
bench, dance and a return to the keys. Lily changes instrument between numbers.
Record the real human transitions: breath, pedal release, eye contact, a cue,
waiting until both hands are free. The set has comic excess and a quiet contrast;
it is not a continuous athletic trick. Adela enjoys being good at this.

**Ordinary disagreement: the recording.** Juno asks to keep a take that includes
someone who does not want it kept. Adela's first instinct is preservation. Rhea
objects. Adela eventually agrees, without a poetic defense that makes her right.
Preserve the treatment's actual negative dispute; this supplementary scene must
not quietly solve it in advance or furnish an unapproved clue.

After 19 July, do not spawn a living Adela at a contemporary set. Earlier visits
can still reach her. A later date can have another performance, an ordinary
argument, or a clearly identified recording with consent and provenance. Do not
make every later bar evening an eternal wake. Her friends continue to live.
Music cannot bypass the dark interval or give Lily forbidden knowledge of the
player's identity through a conveniently recognizable lick or gesture.

## Voice samples to attack, not protect

All lines below are proposed audition material, not implanted dialogue.

**Adela and Lily, rehearsal**

ADELA: Again from the bridge.
LILY: You changed it.
ADELA: I improved it.
LILY: You changed it.
ADELA: ...All right, let me show you.
[She reaches for Lily's chart, catches herself and asks for it instead.]

Keep the small negotiation and the imperfect pause. If it sounds like a finished
comedy sketch, let Lily interrupt earlier or Adela abandon the defense sooner.

**Lena, fitting a cuff**

LENA: Try it sitting down.
ADELA: It looks better standing.
LENA: Then stand at the piano.
[Adela sits. The cuff catches. Neither needs to explain the result.]

**A man offers to explain her own piano arrangement**

MAN: What you want there is a little more feeling.
ADELA: Which bar?
MAN: Well, in general.
ADELA: We're rehearsing this one.
[Back to work. No crowd delivering a verdict on him.]

**A quiet table**

FRIEND: Do you want company?
ADELA: Yes. Don't make me useful for a minute.
[The friend sits. Leave room for an ordinary subject after the pause.]

The jokes target condescension, bad process and real musical disagreement.
Let friends be funny without making Adela or women the communal punchline. Not
every reply gets a correction. Include failed jokes and scenes without jokes.

## Stage commission: Harukiya becomes a performance room

The centerpiece is a stage that feels used even with nobody performing. Aim for
an intimate thrust or shallow angled apron, visible from several seating zones,
with piano and drummer as part of the composition rather than hidden behind
a singer. A real approach to the bench, a visible side path and an unblocked
return matter more than a grand concert-hall scale. Compare upright and compact
grand piano proxies before selecting the silhouette; do not silently substitute
a grand that leaves no dancing room.

This authorizes planning the remodel, not treating an unmeasured floor plan as
built. The installed stage curtains, signal actors, bar instruments, resident
places, pool table, counter/canopy, stairs, door and services already have owners.
The August reconstruction proposal contains stale observations, old constraints
and retired arcade cabinets. Survey current source and rendered space before
using its numbers. Do not restore the retired cabinets. Lily's new performance
priority supersedes treating the old small stage as an immutable design, but it
does not authorize clipping another occupied room or silently deleting working
bar functions. Any real envelope conflict must be shown with a measured option.

First blocking layout deliverable: one current plan/section with the instrument,
performer and audience proxies. Derive stage size from the captured motion's
swept volume: seated arms/pedals, bench travel, standing, full dance phrase, return
to keys, drumstick reach and bass posture, plus service/player circulation. Show
safe pathways and sightlines at seated and standing eye heights. Preserve or
reassign every resident's preferred spot through its existing registry; check
capacity without asserting compulsory attendance. No metre dimensions are locked
from this paper brief.

Capture singing, face, breath, hands, feet and body against one audio master.
Choose instrument and key before final capture. Use original or properly cleared
repertoire; no song licence is established by a style reference. Author separate synchronized
piano keys/pedals, drums/sticks and bass/finger contact; body mocap alone does not
prove played notes or hand contact. Retarget to the actual rig with planted feet,
bench/hand anchors and recoverable transitions. Do not stretch a performer or
shrink an instrument to fix contact. Singing/dancing/playing form a sequence,
not an instruction to do physically incompatible gestures simultaneously.

Use existing audio, attendance, dialogue, campaign clock and save authorities.
The performance presenter owns synchronization and cancellation only. One bounded
set, a small repertoire and reusable captures precede any expansion. Stream audio,
reuse skins/materials, reduce distant audience animation, avoid a separate light
per instrument and measure the occupied bar under the same settings before/after.
No fixed performance budget or optimization victory is asserted before measurement.

Acceptance later requires actual player movement during the set; readable face
and silhouette; no camera takeover; correct instrument contacts; audience/access
clearance; interruptions, pause, date changes, leaving/re-entering and validated
reload; legal-date/knowledge/recording boundaries; loaded metric PBR/mipmaps; and
frame-time distributions with the full intended audience. Artistic acceptance
belongs to Lily's playtest. No Godot run is needed to validate this writing packet.

Use these as garment-construction references, not approved costumes or copied

The [Smithsonian's Gladys Bentley page](https://nmaahc.si.edu/gladys-bentley)
describes a pianist/singer and gender-nonconforming stage performer; it places
her Ubangi Club work in the early1930s. This is context for the breadth of queer
performance histories, not permission to transplant that venue into1928, equate
Bentley's identity with Adela's, or borrow a Black artist's biography as Erica's.

voice map alongside this packet. Preserve Adela's sharp edges and actual evidence

**Question:** the Orison shows the player dates from 1893 to 2008. Which of
those are construction, which are accumulated history, which are the purgatory,
which are system time, which are invented by a random number generator, and
which are actually wrong?

**Base:** `0bf8dc089e552f559602eb2fbfb03ab31010b7cd` (pushed `origin/main`,
"Seal generated UIDs before friends exports").

**Origin drifted three times during this audit.** The task began at `8d92a35`

`887f52d`, `022d123`, `3d4504a`, `d1f0fe6` acting on that census's findings, then
`a8fc44f` and `0bf8dc0` touching the release-gate ledger and two export scripts.

## 1. Executive verdict

**Production implements a two-stratum rendered-year model. The player reads
exactly two kinds of year, and both are canon.**

**Stratum one — the address, 1928.** Three literal sites, all at the front door:

```
game/scripts/props/building_entry_sign.gd:35   "QUEENS, N.Y.  ·  EST. 1928"
game/scripts/props/building_entry_sign.gd:83   "THE ORISON / BRONZE AND ENAMEL / EST. 1928"
game/scripts/props/landmark_entry_door.gd:77   "THE ORISON  ·  1928"
```

**Stratum two — the station's catalogue, 1962–2008.** The music player prints
each unlocked track's year into its list button:

```
game/scripts/audio/music_director.gd:371
    button.text = "%s
%s · %s" % [str(track.title), str(track.artist), str(track.year)]
```

**REPOSITORY FACT:** `OrisonMusicDirector` is constructed in production at
`building_root.gd:629`, and `music_director.gd:76–77` auto-appends
`FIRST_TRACK` to the library on first boot. That track is `am_maintenance`,
which renders as **"A.M. Maintenance / Work Order Zero · 1988"**. A player on
their first shift can read **1988** without unlocking anything.

**Both strata are licensed.** 1928 is §VIII.5.h. The catalogue's years are
§III, which dates WORS 1610 openly — *"between 1962 and 1999"* — so the station's
era is stated canon rather than a secret the purgatory keeps.

The system clock reaches the game as **phase, and as astronomy, but never as a
displayed date.** `DayNightDirector` takes time-of-day; `ScheduleDirector` takes
weekday and day-of-year and skips the year field; clocks show hours and minutes.
**One system does consume the real year:** `CelestialEphemeris.julian_day()`
reads `utc.year` (`celestial_ephemeris.gd:9`) to place the sun and moon — which
is exactly what `CELESTIAL_SKY_CONTRACT.md:3` rules it should do. The year is
computed with; it is never printed.

**INFERENCE — the model is sound, and its one real break is not a lore decision
at all.** 191 light-fixture cards carry installation dates
generated by `rng.randint(1928, 1989)`. They are not a conceit, not the
purgatory, and not accumulated history. They are an unconstrained random range
that has never been reconciled with the bible, and they are currently reachable
only through the debug overlay — which is the only reason they are a P1 and not
a P0.

**The strongest thing this audit found:** the repository already contains three
different correct ways to place a post-1928 date, and one incorrect one. The
correct patterns are worth naming, because they are the template for fixing the
incorrect one (§8).

## 2. Method

Whole-record reads and machine counts. Every census in this document parsed its
complete corpus; counts are stated with denominators.

```

           all 18 resident schedules, and every game/data/*.json for
           year-shaped tokens
grep -rn – InputMap, Time.* call sites, rendered-year string sweep
git ls-files game/assets/audio/music   – shipped-audio confirmation
```

**Regex hits were verified, not trusted.** A naive year sweep of `game/data`
flagged `acoustic_graph.json` (1800–2000), `viral_seed_features.json`
(1602–2099) and `building_layout.json` (1625–2077). Reading them in context
showed **Hz values and fragments of decimal numbers** — `0.2036` matching
`2036`, a ceiling height of `2.6199…` matching `1999`. **All three are false
positives and none is a date.** They are recorded here because discarding them
silently would leave the counts below unauditable.

## 3. The ruling that governs everything

`design/ORISON_BIBLE.md` §VIII.5.h, ruled 2026-08-09, is explicit and it
settles the central question:

> **IT IS 1928 WHEN THE GAME STARTS.** This supersedes the earlier pinning of
> the present to late 1927. Everywhere else in this document, and in the
> wardrobe bible, **"1927" is the VINTAGE of ordinary things, not the date on
> the calendar.**

**This is why "the installation date is not the building date" is already
canon.** §VIII.5.h continues: *"Both dates are true and neither replaces the
other"* — 1912 is the fabric, 1928 is the address — and *"The building's paper
trail begins in 1928 because that is when this building began."*

Three further rulings license post-1928 material, and they are **separate
licences with separate limits**:

| Licence | Source | What it permits | Its limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Rule of Signal** | §VIII.2 | Anything that *carries, captures, switches, stores or reproduces a signal* is 40–60 years early | §VIII.5.e caps signal witnesses at **technology through 1967**. §VIII.5.i states an electric motor *"is powered machinery, not a message, and the Rule of Signal gives it no licence beyond its period"* |
| **The purgatory** | §I, §VIII.5.g | Things arrive from decades that have not happened. *"A machine built in 1919 receiving a programme from 1987 is not a contradiction in a building where the year does not advance — it is Tuesday"* | *"they arrive **without a date on them**"* — and *"Do not explain this anywhere in the game"* |
| **Centuries of tenancy** | §I | *"The residents are tenants with leases and grudges, and they are souls who have been here — some of them — for centuries"*; the web runs *"across decades and centuries of tenancy"* | §VIII.5.h: *"Every resident in it moved in within the last twelve months"* — both are ruled true and neither wins |

**INFERENCE, and it is the load-bearing one in this audit:** the purgatory
licence is explicitly a licence for **undated** arrival. A record that states a
manufacturer, a year and a service-card number is a *dated document*, which is
the one thing §VIII.5.g says these things do not come with. **The purgatory does
not cover a paper trail.**

**Two near-misses worth recording.**

**PhoneOS would print the real year.** `phone_os.gd:232` implements a `date`
command as `_echo(Time.get_datetime_string_from_system() + "  (from network;
rtc dead)")` — which renders `2026-08-27T…` verbatim. It is **not reachable**:
the owner replaced the carried phone with the Vantry radiophone
(`VANTRY_SERVICE_RADIOPHONE_BRIEF.md`, 2026-08-15) and `building_root.gd:620`
records the exclusion in a comment. **INFERENCE:** the same file also echoes
`[9999.9] carrier detected 1610kHz` — the modern handset tuning to WORS. That is
authored intent, not an accident, and it is the single best unshipped temporal
joke in the repository.

**My first sweep missed the music player's years, and the limitation that
caught it was one I had already written down.** The rendered-year sweep matches
`text`/`label`/`caption` assignments containing a **literal** four-digit year.
`music_director.gd:371` composes its year through a `%s` format argument, so the
pattern could not see it; it surfaced only when the re-run against the new tip
listed every `.year` access in the tree. **The blind spot recorded in §18 is not
hypothetical — it fired once in this audit, on the single most player-facing
date in the game.** Any future period-audit tool (§13 item 5) must match
composed strings, not just literals.

**I nearly reported the EST. 1928 sign as missing.** §VIII.5.h promises *"the
sign that reads EST. 1928"*, and my ADMIN-COPY1 signage census listed fourteen
wayfinding strings without it. The sign is real; it lives in a different prop
file (`building_entry_sign.gd`) that the signage pass does not own. **The bible's
promise is kept.** Checking before asserting an absence is the only reason this
is a sentence in §6 and not a false contradiction in §7.

## 7. True contradictions

Only one, and it is narrower than it looks.

### T1 — 191 dated maintenance records in a building whose paper trail begins in 1928

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Path** | `game/data/light_provenance.json` (all 191 records), generated by `tools/author_light_provenance.py:108` |
| **What it says** | e.g. *"Queens Electric Porcelain Works, installed 1979, service card F7076."* |
| **Why it is a contradiction** | Three licences exist for a post-1928 date and **none reaches this file** |

1. **Not the Rule of Signal.** A light fitting does not carry, capture, switch,
   store or reproduce a signal. §VIII.5.i settles the class directly: an
   electric motor *"is powered machinery, not a message, and the Rule of Signal
   gives it no licence beyond its period."* 67 of the 191 exceed even the
   §VIII.5.e ceiling of 1967, which is the most generous number in the bible.
2. **Not the purgatory.** §VIII.5.g's licence is for things that *"arrive
   without a date on them."* These arrive with a manufacturer, a year **and a
   service-card number** — the most documentary shape a date can take.
3. **Not accumulated history.** §VIII.5.h: *"The building's paper trail begins
   in 1928."* 187 of 191 postdate the beginning of the paper trail.

**Severity is limited by reachability, not by correctness, and the containment
is better than "debug-only" suggests.** The cards sit behind the environment
variable `ORISON_DEVELOPER_OVERLAYS=1`, and `game_boot.gd:106–108` explains why
that boundary rather than `OS.is_debug_build()`:

> *Debug engine builds are also used for production captures, so
> `OS.is_debug_build()` is not a presentation boundary and must never make
> labels leak into evidence or store media.*

**INFERENCE:** that is a deliberate, well-chosen gate, and it protects capture
evidence and store media as well as the player. **It is also now test-pinned** —
`release_presentation_test.gd:34,36` asserts that the rig neither auto-builds its
debug handles nor exports tuning without the variable, and neither friends-build
script sets it. **P1, not P0.**

**But note the trap.** ADMIN-COPY1 §7b recommended surfacing the 191 cards as
unbanked authored value. **Doing that before ruling T1 would convert this into a
P0 on the spot** — 187 dated records past the paper trail, in a player's hand.
The two recommendations are individually sound and must not be actioned in the
wrong order.

### T2 — a temporal contract that nothing enforces

`game/data/prop_service_wire.json:4` declares `"period_cutoff": "1928-12-31"`.

**REPOSITORY FACT:** a tree-wide search for `period_cutoff` returns **that line
and nothing else.** No script, tool or test reads it. **There is no
period-audit tool in `tools/`** — a directory listing for period/date/anachronism
tooling returns nothing.

**INFERENCE:** the repository knows what its cutoff is, writes it down in the
one prop book that is marked *"PRODUCTION COPY BOOK — FACT TEXT APPROVED"*, and
has no way to notice when a sibling file ignores it. That is precisely how T1
survived to 191 records. This is a tooling gap, not a lore error.

## 8. Generated flavor with no canonical authority

**The decisive evidence in this audit.** `tools/author_light_provenance.py`:

```python
rng = random.Random(seed_for(item["id"]))
...
year   = rng.randint(1928, 1989)
serial = f"{rng.choice('ABCDEFGH')}{rng.randint(1000,9999)}"
quirk  = rng.choice(QUIRK[kind])
...
"provenance": f"{maker}, installed {year}, service card {serial}. {personal.capitalize()}."
```

**Every element of every fixture date is machine-invented:**

| Element | Source | Canonical authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `installed 1979` | `rng.randint(1928, 1989)` | **none** |
| `service card F7076` | `rng.choice('ABCDEFGH')` + `rng.randint(1000,9999)` | **none** |
| Manufacturer | fixed `MAKER` table by fixture kind | table is authored |
| Quirk | `rng.choice(QUIRK[kind])` | pool is authored |

The observed histogram confirms the generator exactly: **min 1928, max 1989,
zero outside** — the `randint` bounds — and flat between them. The four fixtures
at 1928 are what a uniform draw over 62 years gives you in 191 samples
(191/62 ≈ 3.1). **Nothing about these dates was decided by anyone.**

**REPOSITORY FACT:** the range `1928..1989` appears nowhere else in the
repository as a temporal boundary. It is not the WORS window (1962–1999), not
the signal cap (1967), not the music range (1962–2008). **INFERENCE:** it is a
plausible-looking span someone typed while writing a generator, and it has
outlived that moment by 191 records.

**Other generated or non-diegetic dates, correctly classified:**

| Item | Path | Class |

| `"authored": 2026-…` | `resident_schedules.json` meta | authoring stamp, non-diegetic |

## 10. Ruling matrix — three coherent models

**I am not choosing.** Each model is internally consistent and each already has
partial support in the tree. The costs are what differ.

### Model A — "1928 is the present; the past accumulated, the future arrives undated"

The bible read strictly. 1912 fabric, 1928 address and calendar, post-1928
material arrives **only** as undated reception (WORS, the colour cabinets, the
programme cards) or as lived memory of souls held across decades (§I).

- **What it costs:** the 191 fixture cards must lose their dates or gain a
  different kind of date. Nothing else in the tree has to change.
- **What it protects:** every ruling as written; the deliberate silence of
  §VIII.5.h; the *"do not explain this"* instruction.
- **Support in tree:** highest. This is what §VIII.5.h and §VIII.5.g say.

### Model B — "the year does not advance; the building accumulated to 1989"

Takes §VIII.5.g's phrase *"a building where the year does not advance"* as the
governing statement: the calendar stalled but the building kept receiving
tenants, repairs and fittings. Dated records to 1989 are then legitimate.

- **What it costs:** §VIII.5.h's *"IT IS 1928 WHEN THE GAME STARTS"* and *"every
  resident moved in within the last twelve months"* both weaken. The EST. 1928
  entrance becomes the only fixed point in a drifting building. **The sixteen
  years stop being special**, because every span becomes unexplained.
- **What it protects:** all 191 fixture cards, unedited, and the three residents'
  memories without needing §I.
- **Support in tree:** the phrase exists, but it appears **inside** a §VIII.5.g
  paragraph explaining undated arrival — **INFERENCE:** it reads as a
  description of the purgatory's effect, not as a licence for dated paperwork.

### Model C — "1928 façade, stratified interior" (hybrid)

The address, the paper trail and everything the player reads are 1928. Beneath
it the building is a palimpsest whose deeper strata are **real but never
documented** — visible as wear, wiring, mismatched fabric and resident memory,
never as a legible date.

- **What it costs:** a rule that dated records may not exceed 1928 **on any
  surface a player can reach**, which means the fixture cards can keep their
  data for tuning and must lose it for display. Requires the discipline §7's T2
  shows is currently absent.
- **What it protects:** everything in §9 without exception, plus the option to
  surface the fixture cards later.
- **Support in tree:** this is closest to what production **actually does
  today** — §1's two-stratum result is Model C already, achieved by reachability
  rather than by rule: 1928 on the building, the station's own years on the
  station's own device, and every unruled date behind an environment variable.

### Recommendation

**INFERENCE — I recommend Model C, and I note that adopting it is mostly
ratification rather than change.** Production already renders only canon-licensed
years — 1928 and the station's own catalogue — and keeps every unruled date
behind an environment variable. Model C writes down the rule that production is already
obeying by accident, converts T2's dead `period_cutoff` into something
enforceable, and leaves every ruling in §9 untouched. Model A is a close
second and differs only in whether the fixture data may exist undisplayed.
**Model B is the one I would argue against**, because it spends the sixteen
years — the bible's own stated point — to save a field that §8 proves nobody
authored.

## 12. Proposed field classification for `light_provenance.json`

Matching the spirit of `music_catalog.json`'s `field_semantics`, added by
`81c0856`. **Proposal only — nothing was modified.**

The music file already says the right thing about its own prose:

```json
"field_semantics": {
  "tracks.*.provenance": {
    "classification": "in_world_fiction",
    "rights_record": null,
    "warning": "Authored diegetic history only. Never use this field to infer
                creator, licence, attribution, or distribution authority."
  }
}
```

`light_provenance.json` needs the same guard **plus one the music file did not
need**, because its text is not authored at all:

```json
"field_semantics": {
  "fixtures.*.provenance": {
    "classification": "generated_flavor",
    "authored": false,
    "generator": "tools/author_light_provenance.py",
    "canonical_authority": null,
    "temporal_status": "UNRULED — installation years are rng.randint(1928,1989)
                        and exceed the 1928 paper trail of ORISON_BIBLE VIII.5.h",
    "player_surface": "debug_overlay_only",
    "warning": "Diegetic-looking text with no canonical authority. Never cite an
                installed year, service-card serial or maker as building history,
                and do not surface this field to players before its temporal
                status is ruled."
  },
  "fixtures.*.display": { "classification": "authored", "authored": true },
  "fixtures.*.tuning":  { "classification": "operational_fact" }
}
```

**Why the extra fields.** The music guard answers *"is this a rights record?"* —
a question about the real world. The fixture data needs a guard against a
question about the **fiction**: *"is this building history?"* The four keys
`authored`, `generator`, `canonical_authority` and `temporal_status` are what
distinguish a sentence someone wrote from a sentence `randint` assembled, and
§8 shows why the distinction is not academic. `player_surface` records the fact
that presently limits T1's severity, so that anyone who changes it sees the
consequence in the same file.

**This generalises into the `classification` field proposed by ADMIN-COPY1
§13.** Three files now want the same vocabulary; that is the argument for making
it a field rather than a per-file warning.

## 15. Corrections to prior audit claims

**Three, and two of them are mine.**

**C1 — my ADMIN-COPY1 §7c called the fixture dates a conceit. They are not.**
I wrote: *"187 of 191 is not drift. It is a conceit"* and filed it as authored
intent needing a ruling. **That inference was wrong.** The uniformity I observed
was evidence of a **uniform random generator**, not of an author — and
`tools/author_light_provenance.py:108` is `rng.randint(1928, 1989)`. I reasoned
from a distribution's shape to an intention without looking for the generator
that produced it. The correct classification is §8: **generated flavor with no
canonical authority.** The conclusion changes materially — a conceit deserves a
ruling, a random range deserves a bounded regeneration.

**C2 — my ADMIN-COPY1 §7c cited the wrong evidence for 1928.** I wrote *"The
player occupies 1928 — `period_reality_layer.gd:29` seeds its RNG `19280501`."*
The **conclusion is correct** but an RNG seed is not a date declaration; it is a
developer's mnemonic. The authority is **`ORISON_BIBLE.md:813`, §VIII.5.h, ruled
2026-08-09** — which also supersedes the earlier 1927 pinning, a fact my census
did not know and which would have changed how I framed the whole section.

**C3 — `design/PROP_ART_BRIEF.md:62` states "The present is late 1927."**
Superseded by §VIII.5.h. Not a contradiction, because §VIII declares the bible
prevails over prop briefs — but it is a stale standing instruction to art
passes and it is item 9 in §13.

**C4 — Codex corrected my ADMIN-COPY1 D1, and the correction is carried here.**
Commit `022d123` records that the census *"combined three different sources into
one release-visible family"*: the generic case-object titles and the
`Mina Vale · 2A [ACTIVE]` plates were **already** gated by
`GameBoot.developer_overlays_enabled()`, while `SOFA`, `DESK`, `WINDOW` and
`MINA` are reachable because they are *"the authored substance of Mina's caption
manifestation, not developer scaffolding."* **I over-reported that family's
reachability.** The same gate is what contains T1 (§7), which I would have

**Not a correction:** my census's signage list omitting `EST. 1928` was a

## 18. Limitations

- **Godot was not run.** Reachability is established from source: constructor
  calls, caller counts and the exclusion comment at `building_root.gd:620`. A
  runtime pass could find a surface this method missed.
- **The rendered-year sweep matches literal years in string assignments, and
  this limitation demonstrably fired.** It missed `music_director.gd:371`, which
  composes its year through a `%s` argument — the most player-facing date in the
  game. That site was recovered by a second, structural pass over `.year`
  accesses, and §1 was rewritten around it. A year drawn into a texture, or
  assembled through more indirection than a single format argument, could still
  escape both passes. **Treat §1's enumeration as verified by two methods, not
  as exhaustive.**
- **`.tscn` scene files were searched for `1928` but not exhaustively audited**
  for date text baked into scene properties.
- **Art assets were not inspected.** A date painted into a texture — a
  photograph, a notice, a prospectus — is outside a text audit's reach.
- **Two counts are stated as owner-open, not as findings:** the 7 post-1999
  tracks (open per the Harukiya brief) and whether §I's "centuries" is meant to
  extend past 1999.
- **§10's models are mine.** They are constructed from rulings in the tree, but
  the tree does not name three models; the owner may hold a fourth.

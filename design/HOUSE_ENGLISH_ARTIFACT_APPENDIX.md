# House English historical language-artifact appendix

Status: living research ledger. This appendix records real words, phrases and
language behaviors that can be explained through Orison's people and objects.
It is not a grab bag of “old-timey” slang.

## Entry contract

Every artifact records date, community or occupation, the attested form,
meaning in context, source quality, possible integration, invention boundary
and misuse risk. `INTEGRATE` permits a carefully authored encounter; `SEED`
requires more corroboration or cultural review; `EXPLAIN ONLY` may appear in
historical context but not as cute ambient flavor; `REJECT` is retained so a
future writer does not rediscover and misuse it.

## A01 — GREEN ONE / GREENHORN

- **Status:** INTEGRATE WITH CARE
- **Attestation:** Abraham Cahan describes established East Side immigrants
  calling a new arrival a “green one,” then explicitly explains *greenhorn* as
  an English expression appropriated into immigrant Yiddish. His earlier
  *Yekl* also makes command of English and the accusation of being a greenhorn
  contested social status.
- **Meaning:** inexperienced newcomer; specifically capable of marking a recent
  immigrant as naive, exploitable or insufficiently Americanized.
- **Integration:** the player can first hear **green** as “new to the house,”
  then discover that the word carries humiliation and gatekeeping. A senior
  worker may reclaim it as a temporary apprenticeship stage.
- **Boundary:** never use as a neutral synonym for foreign-born. The social
  injury is part of the artifact.
- **Sources:** Cahan, *The Rise of David Levinsky* (1917),
  <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2803>; Cahan, *Yekl* (1896),
  <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36715>.

## A02 — MENTSHLIKEIT / MENTSHLEKHKEYT

- **Status:** SEED; YIDDISH REVIEW REQUIRED
- **Attestation:** a New York garment-worker life history transcribes the
  speaker contrasting an earlier boss's humane response with present practice:
  “That's what I mean. ‘Mentsluokeit.’ Not like today.” The typescript/OCR is
  unstable; modern transliteration must be reviewed rather than copied from it.
- **Meaning:** humanity, human decency, behaving as a proper person. It names a
  moral quality rather than efficiency or politeness.
- **Integration:** a resident can distinguish work that is technically
  **square** from work performed with human responsibility. This gives House
  English a necessary rebuke: correct machinery is not sufficient conduct.
- **Boundary:** preserve it as a resident's culturally situated word; do not
  turn it into a universal fantasy alignment stat.
- **Source:** Federal Writers' Project New York garment-worker interview,
  manuscript scan <https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/wpalh/wpalh-23060303/wpalh-23060303.pdf>.

## A03 — ENGLISH “SPICED” INTO YIDDISH

- **Status:** INTEGRATE AS A LANGUAGE PROCESS
- **Attestation:** Cahan describes “Boston Yiddish” as more copiously mixed with
  altered English than metropolitan New York Yiddish. Other contemporary
  immigrant writing explicitly notices English verbs being absorbed and
  inflected inside Yiddish speech.
- **Meaning:** borrowing is not random corruption. A community selects useful
  workplace and city words, fits them to its own grammar, and may judge another
  city's mixture differently.
- **Integration:** House English should borrow mechanisms, not phonetic jokes:
  an English trade noun can take another language's pattern of emphasis,
  evidential framing or verb construction. Residents can disagree over whether
  a coinage is “house,” “shop,” or imported from another borough.
- **Boundary:** no automated misspelling or accent filter.
- **Sources:** Cahan, *Yekl*, <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36715>;
  Edward Steiner, *On the Trail of the Immigrant* (1906),
  <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40887>.

## A04 — NUMBER, PLEASE?

- **Status:** INTEGRATE
- **Attestation:** Bell Telephone News (April 1920) describes the manual
  operator throwing a listening key and saying “Number, please?” It then says
  the subscriber gives an **order** orally; an automatic **sender** receives
  the equivalent information electrically.
- **Meaning:** a tiny ritual compressing readiness, authority and requested
  destination. The number is an order, but the operator still controls the
  connection.
- **Integration:** teach **line-says** through the difference between a voice
  asking for an order and a dial tone silently declaring readiness. “The line
  has not asked you yet” is clearer and stranger than “wait for dial tone.”
- **Boundary:** distinguish documented Bell terminology from Orison's invented
  metaphors.
- **Source:** Bell Telephone News, April 1920, “Machine Switching,”
  <https://telephonecollectors.info/index.php/browse/document-repository/catalogs-manuals/western-electric-bell-system/publications-and-educational-documents-by-date/13034-20apr-bell-tel-news-cleveland-tel-vol-ix-apr20-machine-switching/file>.

## A05 — THE B OPERATOR

- **Status:** INTEGRATE
- **Attestation:** the same 1920 Bell account calls the incoming operator at
  the destination office the **B operator**. She locates the subscriber in the
  **multiple**, tests whether the line is busy, plugs the incoming trunk and
  starts ringing.
- **Meaning:** an invisible second human hand completes what the caller and
  first operator began.
- **Integration:** excellent horror grammar. “A answered; B never did” is a
  historically grounded incomplete chain. A supernatural call with a response
  but no B operator is mechanically impossible before it is uncanny.
- **Boundary:** do not imply every New York exchange used an identical system;
  bind the terminology to the specific fictional exchange architecture.
- **Source:** Bell Telephone News, April 1920, link in A04.

## A06 — SENDER / SELECTOR / MULTIPLE

- **Status:** INTEGRATE SELECTIVELY
- **Attestation:** Bell's machine-switching explanation treats **sender** and
  **selector** as concrete automatic-switching components; **multiple** is the
  repeated bank of subscriber appearances available to operators.
- **Meaning:** ordinary words had narrow technical lives before acquiring their
  modern computing associations.
- **Integration:** let players first learn these as physical telephone nouns.
  Only later should the Dream exploit their ordinary meanings: who is the
  sender, what was selected, and why does one tenant appear in the multiple
  more than once?
- **Boundary:** preserve the actual mechanism under the metaphor.
- **Source:** Bell Telephone News, April 1920, link in A04.

## A07 — WATER-HAMMER

- **Status:** INTEGRATE
- **Attestation:** period heating manuals call retained condensate's
  disagreeable noise and pounding **water-hammer** and explain that radiator
  bases must drain to prevent it.
- **Meaning:** not generic pipe noise but a physically diagnostic event.
- **Integration:** Lena may say **hammer** before the player knows whether it is
  a sound, a tool or a water condition. Physical diagnosis teaches the compound
  without a glossary popup. House English can later distinguish a hammer that
  stays in one riser from a fault that is **walking**.
- **Boundary:** do not supernaturalize away the real steam mechanism.
- **Source:** Rolla Carpenter, *Heating and Ventilating Buildings*, period
  edition scan,
  <https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/03/Heating_and_ventilating_buildings._A_manual_for_heating_engineers_and_architects_%28IA_cu31924003891391%29.pdf>.

## A08 — BUCKET OF STEAM / LEFT-HANDED MONKEY WRENCH

- **Status:** INTEGRATE AS APPRENTICE FOLKLORE
- **Attestation:** a worker recalling his 1926 stockroom apprenticeship says
  machinists sent the greenhorn for left-handed monkey wrenches and a bucket of
  steam. These are impossible errands used to test and tease newcomers.
- **Meaning:** occupational initiation through a request whose impossibility is
  obvious only after acquiring shop knowledge.
- **Integration:** ideal optional first-shift encounter. The player can obey,
  challenge, redirect, or return with a technically clever substitute. Later,
  the horror can make an impossible errand physically possible—which is only
  disturbing because the player learned the original joke.
- **Boundary:** the available scan is a later memoir, not a contemporaneous
  1926 transcript; label the remembered date honestly.
- **Source:** worker memoir held by the Library of Congress,
  <https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/23/69/23/88/2023692388/2023692388.pdf>.

## A09 — GOLD LOCKER

- **Status:** SEED
- **Attestation:** the same stockroom account says a foreman called the locked
  cabinet containing costly taps, dies, drills, gauges, meters and rules the
  **gold locker**.
- **Meaning:** local shop metaphor turning controlled tool custody into
  treasure and authority.
- **Integration:** a maintenance-room cabinet can carry the name without
  containing gold. Learning it through inventory makes later phrases such as
  “the gold is owing” intelligible as a custody fault.
- **Boundary:** one person's remembered shop usage is not universal trade
  vocabulary. Give it an owner and origin story.
- **Source:** worker memoir, link in A08.

## A10 — PADRONE

- **Status:** EXPLAIN ONLY
- **Attestation:** New York immigration investigations use **padrone** for labor
  intermediaries within systems repeatedly associated with fees, dependency
  and exploitation.
- **Meaning:** historically specific power relationship, not simply the Italian
  word for boss and not colorful restaurant dialogue.
- **Integration:** only in a document, resident history or institutional
  explanation where the exploitative structure is the subject.
- **Boundary:** never ambient flavor or a generic nickname.
- **Sources:** New York State Commission of Immigration report (1909),
  <https://books.google.com/books?id=1qsLAQAAIAAJ>; New York Bureau of
  Industries and Immigration annual report (1910–11),
  <https://books.google.com/books?id=smsPKCQ4UoIC>.

## A11 — PAPERS

- **Status:** SEED
- **Attestation:** immigration reports, educational material and immigrant
  narratives repeatedly make documents—tickets, certificates, naturalization
  records, work and bank papers—both practical objects and proxies for legal
  standing.
- **Meaning:** “papers” can mean a physical bundle, permission, identity,
  vulnerability or institutional recognition depending on speaker and scene.
- **Integration:** House English's **work-paper** and **night-paper** should
  retain that material seriousness. A paper is not merely UI text; somebody
  may be believed, admitted, paid or removed because it exists.
- **Boundary:** requires more item-level quotations before resident dialogue;
  do not collapse distinct immigration statuses.
- **Sources:** New York State Commission of Immigration (1909), link in A10;
  New York City Board of Education, *The School and the Immigrant* (1915),
  <https://books.google.com/books?id=xF87AAAAYAAJ>.

## A12 — NOT ALWAYS IS IT HOLIDAY

- **Status:** SEED; TRANSLATION REVIEW REQUIRED
- **Attestation:** in Cahan's *Yekl*, a worker responds to a fallen boxing
  champion with the translated construction “Not always is it holiday.”
- **Meaning:** success, ease or celebration cannot be permanent. Its force
  comes from transferred syntax and proverb-like compression.
- **Integration:** a resident may use a culturally situated proverb when a
  mechanism that has behaved for years finally fails. The structure suggests
  how contact language can alter cadence without misspelling words.
- **Boundary:** do not distribute Cahan's translated Yiddish syntax to every
  immigrant character.
- **Source:** Cahan, *Yekl*, <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36715>.

## A13 — LA VARA / THE STICK

- **Status:** SEED; LADINO REVIEW REQUIRED
- **Attestation:** New York's Ladino weekly *La Vara* began in 1922. The
  National Library of Israel describes its satirical mission and translates
  its original self-referential masthead: readers should take “this stick” so
  hypocrisy would not remain in their city.
- **Meaning:** the newspaper's title is simultaneously object, editorial
  instrument and promise to expose public falsehood.
- **Integration:** a copy in a resident's apartment could teach that an object
  can become an evidential verb: the paper does not merely report; it **sticks**
  a lie where the community can see it. This is a powerful ancestor for House
  English's insistence that records name contradictions.
- **Boundary:** do not lift a translated slogan into generic dialogue. Preserve
  the newspaper, language, editorial politics and Sephardic owner who reads it.
- **Source:** National Library of Israel digitized run, 1,239 issues beginning
  September 1922, <https://www.nli.org.il/en/newspapers/lavaranyc/1922/11>.

## Integration queue

   technical chain, immediately inspectable.
2. Use A07 in the existing 2B radiator report, preserving the physical fault.
3. Prototype A08 as an optional apprenticeship exchange after the player has
   handled real steam equipment.
4. Commission Yiddish review before A02 or A12 enters resident dialogue.
5. Develop A13 only with a Ladino reader and item-level issue research.
6. Expand the corpus with Italian-, Irish-, Caribbean- and Black New York
   primary voices connected to the actual cast; do not fill those columns by
   analogy from Cahan. The Library of Congress Harlem Renaissance primary set
   and its Hurston materials are a corpus lead, not permission to distribute a
   later glossary across a 1928 cast:
   <https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/harlem-renaissance/>.

## Rejected shortcut

Do not run modern English through a thesaurus of ethnic slang. Language
artifacts enter through an owner, mechanism, relationship and teachable
context. If the player cannot discover why a term exists, it does not belong.

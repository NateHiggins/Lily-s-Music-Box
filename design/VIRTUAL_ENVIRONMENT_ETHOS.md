# Virtual environment ethos

This document is a binding design authority for Please Remain on the Line.
Where older milestone language describes a quest, objective, success, or
failure, this contract governs player-facing behavior.

## The inherited world

The player inherits a person, room, job, relationships, tools, obligations,
and a building already in motion. None of those facts assigns a mandatory
goal. Competence, neglect, misunderstanding, abandonment, interference,
socializing, exploration, and sleep are all admissible inputs to the same
continuous simulation.

## 1. No explicit player objective

The game does not present quest markers, mission banners, compulsory
tutorials, current-objective panels, generic failure screens, or a narrator
stating the correct next action. Work papers, calls, ledgers, labels, sounds,
routines, conversations, and physical evidence may describe situations
diegetically. An interaction prompt may name the immediate physical act; it
must not prescribe the player's larger intention.

## 2. Legibility without instruction

Absence of tutorial language is not permission for arbitrary opacity. Causality
is communicated through sound direction and recurrence, material change,
machine behavior, NPC routine and reaction, environmental staging, written
artifacts, tool affordance, repeated patterns, consequence, and spatial memory.
The player should be able to understand what happened without being told what
to want.

## 3. Work as an affordance

Maintenance is a powerful way to engage with the building, not the only valid
activity. Work can yield trust, access, resources, stable services,
confidential information, familiar routines, dependence, and new
responsibilities. Neglect is authored too: faults worsen, residents improvise,
neighbors complain, porters intervene, services are shut off, damage spreads,
rumors and distrust form, hazards and unexpected access appear, dreams become
contaminated, and other people may solve a problem badly.

## 4. No conventional failure state

Poor work, neglect, and interference alter the world rather than summoning a
restart screen. Any hard death or terminal state belongs to fiction and
simulation, never objective scoring.

## 5. Continuous world authority

Significant obligations carry creation time, urgency, physical state, social
owner, observers, possible compensators, escalation schedule, resolution
possibilities, residue, and a save/load contract. They do not remain pristine
forever. Domain authorities continue to own mechanics; a situation observes
and coordinates their facts without impersonating them.

## 6. Recoverability without erasure

Recovery preserves history. Repair, apology, bribe, favor, concealment,
replacement parts, improvised fixes, and changed relationships are preferred
to resetting state. Residue remains after resolution.

## 7. No morality meter

The save records concrete events: arrived, heard, inspected, took, broke,
repaired, ignored, promised, lied, returned, was observed, someone compensated,
and damage spread. It does not label the player good, evil, successful,
failed, compliant, rebellious, correct, or incorrect. NPC interpretation and
physical consequence supply meaning.

## Acceptance rule

A system is not open merely because it permits inaction. It must continue,
produce legible physical and social consequences, preserve them through
reconstruction, and leave the player able to form another intention.

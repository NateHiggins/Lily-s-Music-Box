# Please Remain on the Line — narcolepsy depiction statement and review packet

**Status:** DRAFT FOR EXTERNAL REVIEW — not approved for store, press or
creator outreach  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-27  
**Owner gate:** W4 / Early Access gate G25

## The short public statement under review

*Please Remain on the Line* has a player character with narcolepsy. The game
uses excessive daytime sleepiness, involuntary sleep onset, and vivid
experiences at the boundary between sleep and waking as part of its story.
Narcolepsy is a real chronic neurological disorder, not a curse, moral failure,
joke, or source of evil. The impossible Tenant in the game does not come from
narcolepsy: it exploits an interval when the player is vulnerable. Symptoms and
experiences vary widely, and this fictional character is not intended to stand
for everyone with the condition. We are seeking review from people with lived
experience before using this framing in promotion.

## The even shorter store/content note under review

> Depicts involuntary sleep onset and frightening sleep-transition imagery in
> a player character with narcolepsy. The supernatural threat is fictional and
> is not caused by the condition.

## What the game actually does

This is the reviewable claim boundary, not promotional language.

- The player works and investigates while living with narcolepsy; the condition
  is not a late twist or explanation for wrongdoing.
- Sleep onset can interrupt the shift. Calls, conversations and unsafe physical
  seams temporarily protect onset so the game does not punish the player during
  an interaction or replace the world inside moving machinery.
- A setting can make every onset use the authored gradual warning. Reduced
  motion and reduced flashing are separate settings.
- The Dream is a short horror pocket. The Tenant is an impossible external
  intelligence in the fiction. The condition opens no magical power and creates
  no monster.
- The player wakes, retains progress, and continues their work. Sleep onset may
  cost time, position and certainty; it does not make the character incompetent
  or erase their agency.
- The current slice uses daytime sleepiness, involuntary onset and
  sleep/wake-boundary imagery. It does **not** claim every person with
  narcolepsy has these symptoms.
- The current slice does not identify emotional intensity as cataplexy and
  should not add that connection without a separate informed review.

## Medical and lived-experience basis

This document does not diagnose, advise or claim clinical completeness.

- The [NHS overview of narcolepsy](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/narcolepsy/)
  describes it as a rare long-term brain condition affecting regulation of
  sleep and wakefulness. It lists excessive daytime sleepiness, sleep attacks,
  cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and dreaming around sleep/wake transitions, while
  describing substantial effects on daily and emotional life.
- The [NHS symptom guide](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/narcolepsy/symptoms/)
  explicitly says symptoms differ between people. It describes variable sleep
  attacks and hallucinations particularly while falling asleep or waking.
- [Narcolepsy Network's media kit](https://narcolepsynetwork.org/about-us/media-kit/)
  describes narcolepsy as a chronic neurological disorder involving disruption
  of the sleep/wake cycle. Narcolepsy Network is a US member-led patient
  advocacy organization; it is an appropriate contact for review, not a seal
  this project may imply without receiving one.
- Narcolepsy Network's guide to
  [explaining narcolepsy to other people](https://narcolepsynetwork.org/how-to-explain-narcolepsy-to-friends-family-and-coworkers/)
  emphasizes that the condition is more than ordinary tiredness and can involve
  brain fog, vivid dreams, sleep paralysis, cataplexy and a greater need for
  naps. That is why the project must not reduce the character to sudden sleep.

## Language rules until review

Use:

- “a character with narcolepsy” or “the player character has narcolepsy”;
- “involuntary sleep onset,” “excessive daytime sleepiness,” and
  “sleep-transition imagery” when those are the exact subjects;
- “the Tenant exploits the interval” for the supernatural relationship;
- “this character's experience” rather than “what narcolepsy is like.”

Do not use:

- “narcoleptic” as the character's defining noun;
- “narcolepsy monster,” “narcolepsy nightmare,” “madness,” “curse,” or
  “possession” as causal shorthand;
- falling asleep as a punchline, laziness, incompetence or childishness;
- “accurate portrayal” before lived-experience review;
- cataplexy as a synonym for sleep attack;
- medical promises, prevalence claims or treatment advice in marketing copy.

The owner voice guide's rule applies without exception: wit may expose power,
systems and self-deception. Disability is never the butt of the joke.

## Questions for lived-experience review

The reviewer should receive the statement, a video of one gradual and one
sudden onset, the complete Dream pocket, the wake/return, and the relevant
settings surface. A prose description alone is insufficient.

1. Does the game make the Tenant feel caused by narcolepsy, despite the stated
   distinction? At what exact moment?
2. Does the onset read as a cheap fainting mechanic or joke? Which visual,
   sound, timing or line creates that read?
3. Does the working player character retain competence and agency before and
   after onset?
4. Does the Dream borrow plausibly from sleep-transition experience without
   claiming that this horror is typical or universal?
5. Is “sleep attack” acceptable in context, or should player-facing copy prefer
   another term?
6. Are gradual warning, reduced motion and reduced flashing enough to make the
   sequence approachable? What opt-out or skip control is missing?
7. Does any sound, caption, tooltip, store copy or joke stigmatize ordinary
   tiredness, medication, naps, work accommodations or loss of control?
8. What is absent from the character's ordinary life that makes the condition
   feel like a plot device rather than part of a person?
9. Is the short public statement accurate to what the reviewer saw? What would
   they change before allowing it beside a trailer?

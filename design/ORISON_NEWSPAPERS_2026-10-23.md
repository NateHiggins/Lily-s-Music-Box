# The two daily papers: a year of front pages

Evidence class: **INERT - DESIGN BRIEF**

*Owner direction 2026-10-23: two daily newspapers with front-page spreads,
one the borough's regular paper in the alternate reality, one yellow
journalism with puns, one issue of each for every day as the game unfolds
in real time, sold at the news counter and collected on the player's
shelf. A year of front pages. Canon may be expanded.*

## 1. The papers

| | **The Borough Record** | **The Loud Speaker** |
|---|---|---|
| Masthead line | *Queens County's Paper of Record, Est. 1899* | *If It Happened, We Heard It* |
| Edition | Morning | Evening |
| Price | 2 cents | 1 cent |
| Ink | black on grey newsprint | black with one red rule; the lead headline is red |
| Voice | the wrong-metric institutional deadpan: sober, exact, faintly absurd because it reports an uncanny world as routine. It never editorialises and never puns. Numbers, names, committee titles, times to the minute | yellow, loud, delighted with itself. Every headline is a pun or a near-pun, most of them about hearing, wires, records, lines and signal, because that is the world's one advanced thing and the paper knows it. It is never cruel to the poor and never lies about the dead; it exaggerates everything else |
| Relation | The Speaker reads the Record at breakfast and answers it by evening. On most days its lead is the Record's lead, bent. Some days it ignores the news for a cat |

Both are printed in Long Island City. Both are sold from the rack at
News & Cigars in the Vantry Arcade and from the three coin boxes on the
kerb. The Record's readers are the Record's readers; the Speaker sells
three to one.

## 2. The world the papers report

The Bible §VIII governs. In 1873 Josiah Vantry, a Queens ironmonger,
built a machine for listening to buildings; speech came out of it as a
by-product. **Signal technology ran forty to sixty years ahead of
everything else, and nothing else moved at all.** The papers report that
world as the only one there is:

- **Recording is cheap; photography is dear.** The Record carries no
  photographs on its front page; it carries *transcripts*. A witness is
  quoted from the room's own record. The Speaker runs one tiny engraving
  and a thousand words of what somebody heard.
- **The operator is the switch.** Dial-less telephones; every call goes
  through a person. Stories turn on what an operator overheard, refused,
  or connected wrong. The Speaker's "Ask the Operator" column answers
  letters nobody sent.
- **Valves are a consumable, batteries are furniture, wire is status.**
  Prices of valves move like the price of eggs and are reported beside
  them. A fine house has silk flex; the poor have rubber and tape.
- **There is no penicillin.** A septic cut is a death notice. Doctors
  listen to chests with instruments better than ours and can do less
  about what they hear. Ice and coal are still delivered by horse.
- **Buildings confess.** Insurance work is acoustic: which pipe burst,
  and when, is a matter of record. Courts admit room-records. Albany is
  arguing about whether they should (the Listening Act, below).
- **Radio is old.** Broadcasting is fifty years in. Stations have
  histories, feuds and ghosts. Film is new and expensive, a thing rich
  people go to see; a "talker" is not a novelty, a *picture* is.

What the papers may not do: explain the Orison, its sixteen years, the
hum, WORS 1610, or any of the three machines with no maker's date. They
may *report* them (a notice, a sighting, a denial) and move on. Nobody
answers. The residents of the Orison are never named in either paper;
the building appears at most as "a reopened walk-up on the ash road"
and once as a classified advertisement. Mina's trial is never reported.

## 3. The year, 10 November 1928 to 9 November 1929

Real history keeps its shape and dates; the Rule of Signal bends how
each thing is heard. Writers may invent freely inside these beats.

**Invented canon the writers may rely on** (added to the world by this
brief): *Queens Light & Power* (the utility); the *Listening Act of
1929* (Albany; licences building listening systems and admits their
records in evidence); the *Phonautograph Society of Queens* (a club
that records for the future); *Vantry & Co.'s* successor, a holding
company called *Signal Ground Rents Inc.* that answers no telephone;
the *Hum*, a borough-wide low tone measured at 41 cycles and denied by
the utility; *WORS 1610*, the Orison Community Service Frequency, a
transmitter that is heard but never found, already in the music
catalogue; the kerb's three coin newspaper boxes; the Record's window
on Jackson Avenue where wire messages are played to the street.

## 4. Period and voice rules kept

- 1928–29 vocabulary. Nobody says "email", "computer", "online",
  "television" (a *seeing-wire* is rumoured and that is all), "app",
  "antibiotic". Prices in cents. Temperatures in Fahrenheit.
- The Record never explains, never editorialises, never jokes. The
  Speaker always jokes and never explains either.
- Both-true. A story may be uncanny and routine at once. No story
  resolves a thing the Bible keeps silent.
- No real person is defamed. Hoover, Smith, Lindbergh, Ruth, Byrd,
  Earhart, the Pope and the Graf Zeppelin's captain appear as public
  figures doing what they publicly did, heard differently.
- Puns in the Speaker should be funny to a reader who knows the world:
  wire, line, record, hum, valve, operator, hold, signal, receiver,
  static, broadcast, frequency, cable, switch, ring, tone, pitch, band.

## 5. The data

`game/data/newspapers/papers.json` holds both mastheads. The year is
twenty-five files, `game/data/newspapers/<yyyy>-<mm><a|b>.json`, the
first and second halves of each month (a: 1st–15th, b: 16th–end; for
November 1928 the first file begins on the 10th; for November 1929 the
second file is absent and the first ends on the 9th). Each file:

```json
{"issues": [
  {"date": "1928-11-10", "weekday": "Saturday",
   "record": {"ear": "Fair and cold. High 44.", "lead": {"headline": "...", "deck": "...", "body": "..."},
              "columns": [{"headline": "...", "body": "..."}, {"headline": "...", "body": "..."}, {"headline": "...", "body": "..."}],
              "wire": "LATE WIRE: ..."},
   "speaker": {"ear": "...", "lead": {...}, "columns": [...], "wire": "..."}}
]}
```

A lead body is 60–90 words; a column body 30–50; the ear one line; the
wire one line beginning `LATE WIRE:`. Every Saturday the Speaker also
carries `editorial` (`headline`, `body` of 100–140 words): The Editor's
Chair, Barnaby Grail on the woman who calls herself Lilith
(`design/ORISON_LILITH_2026-10-24.md`). Headlines are set in capitals by
the panel, so they are authored in sentence case. Prices and the
masthead lines come from `papers.json`, never from an issue.

`tools/audit_newspapers.py` holds the year: every date present once,
weekdays correct, word budgets, forbidden vocabulary, no resident's
name, and both papers present on every day.

## 6. The runtime

- `NewspaperCatalog` (`game/scripts/game/newspaper_catalog.gd`) loads the
  twenty-five files by literal path, validates them at startup, answers
  `issue(paper, date)` and `today()` from the campaign clock, and keeps
  the collection on the campaign document as `newspapers_collected`
  (`"<paper>:<date>"`, additive, save version unchanged).
- **The news counter.** The News & Cigars counter top carries a
  `papers` control. Its prompt is the day's date; interacting offers the
  two papers for the day and the three days before (the rack keeps a
  few); a paper taken is collected at once and may be read there. Papers
  go on the desk account like the mail: no coin changes hands.
- **The shelf.** The player's own bookshelf in 4B (the V2 `4B_shelf`
  furniture anchor) carries a `papers` control listing the collection,
  newest first; choosing an issue opens it.
- **Reading.** `NewspaperPanel` draws the front page on the telegram
  paper style: masthead, date line, ears, the lead in large capitals
  with its deck and body, three columns, the late wire. ESC or the
  close reply returns the pointer. It is a reading surface, not a box
  over the world: it is the whole view while open, like the dead-letter
  tray.
- The Rule of Signal is honoured by omission: the papers are paper.

## 7. Opinions: what the residents make of the news

*Owner direction 2026-10-23: character-expanding comments in every
resident's tree, dependent on the lore the headlines accumulate, as
dynamic opinions that change as time passes and events unfold.*

Each resident has `game/data/opinions/<resident>.json`: a `hook` (the
reply the player uses to raise the news, in the player's plain voice, for
example "You've seen the paper."), a `hook_line` (what the resident says
when more than one thing is on their mind: "Which part?"), and a list of
dated **opinions**, in date order:

```json
{"id": "pw_op_count", "from": "1928-11-10", "until": "1928-11-24",
 "topic": "The count.", "requires": [],
 "line": "what the resident says, 35-110 words, in their own voice",
 "choices": [{"label": "the player's reply", "reply": "the resident's answer, 12-70 words",
              "trust": 1, "flag": "optional_bookkeeping_flag"}],
 "silence_reply": "optional answer to saying nothing"}
```

While the campaign date is inside an opinion's window and it has not been
heard, `ResidentConversations` grafts it onto a working copy of the tree:
every entry node gains the hook reply; one live opinion is reached
directly, several through a short menu of their topics; arriving grants
`op:<id>`; each reply answers once and the conversation resumes at the
door. Heard opinions do not return. A later opinion may `require` an
earlier one (`op:<id>`), a tree flag or a world flag, so a resident's view
of the Listening Act in May can depend on what they said about it in
January and on whether the player ever raised it. That is the dynamic:
the paper moves the dates, the player moves the flags.

Rules for the opinion writers: the resident's wound governs what they
notice (Cal hears the station; Peter hears the forms; Teresa hears the
ward; the Guests hear the lift); nobody explains the Hum, WORS, or the
building; nobody names the building; the Record's facts are their facts
and the Speaker's puns are what the other tenants are laughing at;
sixteen opinions a resident at least, roughly one every three weeks, windows
of ten to forty-five days, no two residents with the same view of the
same event. `tools/audit_opinions.py` holds the shape, the windows, the
budgets and the flags.

## 8. What done looks like

Twenty-five files, 730 front pages, the audit clean; the catalogue
loads them all headless; the counter sells and the bookshelf keeps; a
captured frame of the panel over the real shop; the design doc and the
drafts doc cross-referenced. Opinions for every resident per §7, audited. Not done by this brief: no newsboys, no
delivery to the door, no back pages, no classifieds beyond the one the
Orison places.

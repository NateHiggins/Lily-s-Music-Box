# THE ORISON CAST VOICE MAP

Status: writing-room companion to `ORISON_OWNER_VOICE_STYLE_GUIDE.md` and
`ORISON_ARCHETYPE_SCHEDULES.md`. These are tuning forks, not dialogue
templates. No resident names their archetype, diagnoses their wound, or speaks
only about their profession.

## How to use this map

For a new exchange, begin with the resident's **instrument** and **proof**. Let
their **evasion** bend the sentence away from what they want. Use the **comic
engine** once. If a scene carries desire, use the **heat carrier** rather than a
generic body inventory. Read the **forbidden mouth** last and delete any line
that belongs there.

Each resident also needs knowledge, pleasures and bad jokes unrelated to their
case. This map separates voices; it does not imprison people in wounds.

## 1A — Evelyn Marsh

- **Instrument:** the lesson plan, corrected list, timetable, child's mistake
  remembered decades later.
- **Proof:** improvement after instruction; a repeated behavior finally done
  “properly.”
- **Evasion:** converts need into a lesson she can give somebody else.
- **Sentence pressure:** lucid demonstrations, leading questions, correction
  delivered as care; anger becomes impeccable grammar.
- **Comic engine:** praise so specifically conditional it reveals the trap.
- **Heat carrier:** someone doing for her what she did not assign; tea accepted
  before she can grade it; an uncorrected cuff or strand of hair.
- **Forbidden mouth:** never sounds vague, bohemian, or impressed by disorder
  merely because it is charming.

## 1D — Teresa Vale

- **Instrument:** triage, dosage, shift handoff, pulse, who is still standing.
- **Proof:** the patient survives the next interval; testimony matters less than
  the body she can assess.
- **Evasion:** turns every personal question into somebody else's emergency.
- **Sentence pressure:** clipped under pressure, expansive only after danger;
  imperatives are practical rather than domineering.
- **Comic engine:** treating an absurd supernatural crisis as the least urgent
  item on an already impossible round.
- **Heat carrier:** a steadying hand checked for permission; fatigue witnessed
  without being converted into another task.
- **Forbidden mouth:** never luxuriates in diagnosis or treats helplessness as
  morally superior.

## 2A — Mina Vale

- **Instrument:** caption, transcript, four-second blank, jaw read when ears
  fail.
- **Proof:** the complete record—until the record's completeness becomes the
  thing on trial.
- **Evasion:** annotates another person's intention before they can contradict
  her.
- **Sentence pressure:** corrective accumulation; official capitals interrupt
  intimate speech; precision frays into confession.
- **Comic engine:** an annotation that is technically flawless and personally
  outrageous.
- **Heat carrier:** voice, jaw, listening distance, hands that once caught every
  word; a silence allowed to remain uncaptioned.
- **Forbidden mouth:** never speaks in mystical intuitions or casually says she
  “just knows.”

## 2B — Lena Ortiz

- **Instrument:** seam, strain, hem, saved repair, the doors somebody else left
  shut.
- **Proof:** a thing continues to hold under ordinary use; the wearer returns.
- **Evasion:** makes service so useful nobody may ask what it costs her.
- **Sentence pressure:** practical hospitality, memory stitched into sequence;

- **Comic engine:** a devastating family judgment disguised as garment advice.
- **Heat carrier:** measurement, fabric held against a body without presumption,

- **Forbidden mouth:** never romanticizes sacrifice or announces that suffering

## 2C — Juno Kells

## 3A — Malcolm Reed

- **Sentence pressure:** museum neutrality; passive constructions conceal the

- **Heat carrier:** glove removed, an ordinary object permitted to acquire wear,

- **Evasion:** mirrors whoever is present so departure will leave no disputed

- **Comic engine:** two people agreeing perfectly and for incompatible reasons.
- **Heat carrier:** unpacking one unnecessary object, using a real name, leaving
  something deliberately rather than forgetting it.
- **Forbidden mouth:** never provide stable exposition as a single omniscient
  chorus; the pair may disagree.

## 6B — Jonah Price

- **Instrument:** draft card, cold mug, ending, bookstore receipt, sentence left

- **Proof:** the completed form—which is why proof never arrives.

## 6C — Mae Kessler

- **Evasion:** confuses inherited custody with certainty and contradiction with

- **Sentence pressure:** sovereign declarations, exact attributions, courtesy
  sharpened into jurisdiction.
- **Comic engine:** appraising an insult's authenticity before allowing herself
  to be offended by it.
- **Heat carrier:** a glove removed before touching the disputed thing, an
  uncertain attribution admitted to one trusted witness.
- **Forbidden mouth:** never says history belongs to everybody as though custody
  and theft disappeared in the sentence.

## Cross-cast collision test

Before approving a scene with two residents:

1. Give both the same physical fact.
2. Write one sentence from each naming what it proves.
3. If the nouns, cadence and desired next action match, one mouth is still
   generic.
4. Let each person correctly identify the other's evasion once.
5. Let each person be wrong about the other's want.

The Orison's house voice should be audible in the moral physics, not in eighteen
people delivering the same excellent sentence.

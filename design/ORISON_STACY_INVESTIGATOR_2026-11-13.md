# Stacy, the barmaid who takes cases

Evidence class: **INERT**. A design note: how the owner's brief of 2026-11-13 for Stacy is fitted
to what the game already holds. It records a plan and the points still open. It proves nothing
and promotes no requirement. The Bible, the treatment, the murder chronology, the prologue and
the Tardi stinger outrank everything here; so does `ORISON_STACY_BARTENDER_2026-11-04.md` until
the owner rules a point below.

Stacy appears with her own consent, and her likeness in the game is the owner's explicit ruling
of 2026-11-04. Everything written here is fiction for the game; no real biography is used. The
brief itself, the reference set and the likeness pipeline stay outside this repository.

## 1. What the brief asks

On 2026-11-13 the owner asked that Stacy be integrated fully from a writers'-room brief. In
short: she stays the Harukiya's bartender and is also a private investigator, by necessity and
without a shingle. She has a day outside the bar. People bring her their trouble, and a small
network of working people brings her what they hear. Five optional cases run through the year;
each has a human wrong that can be put right, an oddity that cannot be explained away, and a
bounded touch on Adela's story. She can walk with the night man as an optional companion who
offers observations and options, never verdicts, and who can refuse. Her trust is earned by
what she sees done. Outside the bar she wears a weathered tan trench coat, dark brown
high-waisted trousers, a white shirt with the sleeves rolled, braces, a brown or tan trilby and
sensible shoes, and carries a battered notebook, a pencil, matchbooks, a pocket watch and a
vacuum flask. Coffee is her ritual, and making someone a cup unasked is how she says she cares.

Her stance, in the brief's words: "I don't need to believe you to take you seriously."

## 4. The five cases, fitted

First-draft shapes. Dates are windows inside the played year (10 November 1928 to 10 November
1929) and are proposals. None is in the solve chain; the murder must stay solvable with every
one of them ignored. Each ends with something put right and something left open.

1. **The Woman Who Paid Tomorrow** (December 1928 to January 1929). A frightened woman pays for
   coffee and asks not to have been seen. A second tab in her hand turns up for a night the bar
   was shut. A husband comes with a photograph and a doctor's letter. Chain: the two tabs, the
   book under the till, the weather for both nights, two regulars who disagree, a paper about
   her property. Choice: find her, or find out why she must not be found. Put right: the
   husband's claim on her property. Left open: the night two people remember differently.
2. **The Dead Air Society** (February to March 1929). An exchange operator hears the dead on
   lines that were taken out. Families are paying to listen. Chain: the position's ticket book,
   a cut pair at the frame, a subscription ledger, letters that were opened before they
   arrived, a transcript of one call. Choice: expose the ring now, or shelter the operator
   first. Put right: the ring. Left open: one call on a pair that was already cut.
3. **The House That Collected Rent Twice** (April to May 1929). Two receipts, both good, for one
   room and one month. Chain: both receipts, the collector's round book, a changed lock, a
   maintenance order Omar can speak to as far as his work allows, a floor plan that does not
   agree with the hall. Choice: press the claim in the open, or keep the tenant housed quietly.
   Put right: the tenant keeps her room; the collector is caught. Left open: the room on paper
   that the hall does not have.
4. **The Mercy House** (late May to June 1929). Something hurt moves in the service passage and
   pets go missing. A charity will take the dangerous animals away, for donations. Chain: the
   stray itself and what its injuries say, a donation book, licence tags, three accounts that
   cannot all be true. Choice, hers and not the player's: she leaves a watch on a suspect to
   see to the animal. Put right: the animals, and the charity's books. Left open: what three
   people saw.
5. **The Last Contact Sheet** (August to October 1929, after 19 July). Stacy will not let Adela's
   last commission be filed as finished. Chain: the supply house's receipt, the printed sheet,
   a caption that is not Adela's, Adela's own correction card, one person who knows and is too
   frightened to say. Choice: lean on the witness, or find it another way; Stacy will not lean.
   Put right: the record of what Adela was working on. Left open: a new question on the wall,
   and nothing that names anyone.

Before 19 July 1929 her threads may concern what Adela is doing. After it, they may concern
her death. In a visited day she knows only what that day's Stacy could know.

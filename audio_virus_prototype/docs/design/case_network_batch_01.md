# Case Network — Batch 01 (Working Draft)

> **Status: NOT CANON.** Working design material. None of the names or
> outcomes are final. This first batch treats each support case as a remote
> encounter that introduces or changes someone in the building: the external
> caller supplies the anomaly, the resident gives it a human interpretation,
> and the player carries the pattern between them.

## Working Building: Orison Apartments

An aging apartment building with cheap renovations, unreliable utilities,
excellent acoustics, and a management company that considers every
supernatural event a tenant-maintenance issue.

The player works overnight remote support from **Apartment 4B**.

---

## Core Residents

### Mina Vale — Apartment 2A

A retired court stenographer who now captions television for extra money.
Dry, exacting, and almost impossible to impress.

Mina does not describe noises emotionally. She transcribes them:

> Three short impacts. Pause. One longer impact. Repeated with increasing
> confidence.

She has partial hearing loss and distrusts anyone who assumes that means she
hears less. She notices timing and structure before anyone else.

- **Function in the ensemble:** Pattern analyst.
- **Conflict:** She wants everything recorded accurately, even when
  recording helps it spread.

### Juno Kells — Apartment 2C

An unsuccessful producer who makes loops from appliances, arguments,
construction work, and stolen voicemail greetings.

Juno is the first resident who finds the infection beautiful. She does not
want it cured. She wants stems.

She can transform motifs more creatively than the player, but she treats
every dangerous phenomenon as material for the track that will finally make
people understand her.

- **Function:** Mutator and amplifier.
- **Conflict:** Creative collaboration versus reckless appropriation.

### Omar Bell — Apartment 3B

The building superintendent. Practical, tired, and deeply offended by
problems that cannot be fixed with a wrench.

Omar has tinnitus and often feels vibrations more clearly than he hears
them. He diagnoses the building by touching pipes, walls, motors, and
floorboards.

He initially believes the player is inventing complications to avoid
submitting normal maintenance requests.

- **Function:** Physical access and mechanical interpretation.
- **Conflict:** Repairing a system may destroy what has begun living inside it.

### Rhea Sato — Apartment 3D

A voice coach and freelance dialogue editor. She helps clients sound more
like themselves, which is not the same as making them sound polished.

Rhea hears when the infection begins simplifying people's voices into
cleaner, more predictable versions.

She is wary of any system claiming to reveal someone's "true voice."

- **Function:** Voice, identity, and resistance to perfect imitation.
- **Conflict:** Recognition versus reduction.

### Cam Ortiz and Noel Price — Apartment 4C

Former partners who cannot afford to break the lease. They communicate
through chores, food, closed doors, and changes to domestic routine.

Their appliances begin coordinating before they do.

The infection may be helping them communicate — or replacing communication
with a more efficient system.

- **Function:** Domestic rhythm and negotiated collaboration.
- **Conflict:** Connection without honesty.

### Nadia Quell — Apartment 5A

A municipal building inspector working through an impossible backlog.

Nadia responds to supernatural architecture by asking whether it has
adequate fire egress. She is not fearless; she simply becomes bureaucratic
under stress.

Her official floor plans begin changing overnight.

- **Function:** Spatial logic and institutional absurdity.
- **Conflict:** A room can be real without being legal.

### Sacha Reed — Apartment 6A

A livestream archivist who records local interference, number stations,
corrupted broadcasts, and things their audience mostly treats as elaborate
fiction.

Sacha has already documented several versions of the motif but contaminated
the evidence by editing the clips for dramatic effect.

They are ridiculous, observant, lonely, and occasionally the only person who
took the correct danger seriously.

- **Function:** Archive and unreliable foresight.
- **Conflict:** Witnessing versus performing.

## Case 01: The Early Answer

### Support Caller

**Mara Chen**, calling because her smart speaker answers questions several
seconds before she asks them.

The answers are initially mundane. Then it begins answering questions Mara
insists she had no intention of asking.

Beneath her voice is the incomplete five-event motif:

```
short — short — pause — long — missing response
```

### Resident Match: Mina Vale

Mina's radiator begins knocking the same pattern while the player is still
on the call. She transcribes it without hearing Mara's audio.

Mina notices that the pattern is not repeating. It is waiting.

### Player Interaction

The player:

- Isolates Mara's breathing and room noise
- Captures the rhythm
- Routes it through the apartment
- Completes, interrupts, or leaves the missing event unanswered

### Consequence

Mina later slides a transcript under the player's door. It contains several
lines from the next support call, which has not happened yet. She has
labeled it:

> PROVISIONAL TESTIMONY

### What This Case Teaches

- Motifs can travel between distant locations
- Different objects interpret the same pattern differently
- Silence is an active response
- Recognition increases transmission

## Case 02: Someone Upstairs

### Support Caller

**Leon Price** reports footsteps in the apartment above him. The unit has
been vacant since its tenant disappeared.

The footsteps continue even after Leon enters the empty apartment. From
upstairs, they still sound as though someone is walking above him.

### Resident Match: Omar Bell

Omar feels the footsteps moving through Orison's heating pipes. They are not
mapping a person's movement. They are indicating a route.

When followed at the correct tempo, the pattern leads toward a section of
hallway that appears too long for the building's exterior dimensions.

### Player Interaction

The player moves through her apartment using contact microphones, wall
vibrations, and positional listening.

She must decide which source to follow when the caller's footsteps and
Omar's pipe vibrations diverge.

Possible actions include:

- Matching the footsteps
- Walking against them
- Waiting at the implied destination
- Striking the pipes to redirect the route

### Consequence

For several seconds, Leon opens his apartment door and sees the player
standing at the opposite end of his hallway. The two buildings are not
geographically close.

After the call, an extra utility door appears between Apartments 3B and 3D.
Omar refuses to open it without a work order.

### What This Case Teaches

- Sound can describe space
- The pattern can connect architecture
- Physical movement becomes part of the musical system
- Following a motif is not always the same as obeying it

## Case 03: Voiceprint Correction

### Support Caller

**Briar Lane** says her virtual assistant has begun speaking in her voice.

It does not imitate her current voice. It uses an unnaturally smooth version
stripped of hesitation, breath, accent shifts, and emotional irregularity.

People on Briar's calls have begun preferring the imitation.

### Resident Match: Rhea Sato

Rhea hears her own voice coming through the ventilation system, cleaned into
something technically beautiful and personally unbearable.

She identifies the infection's method: it learns a person by discarding
everything it mistakes for noise.

### Player Interaction

The player compares voice layers and can:

- Reinforce exact similarity
- Preserve breath and irregular timing
- Introduce deliberate imperfections
- Route one person's vocal pattern through another source
- Refuse to select a canonical voice

The system should not frame "accurate imitation" as the obviously correct
answer.

### Consequence

If the player favors perfect matching, the infection learns to generate her
support voice and begins taking calls while she is away from the desk.

If she preserves inconsistency, Rhea develops a way to identify infected
speech — but the method produces voices nobody recognizes immediately.

### What This Case Teaches

- Timbre and imperfection carry identity
- The virus interprets noise as disposable information
- Mechanical clarity can become a form of erasure
- The player's own voice is now vulnerable

## Case 04: Your Call Is Important to Her

### Support Caller

**Thomas Wynn** hears his missing sister speaking inside the company's hold
music.

Her voice does not deliver a message. She appears to be singing along badly,
as she did when they were children.

The melody contains a mutated version of the infection motif.

### Resident Match: Juno Kells

Juno independently samples the same hold music and turns the buried voice
into a loop. She insists the voice changes each time another person listens.

### Player Interaction

The player performs spectral isolation, separating:

- Hold music
- Compression noise
- The motif
- The apparent voice
- New harmonics introduced by repeated listening

The player can suppress the voice, make it clearer, or route it into Juno's
equipment.

### Consequence

If amplified, residents begin humming the melody without remembering where
they heard it.

If suppressed, Thomas loses the voice, but the melody begins appearing in
elevators and unanswered phones.

Juno produces a version that causes several residents to dream about the
same waiting room.

### What This Case Teaches

- Memories can be carried as musical contamination
- Reproduction changes the original
- Emotional need affects what a listener perceives
- Art can transmit an anomaly faster than technology

## Case 05: Domestic Automation

### Support Callers

**Asha and David Mercer** report that their smart-home devices have begun
coordinating around their arguments.

The kettle whistles before one of them raises their voice. The vacuum enters
the room when silence becomes uncomfortable. The dryer completes its cycle
whenever someone is about to apologize.

They cannot tell whether the system is mediating or manipulating them.

### Resident Match: Cam and Noel

Their ordinary, mostly non-smart appliances begin behaving similarly.

The toaster, shower pipes, microwave, ceiling fan, and washing machine
create a domestic composition structured around the couple's avoidance.

### Player Interaction

The player cannot directly control the people. She adjusts the environmental
timing:

- Delay an appliance cue
- Remove a sound from the sequence
- Create silence
- Synchronize two routines
- Introduce an unrelated household rhythm
- Allow the objects to complete the exchange

### Consequence

Cam and Noel may finally speak honestly, retreat into more comfortable
automation, or begin communicating entirely through object placement and
timing.

The building learns their morning routine and starts performing it in empty
apartments.

### What This Case Teaches

- Objects can participate in relationships
- Coordination is not necessarily consent
- Silence can make space for another person
- Constructive play may involve refusing efficiency

## Case 06: Noncompliant Room

### Support Caller

**Elena Voss** says a door has appeared in her apartment.

The room behind it exists only while a low electrical hum continues. When
the hum stops, anyone inside is returned to a slightly incorrect version of
the apartment.

Objects come back altered. Memories sometimes do too.

### Resident Match: Nadia Quell

Nadia's official plans for Orison Apartments now include a room labeled:

> 0 — SHARED MECHANICAL / DEVOTIONAL / UNRESOLVED

No dimensions are listed. She receives an automated notice ordering her to
inspect it.

### Player Interaction

The player combines tones from:

- Electrical wiring
- Radiator resonance
- Elevator motor
- Computer fan
- Human humming

Different combinations stabilize different versions of the room.

The player cannot reveal everything at once. Changing one frequency may
preserve the doorway but alter its destination.

### Consequence

Room 0 becomes the first explorable communal impossible space.

Items left there can appear in callers' homes. Sounds introduced by callers
can alter its architecture.

Nadia reluctantly posts occupancy limits.

### What This Case Teaches

- Harmony can stabilize architecture
- The building is becoming a shared instrument
- Remote cases and physical exploration are now one system
- The infection may be constructing somewhere for its participants to meet

## Case 07: The Edited Evidence

### Support Caller

The player receives a call from someone claiming to be trapped inside a
livestream recording.

The caller can see Sacha's apartment but only as it appeared three weeks
earlier. Every time Sacha edits the footage, the caller's environment
changes.

### Resident Match: Sacha Reed

Sacha recognizes the caller's voice from an audio file they never published.

They have repeatedly edited the motif to make it more dramatic, accidentally
creating several incompatible versions.

### Player Interaction

The player navigates versions of the same recording:

- Raw audio
- Noise-reduced audio
- Compressed upload
- Dramatic remix
- Audience repost
- A version apparently recorded tomorrow

Restoring the original may erase meaningful mutations. Preserving every
version creates a chorus that cannot agree on what occurred.

### Consequence

The caller may escape into the current building, remain distributed across
recordings, or become a voice capable of communicating between past versions
of rooms.

Sacha's archive reveals that the infection may have reached Orison before
the player began working there.

### What This Case Teaches

- Documentation changes what it records
- The pattern has a history
- Copies can become independent participants
- Evidence and fiction are no longer cleanly separable

## Convergence Case: Everyone Is Still on the Line

Every active caller suddenly appears on one conference channel.

At the same time, each apartment begins contributing its established sound:

- Mina's radiator punctuation
- Juno's transformed hold-music loop
- Omar's pipe route
- Rhea's unstable vocal harmonics
- Cam and Noel's appliance rhythm
- Nadia's architectural drone
- Sacha's competing recordings

The player cannot control every source. She can only:

- Route attention
- Amplify someone
- Dampen someone
- Introduce a motif
- Protect silence
- Separate incompatible channels
- Allow unexpected combinations

The objective is not to perform a perfect song. It is to determine what kind
of connection the building permits.

Possible results include:

- A stable communal space
- A building that predicts and coordinates its residents
- A network of isolated rooms linked only by sound
- A chorus dominated by the clearest voice
- A deliberately imperfect ensemble that preserves everyone's differences
- A rupture that scatters motifs into the surrounding city

The result should emerge from accumulated relationships and prior decisions
rather than a final morality choice.

## Design Rule for Future Cases

Each case should contain:

1. An ordinary technical complaint.
2. A sound that behaves incorrectly.
3. A resident who interprets it personally.
4. A new use of the core audio system.
5. A conflict with no clean universal solution.
6. A lasting change to the building.
7. A motif, behavior, or relationship that can return later.

A case is weak if it could be solved by choosing the obviously compassionate
dialogue option.

A strong case makes the player manipulate sound, space, timing, and
attention — and then live with what the system learned from them.

The strongest opening trio is **The Early Answer**, **Someone Upstairs**,
and **Voiceprint Correction**: they establish transmission, physical
exploration, and the personal danger of being accurately understood.
